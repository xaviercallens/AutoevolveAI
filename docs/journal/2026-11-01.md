### Notes on the ranked items
- GALE-101 is the best item for an immediate start: tiny cost, a clean yes/no, a claim from an AI-assisted pipeline
  (the paper says GPT-6 Astra helped develop the algorithms; it also says "there is no full formalization in Lean 4 or
  an independent review"). Controls: built-in count (paper says 2030 edges and 101 points, so the parse of the
  two-column appendix must reproduce both); positive control: the regular-tetrahedron partition of S^2 gives 0.88807
  and must not certify more; negative control: perturb one coordinate or delete one edge of the 4-core and the
  non-4-colourability certificate must be able to fail. Pitfall: the threshold 0.88928 is a rounded decimal and the
  coordinates are rounded to 7 digits; the exact statement is about the rounded coordinates themselves (the paper says
  Theorem 2 holds for them) and must be decided with exact squared distances and a rational threshold.
- The statement concerns finite sets and a distance graph; it is not about Borsuk's conjecture (the paper says so).
- MS-CENSUS cannot verify the proof; say so in any write-up. The Figure 1 graph is a built-in control.
