## 3. Human proof, step by step

- **Step 0 / Lemma 1** (bounds on erfcx and w): correct.
- **Step 1** (moments): correct. 1/(2a) = 2u and u^N e^{-cu} ≤ N!/c^N hold. On (1,∞) the bound
  (2√π)^{-1} a^{-3/2} holds.
- **Step 2** (differentiation under the integral, induction, sign): correct.
- **Step 3**: correct.
- **Step 4** (non-vacuity): correct, and important.
- **Step 5** (Leibniz rule): correct.
- **Step 6**: the ⊤ ≤ ⊤ reading is correct. The 2D lattice sum of 1/|x| diverges linearly.
- **Step 7** (Tier C): I re-derived each part.
  - (b) The Laplace pair gives 1/(1+r₀k) = k∫w e^{-ak²} da. Correct.
  - (c) The Fubini argument is correct: Tonelli in a first gives ∫|J₀(kr)|/(1+r₀k) dk, which is O(k^{-3/2}) at
    infinity.
  - (d) Weber's integral: correct.
  - (e) DLMF 11.5.2 with ν = 0 gives the prefactor 2/π. Correct.
  - Gap: in (a), V and V̂ are not in L¹, so "inverting the radial Fourier transform" is an improper or
    distributional inversion. That is acceptable at Tier C but should be stated. Parts (a), (b) and (d) also rely
    on uncited standard integrals. The paper says so for (a) only.
  - **Suggested strengthening (nonblocking; auditor-derived, Tier C, mpmath-checked).** Route (e) alone proves
    Theorem 2, with no Hankel table integral and only positive integrands (Tonelli). Insert the subordination
    formula into V(r) = r₀⁻¹∫e^{-u√t/r₀}(1+u²)^{-1/2}du. The inner integral is elementary:
    ∫₀^∞ u(1+u²)^{-1/2}e^{-λu²}du = ½√(π/λ)·erfcx(√λ), by the substitution v = √(1+u²). This gives V = ∫e^{-ta}ρ(a)da
    with ρ(a) = erfcx(1/(2r₀√a))/(2r₀a). The change of variable a = 1/(4b) turns ρ(a)da into exactly w(b)/(2b) db.
    mpmath (dps 25, r₀ = 0.7, r = 1.3) gives:
    - Struve form: 0.66931236059094967
    - Laplace form: 0.66931236059094652
    - Gaussian mixture: 0.66931236059094957
    - the inner-integral identity agrees to all printed digits.

    This would remove the "table integral checked only numerically" limitation, leaving only the subordination
    formula to cite.

## 4. Tiers and overclaim

The tiering is honest overall. The paper states:
- conditionality on upstream (AI-generated, not peer reviewed), in the abstract, §2, Step 6, §4 and Limitations;
- that the raw corollary is expected to read ⊤ ≤ ⊤ (Tier C), and that finiteness of the regularised energy is not
  proved;
- that Tier X never supports a claim. Every X row in the ledger has `claim_licence: false`, and no A, L or C row
  depends on an X row;
- novelty modestly ("None of this is a breakthrough"; the novelty search is called "weak evidence").

Corrections:
- **(Required) §3, lines 107–108:** "Step~7, the link to the Struve function, is not \tier{C}." As typeset this
  reads "is not [C]", which inverts the claim. Step 7 *is* Tier C and is *not* machine-checked. Suggested text:
  "Step 7, the link to the Struve function, is not formalized; it is \tier{C}."
- Same paragraph: "Steps 1–6 are mirrored in Lean" conflicts with §4's "Formalized: Steps 0–6". Step 0 (the erfcx
  bounds) is in Lean. Use "0–6" in both places.
- Title and abstract, first sentence: "We show that … it is a positive mixture of Gaussians". This headline claim
  is Tier C (a human proof, not machine-checked). The abstract then tiers it correctly, but adding "(Tier C, human
  proof)" to the first sentence would prevent misreading. The title is acceptable if the abstract is tagged.
- Step 0 / KT-X-0001 in the paper: "a perturbed integrand is off by at least 5.6×10⁻³" is false at x = 0. There the
  perturbation (2xy → xy) has no effect: `perturbed_rel_err` = 0.0. The ledger says "at x > 0" correctly; the
  paper should say the same.
- §6 "Screening lowers both the magnitude of the energy …": the lowered magnitude mostly comes from the
  lattice-independent +πρr₀ background convention. The figure captions say so; this sentence should too.
