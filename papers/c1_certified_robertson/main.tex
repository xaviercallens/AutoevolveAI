\begin{abstract}
A validated enclosure of Robertson's stiff problem on $[0,4000]$ was published in 2026 with its key step theorem argued in prose
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.24216837). We (i) state and prove that step theorem in Lean 4 for Robertson's reduced system, with existence,
uniqueness, a one-sided (logarithmic-norm) Gr\"onwall bound valid for any sign of the constant, tube invariance, chaining through
basis changes with the exact $2\times2$ spectral norm, gluing of steps, and (new in v2) a theorem for the chain of $N$ steps, for any $N$; and (ii) write an independent checker in Rust with Arb
that recomputes, from exact per-step records, the numerical hypotheses of those theorems for \emph{every} step of the run. All
$2{,}843{,}424$ steps are certified (one streaming pass, 6 h 21 min, 5.6~MB of checker memory), and the checker's final boxes for $(y_2,y_3)$ lie inside the
stored checkpoint enclosures, which lie inside the published ones. The Lean chain theorem (\texttt{robertson\_multistep}) says that if every step satisfies the one-step hypotheses and the
initial error bound of each step is chained from the previous one, an exact solution exists on the whole interval and stays inside every step's tube; it has \emph{not} been instantiated with
the $2.8$ million real steps: the link ``the checker accepted every record'' $\Rightarrow$ ``the Lean hypotheses hold for these records'' is a reviewed correspondence, not a proof. After $t=400$ the checker's bound is much tighter than the producer's (by a factor $7$ at $t=1000$, $18$ at $t=4000$); we
give evidence, on two record windows and a 281-point sample of $t\le1000$ and with the producer's source, that this comes from interval division in the producer at
occasional steps, not from optimism in the checker; the range $1000<t\le4000$ was not examined finely. This is a Tier~C
result: the checker is unverified Rust code, the link between its arithmetic and the Lean hypotheses is a reviewed correspondence
and not a proof, and no human has reviewed the Lean statements. The work is AI-generated under human direction and is not peer reviewed.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Claimed:} (1) the Lean theorems below compile under that gate; (2) an independent checker certifies every step of the run

\section{Background}
The published enclosure \tL{} integrates Robertson's problem
$y_1'=-0.04y_1+10^4y_2y_3$, $y_2'=0.04y_1-10^4y_2y_3-3\cdot10^7y_2^2$, $y_3'=3\cdot10^7y_2^2$ in the reduced variables $(y_2,y_3)$ with
$y_1=1-y_2-y_3$. Per step of length $h$ it uses a reference polynomial $q$ (Taylor, order $p=24$), the defect $D=f(q)-q'$ of that
polynomial, an invertible matrix $P$ chosen per step (an eigenvector basis), and a tube of radius $\rho$ around $q$. Its Theorem~1
gives, for the scaled error $w=P^{-1}(z-q)$ of an exact solution $z$, the bound
$\nrm{w(t)}\le \varepsilon_0e^{\nu t}+\bar d\int_0^te^{\nu s}ds<\rho$ under the tube condition
$(\varepsilon_0+\bar dh)e^{\max(\nu,0)h}<\rho$, where $\nu$ bounds the logarithmic norm of $P^{-1}J(y)P$ over a set containing the tube and
$\bar d\ge\nrm{P^{-1}D}$. Steps are chained through $\varepsilon_0'=\sigma_{\max}(P'^{-1}P)\,\varepsilon_{\rm end}+\nrm{P'^{-1}\mathrm{jump}}$.
That paper proved Theorem~1 in prose only; its producer used 160-bit Arb balls in Sage 9.5, and its production run was interrupted and resumed from checkpoints.
Our regenerated producer, run once without interruption, reproduces the published step counts and final errors at the output times of its log up to $t=4000$.

\section{The Lean side}
All files are in the evidence bundle (\texttt{lean/}); Lean 4.34.0-rc2 with a partial local Mathlib; gate:
compile plus \texttt{\#print axioms} per theorem with a whitelist. Every theorem named below passed the gate with exactly
\{\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}\}.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Midpoint identity.} For the quadratic field $f$ of Robertson, $f(z)-f(q)=J\!\big(\tfrac{q+z}2\big)(z-q)$ exactly, in the 3-variable and
reduced forms (\texttt{robertson\_midpoint\_identity\_3d}, \texttt{\_reduced}), and the written-out matrix is the Fr\'echet derivative of the field on
$\mathbb{R}^2$ (\texttt{robertson\_hasFDerivAt}); a cubic counterexample shows the identity is a property of quadratics.
\item \textbf{One-sided Gr\"onwall.} In a real inner-product space, if $w'=Aw+b$, $\ip{w}{Aw}\le\nu\nrm{w}^2$ ($\nu$ of any sign), $\nrm{b}\le d$
and $\nrm{w(a)}\le\varepsilon_0$, then $\nrm{w(x)}\le\texttt{gronwallBound}(\varepsilon_0,\nu,d)(x-a)$
(\texttt{onesided\_norm\_le\_gronwallBound}). Mathlib's ready-made lemma bounds $\nrm{A}$ instead, which loses the sign of $\nu$; along the trajectory $\nrm{J}$ is of order $10^3$ to $10^4$ (we measured $\nrm{P^{-1}JP}\le6.5\cdot10^3$ at 6{,}684 records) and the factor $e^{\nrm{A}h}$ over $2.8$ million steps is ruinous, whereas $\nu\le244.7$ only briefly and is negative or tiny for most steps.
\item \textbf{Scaled error and tube.} \texttt{scaled\_error\_hasDerivAt}: $w=P^{-1}(z-q)$ satisfies $w'=(P^{-1}J(m)P)w+P^{-1}D$ exactly;
\texttt{truncated\_error\_stays\_in\_tube}: with the field clamped to the tube by a retraction (2-Lipschitz), invariance follows from the
one-sided theorem with $\max(\nu,0)$ and the tube condition, with no first-exit argument.
\item \textbf{Existence and uniqueness.} \texttt{robertson\_onestep\_complete}: under the tube condition, a uniform one-sided bound on a set
$U$ containing every $q(t)+Pu$ with $\nrm{u}\le\rho$, the defect bound and the initial bound, there \emph{exists} a solution of $z'=f(z)$ on the
step with $z(a)=z_0$, strictly inside the tube, satisfying the sharp Gr\"onwall bound; every continuous, right-differentiable solution with the same initial
value coincides with it (Picard--Lindel\"of on the clamped field; uniqueness from Mathlib's theorem and a Lipschitz bound of $f$ on balls). A bound on $\nrm{q}$ over the step is also a hypothesis; the checker
does not discharge it separately (it is trivially satisfiable for a polynomial on a compact interval).
\item \textbf{Chaining.} \texttt{chain\_bound} and \texttt{chain\_sigma}: $\nrm{P'^{-1}(z-m')}\le s\,\nrm{P^{-1}(z-q)}+\nrm{P'^{-1}(q-m')}$ with $s^2=(F^2+\sqrt{F^4-4\det^2})/2$,
the exact $2\times2$ spectral bound (\texttt{sigma\_opNorm\_le}; $F$ the Frobenius norm), given $P'^{-1}P=C$ for an explicit matrix $C$ (a hypothesis discharged by the
checker); a Frobenius version is also proved. Consecutive solutions glue into one (\texttt{glue\_solutions}, \texttt{robertson\_glue}).
\item \textbf{The chain of $N$ steps (new in v2).} A structure \texttt{RobChain} collects, for $i<N$, the one-step hypotheses (reference curve with defect, tube set, one-sided bound $\nu_i$, defect bound $d_i$, $\rho_i$,
tube condition with $\max(\nu_i,0)$) and the chained initial bound $s_i\,g(\varepsilon_{0,i},\nu_i,d_i,h_i)+\nrm{P_{i+1}^{-1}(q_i(t_{i+1})-q_{i+1}(t_{i+1}))}\le\varepsilon_{0,i+1}$, where $g$ is the Gr\"onwall bound and
$s_i\ge\nrm{P_{i+1}^{-1}P_i}$ is a separate hypothesis. \texttt{robertson\_multistep}, proved by induction on $N$ (one-step existence on the last step started from the glued solution, \texttt{chain\_initial\_bound}, \texttt{glue\_solutions}), concludes that there is a
solution $z$ with $z(t_0)=z_0$, continuous on $[t_0,t_N]$, with right derivative $f(z)$ on $[t_0,t_N)$, such that on every step $\nrm{P_i^{-1}(z-q_i)}\le g(\varepsilon_{0,i},\nu_i,d_i,t-t_i)$ and $<\rho_i$.
No continuity of $q$ across step boundaries is assumed: the jump enters only through the chain inequality. Companions: coordinate bounds for the last step (Cauchy--Schwarz on the rows of $P$) and a version with the exact $2\times2$ spectral norm instead of $s_i$.
Gate: 44 theorems, each with its own axiom report, axioms $\subseteq\{$propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound$\}$, no \texttt{sorry}; a non-vacuity witness ($N=2$ at the Robertson equilibrium, degenerate: it shows the hypotheses are consistent, not sharp);
three negative controls (chain without the jump term, $\nu$ instead of $\max(\nu,0)$ in the tube condition, the operator-norm bound reversed) are rejected, each by an application type mismatch at the intended step. The gate was re-run by a session other than the one that wrote the file.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} Each file has a non-vacuity witness or counterexample (for example $w=\varepsilon_0e^{-t}$ with $\nu=-1$ giving the tight bound) and at least one gate
negative control, some files several: copies with one changed constant or hypothesis (a wrong Jacobian coefficient, $\nu$ instead of $\max(\nu,0)$, $F^2-\sqrt{\cdot}$ instead of $F^2+\sqrt{\cdot}$, a wrong
defect sign, a dropped term) are rejected by the gate. The counts per file are in the bundle's results notes. The gate must be given the \texttt{\#print axioms} lines inside each file. The Lean files are standalone: they do not import each other, and the field \texttt{robF} is defined separately in three of them (agreement is textual, checked by reading, not by the kernel). \textbf{The multi-step theorem holds for any $N$ but has not been instantiated with the actual data:}
the $2{,}843{,}424$ tuples $(t_i,q_i,P_i,d_i,\nu_i,\rho_i,\varepsilon_{0,i})$ are not turned into Lean terms and the hypotheses $hq$, $hU$, $h\nu$ are not proved in Lean for them. What connects the checker to the theorem is a reviewed correspondence (checks C0--C7 to the structure's fields),
matched by reading, not by the kernel. Three fields are not reported as checked by the checker's README: the bound on $\nrm{q}$, the nonnegativity of $\varepsilon_{0},d,\rho$ (implied by the checker's strict positivity but not separately reported) and the initial bound of the first step.
Uniqueness is not carried to the glued solution. The Lean statements were written and read by the same AI agent family; no human has reviewed them.

\section{The checker}
A Rust crate with Arb through \texttt{flint3-sys} 3.6.1, 256-bit balls. For each step it takes only exact rational data (start time, $h$, the reference
polynomial's coefficients, the basis $P$ as exact rationals, the claimed numbers) and recomputes, ignoring the producer's claims (mode \emph{derive}, exact chain):
\textbf{C0} continuity, shape, index; \textbf{C1} $P$ is invertible (its inverse is computed exactly in rationals, so $P'^{-1}P=C$ is an exact rational matrix and the hypothesis of \texttt{chain\_sigma} holds
literally); \textbf{C2} the defect polynomial $D$ and a bound $\bar d$ on $\nrm{P^{-1}D}$ over $[0,h]$ (sum of coefficient moduli times $h^k$); \textbf{C3} the one-sided constant $\nu$ as the largest eigenvalue of the
symmetric part of $P^{-1}JP$ over a box containing the tube; \textbf{C4} the tube condition with $\rho=8(\varepsilon_0+\bar dh)$; \textbf{C5} the Gr\"onwall end bound evaluated at the \emph{scalar}
upper bound of $\nu$; \textbf{C6} the chaining bound with the exact $2\times2$ spectral norm; \textbf{C7} the output box. A step is certified only if all pass.
\textbf{What is not proved:} that these Rust formulas are the Lean hypotheses (a human-reviewed correspondence; the Neumann-type inverse enclosure used in a secondary mode, the box enclosure by Horner
evaluation and the closed-form eigenvalue have no Lean proof); the hard-coded field and Jacobian.
\textbf{What gates a step in the headline mode} (derive, exact chain): continuity and shape (C0), invertibility (C1), the tube condition (C4) and finiteness. C4 adds little on its own: since $\rho=8(\varepsilon_0+\bar dh)$,
it reduces to $\nu_+h<\ln 8$, so the largest ratio $0.1314$ (step index 26) only says $\nu_+h\approx0.05$ there; it is not a robustness margin. C2, C3, C5 and C7 are derivations that fail only on non-finite values; their correctness
rests on the code.
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Record corruptions}, by mode: in the headline mode, doubling $h$ is rejected (C0, at the corrupted step; on a stream of 11{,}382 records the first failure is the corrupted index) and a singular $P$ is rejected (C1);
corrupting the producer's claimed $d$, $\nu$ or carried error has no effect there, because no claim is consumed. In the secondary \emph{claims} mode (the producer's $d,\nu,\varepsilon_0$ verified and consumed) dropping $d$ and lowering $\nu$ are rejected at C2 and C3 at every step,
which shows that the checker's own derived $d$ and $\nu$ are non-trivially larger than the corrupted claims; a halved carried error is rejected at C6 in the claims mode only, and a wrong claimed inverse is rejected at C1 in the modes that use a claimed inverse (derive with the Frobenius or spectral chain, and claims) but not in the exact mode, where the inverse is computed exactly and a wrong claimed one only raises a non-gating note.
\emph{Mutation of the checker's source} (one change each, on a throwaway copy, first 265 steps, headline mode; bundle \texttt{mutation/}): a wrong Jacobian coefficient is rejected at 209 of 265 steps and a wrong field coefficient at 236 of 265;
but taking the \emph{smaller} eigenvalue for $\nu$ is \textbf{accepted} at all 265 steps (final $\varepsilon_{\rm end}$ falls by seven orders of magnitude) and truncating the defect to the first $p$ coefficients is accepted (median $d$ changes by $0.06\%$).
The former is caught only by comparing the checker's $\nu$ with the producer's recorded $\nu$ (relative differences $10^3$ to $10^5$ against $4\cdot10^{-5}$ unmutated); the latter is not reliably caught by anything here. So in the headline mode certification of C2/C3 rests on the checker's code being right and on this cross-check against the producer.
A stricter first window needed one documented tolerance (a $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance on the producer's $\nu$ claim in the claims mode); the exact mode used for the headline needs none.

\section{The discrepancy after $t=400$, and its cause}
An enclosure tighter than the producer's is acceptable only if understood. In a rerun to $t=1000$ printing every 2{,}000th step (a diagnostic copy of the checker), our $\nu$ equals the producer's claimed $\nu$
at the printed five significant digits at 213 of 281 samples; the other 68 differ by $2$ to $4\cdot10^{-8}$ (one-sided, ours smaller), first at step 426{,}000, a consequence of the producer's larger tube after its largest leap. The difference is a set of
discrete leaps in the producer's claimed error, not a different growth rate: for 4{,}310 consecutive transitions in two extracted windows the producer's $\varepsilon_0$ never exceeds the exact chain
value (exact $\sigma_{\max}$ times its own previous bound plus the exact jump norm) by more than $0.1\%$, but inside steps 425{,}749 to 425{,}752 its recorded $\varepsilon_{\rm end}$ exceeds the Gr\"onwall value of its own recorded $\varepsilon_0,\nu,\bar d$ by factors $1.04$, $1.12$,
$1.34$, $2.04$ (about the growth of the step too, since $\nu\approx0$).
The cause: the producer records the \emph{upper} end of its $\nu$ ball and evaluates $g=(e^{\nu h}-1)/\nu$ with $\nu$ a ball. When the ball is wide (lower end near $-2.6\cdot10^{-3}$) but its upper end is slightly negative,
ball division treats numerator and denominator as independent and the upper end becomes $|e^{\nu_{\rm lo}h}-1|/|\nu_{\rm up}|$, up to $\approx 540$ at step 425{,}752 and a few hundred times $h$ at the other leap steps, against the true value $\approx h=1.6\cdot10^{-3}$: sound but extremely pessimistic.
This is exactly what the producer's source does (\texttt{ph2\_enclose.py}, lines 236--248: $\nu$ is a ball, \texttt{nu.upper()} is recorded, and $g$ is computed by ball division whenever \texttt{nu.upper()<0}).
A consistency check of this reading (it inverts the producer's own $\varepsilon_{\rm end}$ under the model, so it is consistent with it, not an independent proof): the lower end implied by the producer's own $\varepsilon_{\rm end}$ at the 15 leap steps of the window around step 425{,}000 is $-2.636\cdot10^{-3}$ to $-2.645\cdot10^{-3}$ although their
$\nu_{\rm up}$ ranges from $-8\cdot10^{-9}$ to $-8\cdot10^{-6}$, and $-3.517\cdot10^{-3}$ to $-3.520\cdot10^{-3}$ at the 3 leap steps near step 289{,}000. The Lean theorem takes a scalar $\nu$ and the checker evaluates the bound at the scalar upper end, so it has no such artefact.
\textbf{Limits:} established on two record windows and a 281-point sample of $t\le1000$; the radius ratio falls further from $0.140$ at $t=1000$ to $0.057$ at $t=4000$, a range not examined finely, only through the final comparison. This analysis explains the producer's looseness;
it is not what makes the checker sound, which rests on C3 and C5 and on the code being right.

\section{Limits and what remains}
Tier~C throughout. The checker is unverified code (a verified checker is a much larger project); Arb, FLINT, GMP and MPFR and the \texttt{unsafe} bindings are assumed correct; the checker and the producer use the same mathematics
(independent code, not an independent method); the stored enclosure is the producer's own output, so agreement of boxes is not independent evidence; the Robertson field and Jacobian in the checker are hard-coded;
the independent AI audit compared them coefficient by coefficient with the Lean field and found them identical, but the human comparison is still owed; no human has reviewed the Lean statements (the only route to Tier~A). One problem, one precision, one producer.
What remains, in order of value: instantiate \texttt{RobChain} mechanically for a finite window of the real stream (the first $71$ steps, $t\le0.1$) to test the correspondence; a human review of the Lean statements and of the check-to-field mapping; a finer examination of $1000<t\le4000$ (a replay with sampling diagnostics over the whole run was started and is not part of this version).
