\begin{abstract}
We report a small, end-to-end example of certified numerics for cosmology. At $\Omega_m=0.29743$ (with $h r_d=101.543$\,Mpc), a radiation-off flat $\Lambda$CDM fit to the DESI DR2
baryon-acoustic-oscillation data computed with rusty-SUNDIALS (DESI quotes $\Omega_m=0.2975\pm0.0086$~\cite{desi}), Lean 4 theorems, checked by the kernel with
only the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound} and without \texttt{native\_decide},
enclose the dimensionless comoving distance $\chi(z)=\int_0^z dz'/E(z')$ at all seven DESI DR2 effective redshifts with
relative width between $\MinWidth$ and $\MaxWidth$ (the Einstein--de Sitter control, a different model, has widths up to $6.6\times10^{-4}$). The method is elementary: $1/E$ is decreasing, so Riemann sums on a grid
bound the integral, and an untrusted program supplies integer bounds of $1/E$ at $\NNodes$ grid nodes which the kernel
re-checks by exact integer arithmetic. The certified enclosures were then used as a reference to audit a Rust CVODE
reimplementation (rusty-SUNDIALS) and astropy: their values lie inside (a test at the $10^{-4}$ resolution of the enclosures only;
a run at $r_{\rm tol}=10^{-3}$ also lies inside); a deliberately loose CVODE run falls outside at
$\LooseOut$ of $\NZ$ redshifts; and the enclosures for $\Omega_m\times1.01$ are disjoint from those of the fit.
All hypotheses and controls were preregistered. The paper also records, as research directions, how a survey of the
\texttt{openai/math} Lean library bears on this programme: it contributes certificate methodology, not results on the
underlying physics. Nothing here is a new physical result; the contribution is a verified reference computation and a
reusable pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and scope}
Floating-point solvers can fail silently or degrade unnoticed. In the same programme we found that a Rust CVODE
reimplementation had an Adams method stuck at order one (an accuracy loss no test caught) and a Nordsieck-array shift bug that
aborted runs at tight tolerances; the commit introducing the faulty rescaling cited, as justification, an unproved Lean axiom whose
statement is too weak to certify it. A kernel-checked enclosure removes the solver from the trusted base: if the theorem compiles,
the Lean-defined $\chi(z)$ is where the theorem says it is, whatever the solver did; whether that definition is the intended model is
a separate, human-checked question.

\paragraph{What is claimed.} For $E(\Omega_m,z)=\sqrt{\Omega_m(1+z)^3+1-\Omega_m}$ with $\Omega_m=29743/100000$ and
$z\in\{0.295, 0.510, 0.706, 0.934, 1.321, 1.484, 2.330\}$, the Lean theorems state rational lower and upper bounds for
$\chi(z)$ and for $1/E(z)$ (Table~\ref{tab:enc}). \paragraph{What is not claimed.} The fit point is a rounded value of a
floating-point minimisation and is not certified as the $\chi^2$ minimum; $D_V/r_d$, the conversion to $D_M/r_d$,
$D_H/r_d$ and the comparison with DESI are computed outside Lean from the certified bounds (the pulls in floating point, with diagonal errors only); the model (flat $\Lambda$CDM,
no radiation) is a modelling choice, not a theorem; no statement about dark energy is made.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Model.} $\chi(z)=\int_0^z 1/E(\Omega_m,x)\,dx$ in units of $c/H_0$; $D_M/r_d=\frac{c}{100\,h r_d}\chi(z)$ and
$D_H/r_d=\frac{c}{100\,hr_d}\frac{1}{E(z)}$ with $c=299792.458$\,km/s and $h r_d=101.543$\,Mpc.

\paragraph{Riemann bounds.} For $0\le\Omega_m\le1$, $1/E$ is antitone on $[0,\infty)$ (Lean: \texttt{invE\_antitoneOn}). On the grid
$x_i=i/S$ ($S=2000$), every cell satisfies $f(x_{i+1})/S\le\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}f\le f(x_i)/S$ (\texttt{cell\_bounds}). Every DESI DR2
redshift is a multiple of $1/2000$, so no endpoint is rounded.

\paragraph{Integer certificate.} With $\Omega_m=p/q$, $A(x_i)=\Omega_m(1+x_i)^3+1-\Omega_m=a_i/b$ exactly, with
$a_i=p(S+i)^3+(q-p)S^3$ and $b=qS^3$. For integers $\ell_i,h_i$ and $D=10^{12}$, the conditions
\[ \ell_i^2\,a_i\ \le\ D^2 b\ \le\ h_i^2\,a_i \]
imply $\ell_i/D\le 1/E(x_i)\le h_i/D$ (\texttt{node\_bounds}). An untrusted Python program computes $\ell_i=\lfloor D/\sqrt{A(x_i)}\rfloor$
by integer square roots and $h_i=\ell_i+1$. A Boolean function \texttt{checkFrom} tests all $\NNodes$ conditions and is evaluated
by the kernel (\texttt{decide +kernel}); the kernel's arbitrary-precision natural-number arithmetic makes this exact.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{BAOCert.seg\_bounds}]
Let $p\le q$, $q,S,D>0$, and let $L=((\ell_0,h_0),\dots,(\ell_k,h_k))$ satisfy \texttt{checkFrom}$\,p\,q\,S\,D\,i_0\,L$. Then
\[ \frac{1}{DS}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\ell_j\ \le\ \int_{i_0/S}^{(i_0+k)/S}\frac{dx}{E(p/q,x)}\ \le\ \frac{1}{DS}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}h_j .\]
\end{theorem}
The proof is by induction over the table, using the cell bounds and additivity of the interval integral (Mathlib). For each
DESI redshift $n/S$ a generated theorem (e.g.\ \texttt{BAOCert.Fit.chi\_z0934}) instantiates it with the prefix of length $n+1$ and
the two sums evaluated by the kernel.

\paragraph{Toolchain.} Lean 4.34.1~\cite{lean} with Mathlib~\cite{mathlib} at commit \texttt{d13f23b7}, pinned Mathlib imports,
\texttt{autoImplicit} off. The core compiles in $\CoreSeconds$\,s and the fit-point certificate (table check plus fourteen theorems)
in $\FitSeconds$\,s wall-clock (the two control certificates take about the same), one \texttt{lean} process on an 8-core virtual machine.
Data: \path{desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_mean.txt} and \path{_cov.txt} of the DESI DR2 public BAO release~\cite{desi}.

\section{Preregistration and controls}
Before any table was generated, we committed five hypotheses: H1 (enclosures with relative width $\le5\times10^{-4}$, standard axioms
only), H2 (rusty-SUNDIALS and astropy values inside), H3 (enclosures at $\Omega_m\times1.01$ disjoint from the fit's for $z\ge0.5$),
H4 (a loose CVODE run, $r_{\rm tol}=10^{-2}$, outside at least once) and H5 (a certified $\chi^2$ interval, secondary). Controls: C1, the Einstein--de Sitter
closed form $\chi=2(1-(1+z)^{-1/2})$ lies inside the $\Omega_m=1$ enclosures (checked exactly by squaring); C2, a table with one upper
bound lowered by $10^6$ units is rejected by the kernel; C3, a \texttt{sorry} proof and a proof from a declared axiom are flagged by the
axiom audit. All three controls passed. H1--H4 passed; H5 was not attempted. One deviation: the loose run of H4 also set
$a_{\rm tol}=10^{-2}$, which the preregistration did not name. An independent verifier recompiled the core and all three certificate files, re-ran the axiom
audit, planted its own negative controls (a lowered upper bound is rejected by the kernel; a false stated bound fails to elaborate)
and recomputed every enclosure check with 50-digit arithmetic; it confirmed the results with the scope corrections made here.

Table~\ref{tab:audit} shows the intended use: the certificate grades a solver, and it catches the loose run. Its resolution is the
enclosure width: the run at $r_{\rm tol}=10^{-3}$ stays inside, so at this width the certificate cannot tell it from the default runs
(whose errors the crate's own tests put at or below about $10^{-6}$). H2 is therefore a test at the $10^{-4}$ level only, and only
of $\chi$: the solvers' $1/E$ values were not compared; second-order bounds (Section~\ref{sec:dir}) would close that gap.
The enclosures at $\Omega_m\times1.01$ are disjoint from the fit's at every redshift, so the certificate resolves a 1\% change in $\Omega_m$.

\section{Research directions}\label{sec:dir}
A read-only survey of the author's Lean 4 projects against the \texttt{openai/math} library (commit \texttt{adc7f124}) found no
overlap with the K3, moonshine and T-duality core of the programme; the overlap is certificate infrastructure (rational ball
arithmetic with a soundness theorem; residual-based matrix-inverse bounds, located by the survey but not line-checked; and integer
tables closed by \texttt{decide +kernel}, which occurs 1,354,379 times in its \texttt{.lean} files while \texttt{native\_decide}
occurs zero times). The present pilot uses that discipline, not that code. The ranked directions are:
(R1) a certified DESI DR2 $\chi^2$ interval and a certified exclusion of Einstein--de Sitter, then second-order enclosures (width
$\sim10^{-8}$) and evolving dark energy; (R2) verified integrator theory for rusty-SUNDIALS (BDF order conditions and zero-stability,
Nordsieck rescaling), replacing vacuous axioms in that project; (R3) validated Taylor enclosures for a nonlinear ODE with an exact
invariant (a 0-D tokamak current quench); (R4) sanity theorems supporting a human audit of Comparator-accepted Lean statements;
(R5) removing inconsistent axioms from the existing corpus; (R6) a certified follow-up of a published null result on the matrix
Lieb--Thirring inequality~\cite{ltnull}. Details are in \texttt{docs/RESEARCH\_DIRECTIONS\_2026-10-08.md}.

\section{Limitations}
The enclosures are first order: relative width $2.5$--$2.8\times10^{-4}$ at the fit point and at $\Omega_m\times1.01$, up to $6.6\times10^{-4}$ for the Einstein--de Sitter control. Only $\Lambda$CDM without radiation is covered, at one parameter point.
The trusted base is Lean's kernel and Mathlib; the emitter, the solver runs and the DESI comparison are not trusted and need not be.
The rusty-SUNDIALS checkout was a pull-request branch, not its main branch.
