VERDICT: NOT SCOOPED. None of the papers read contains a bistability census of the class (3 species, molecularity <= 2, <= 3 internal reactions, inflow and linear removal for every species). Partial overlaps exist and narrow the claim (below). Tool: alphaXiv `get_paper_content` (full extracted text; I grepped and read the relevant passages from the saved text, not every page) and `discover_papers` (3 searches).

## What was read
1. Tang-Xu, arXiv:2008.03846 (full text, read abstract, introduction, Theorems 2.4/2.6 statements by grep + intro; proofs not read). Classifies multistability for networks with exactly two reactions, or one irreversible + one reversible, or two reversible pairs, by smallest number of species/reactants. No inflow/outflow structure, no census, no 3-species bimolecular enumeration. Statement (Remark 4.16): `X1 -> X2+X3, 2X1+X2+X3 -> 3X1` is not multistable; it has 4 reactants, so lies outside our class. NO OVERLAP with the census.
2. Joshi-Shiu, "Which small reaction networks are multistationary?", arXiv:1603.06441 (full text; read abstract, intro, Cor. 3.12, Cor. 4.12, Rem. 5.4, Cor. 5.5). Networks with 1 species or <= 2 reactions (flows counted as reactions). Cor. 5.5: an at-most-bimolecular network with exactly two reactions is not multistable. Our networks have 6 flow reactions plus 2 or 3 internal ones, so that corollary does not apply to them and does not conflict. NO OVERLAP with the census; it is a tool for networks without flows.
3. Joshi-Shiu, "Atoms of multistationarity in chemical reaction networks", arXiv:1108.5238 (full text; read abstract, intro, Thm 3.1, Rem 3.3, Section 6 setup; Section 7 tables not read). PARTIAL OVERLAP:
   - Thm 3.1 (exact statement): if N is a subnetwork of G with the same stoichiometric subspace and N admits multiple positive exponentially stable steady states, then G does too. With all flows present every network in our class has stoichiometric subspace R^3, so this is an inheritance theorem for our supersets.
   - Sec. 6/7: enumeration of the 386 reversible bimolecular two-non-flow-reaction fully open CFSTRs (<= 2 non-flow reactions or reversible pairs), 35 multistationary, 11 atoms. This is MULTISTATIONARITY (not stability) for 2 non-flow reactions; our 2-internal-edge bistable networks are a stability-level statement in the same family. Not compared edge by edge (Section 7 list not read).
   - Rem. 3.3: Siegal-Gaskins et al. (BMC Syst Biol 2009; cited there, not read) classified 40,680 small gene-regulatory networks, 14,721 bistable via Advanced Deficiency Theory, 22,050 more by network ancestry. Different class (gene-regulatory kinetics), but it is a precedent for a census of bistability with the ancestry method.
4. Joshi, arXiv:1207.6018 and Joshi-Shiu survey arXiv:1412.5257: appeared in searches, abstracts only. Joshi 2013: fully open networks with ONE non-flow reaction.

## Consequences for our claim (narrowed)
- Claim only: to our knowledge no published census of exact-witness bistability for this class (3 species, <= 3 internal reactions, full inflow/outflow). Multistationarity of fully open bimolecular networks with 1 or 2 non-flow reactions is known (Joshi 2013; Joshi-Shiu 2013); we do not claim it.
- The inheritance theorem (Atoms Thm 3.1) predicts supersets of bistable subnetworks are bistable. Using the census itself: 609 three-internal-edge orbits contain a BISTABLE_CERT 2-edge or smaller subnetwork; the census certifies 552, has 38 as MULTISTATIONARY_CERT and 19 as UNDECIDED, and none as excluded (consistency, 0 contradictions). By the theorem these 57 are bistable too; we do not include them in the headline counts. This also shows the 37% UNDECIDED contains genuine bistables.
- The census method (witness search + Craciun-Feinberg determinant criterion + subnetwork lookup) is standard; novelty is the enumeration with exact witnesses, not a new technique.
