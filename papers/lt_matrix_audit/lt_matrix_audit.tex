\begin{abstract}
We report two things, neither of which is a mathematical breakthrough. First, an adversarial numerical search for
a violation of the matrix-valued sharp one-dimensional Lieb--Thirring inequality $\operatorname{Tr}(-\partial_x^2\otimes 1_m-W)_-^{\gamma}\le \Lone(\gamma)\int\operatorname{tr}W^{\gamma+1/2}\,dx$
for $1/2<\gamma<3/2$, with $\Lone$ the one-bound-state constant. The instrument is one-sided by construction (the computed numerator is a lower bound of the true one; the ratio is a lower bound up to
quadrature and truncation error, which we measured at the $10^{-7}$--$10^{-6}$ level), and was validated by positive and negative controls before any result was read.
Across $\MTwoCells$ cells at $m=2$ ($\MTwoRestarts$ restarts), $\MThreeCells$ cells at $m=3$, a second-variation test at the embedded scalar extremiser,
and an independent search of the dual kinetic form, no violation was found: $\MTwoFlagged$ restarts exceeded $\Lone$ by the flag threshold, and the closest approach is
$\MTwoClosest$ below $\Lone$, which is below the instrument's resolution of about $10^{-6}$. This does \emph{not} prove the matrix inequality.
Second, using upstream's pinned Lean and Mathlib (the Comparator tools themselves built from their v4.34.0 tags), we ran \texttt{leanprover/comparator} on the scalar sharp Lieb--Thirring theorem claimed in the
\texttt{openai/math} repository; the Lean default kernel accepted it with only the three standard axioms (no external kernel was run). The Lean statement is scalar; the Comparator
certifies that the solution proves the challenge statement, not that the statement is a faithful formalisation. If it is faithful, the theorem would settle the scalar sharp inequality for $1/2<\gamma<3/2$,
which we understand to be an open conjecture (we did not search the literature exhaustively); that is why the statement audit, which has not been done, matters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim, scope and what is \emph{not} claimed}
For $m\ge1$, a matrix potential $W\ge0$ on $\R$ with values in $m\times m$ Hermitian matrices, and $\gamma\in(1/2,3/2)$ the inequality in question is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LT}
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(-\partial_x^2\otimes 1_m-W\bigr)_-^{\gamma}\ \le\ \Lone(\gamma)\int_{\R}\tr W(x)^{\gamma+1/2}\,dx,\qquad
 \Lone(\gamma)=\frac{2\bigl(\tfrac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+1/2}\bigr)^{\gamma-1/2}\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{2\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\gamma+\tfrac32)} .
\end{equation}
Here $\Lone$ is the constant for which equality holds at the one-bound-state potential $W=(r+1)\operatorname{sech}^2(rx)\,ee^{\dagger}$, $e$ a unit vector, with $r=1/(\gamma-\tfrac12)$
(one bound state $\operatorname{sech}^{\gamma-1/2}(rx)\,e$ at energy $-1$). For $m=1$ this is the scalar case of the Lieb--Thirring conjecture~\cite{LT} with the one-bound-state constant; for $\gamma\ge3/2$ the operator-valued inequality with the \emph{classical} constant is
a theorem of Laptev and Weidl~\cite{LW}. The classical constant coincides with $\Lone$ only at $\gamma=3/2$ (where it equals $3/16$), which we use as a control. The statement that~\eqref{eq:LT} holds for $m\ge2$ in the range $1/2<\gamma<3/2$, together with a classification of the equality cases,
appears as a headline claim of one family in the public \texttt{openai/math} repository (accessed 2026-10-08), which presents it as the work of an AI model. We did not assess its literature status.
The matrix claim is \emph{not} part of the Lean formalisation in that repository, which is scalar.

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} It does not prove or refute~\eqref{eq:LT} for $m\ge2$; it does not show the Lean statement is an adequate formalisation of any conjecture;
it contains no new theorem. A search that finds nothing is evidence of the instrument's reach, not of truth.

\section{Instrument and preregistration}
\paragraph{One-sided design.} The eigenvalues of $-\partial_x^2\otimes 1_m-W$ are computed on the Dirichlet box $[-L,L]$ ($L=24$) in a sine basis by Rayleigh--Ritz.
Dirichlet restriction can only raise eigenvalues (domain monotonicity) and a finite basis raises them again (min--max), so every computed negative eigenvalue lies above the true one, and the computed numerator $\sum|E_j|^\gamma$ is a lower bound of the whole-line numerator, up to quadrature error in the matrix elements
(Gauss--Legendre, $Q=1200$ nodes; $L=24$ in the campaigns, $L=14$, $Q=1600$ in the Hessian runs). The denominator $\int\tr W^{\gamma+1/2}$ is also computed by quadrature on the box, so the ratio is a lower bound only up to
quadrature and truncation error, which we measured at the $10^{-7}$ to $10^{-6}$ level. Potentials are $W=B^\dagger B$ with $B$ built from $\operatorname{sech}$ times Chebyshev polynomials in $\tanh$ with complex coefficients and shifts.
Candidate violations were to be re-evaluated on a refined grid and by an independent finite-difference scheme with Richardson extrapolation (agreement about $10^{-6}$); a candidate counted as certified only if the refined-grid excess exceeded $10^{-5}$ and the finite-difference excess exceeded $5\times10^{-6}$.
The verdict rule was interval-style and abstaining: VOID, VIOLATION\_CERTIFIED, UNDECIDED, NO\_VIOLATION\_FOUND.

\paragraph{Preregistration.} Hypotheses H-LT1 (no violation at $m=2$, then $m=3$), H-LT2 (structure of near-maximisers), H-LT3 (second variation at the embedded scalar extremiser), H-LT4 (instrument validity)
and H-LT5 (dual kinetic form) were registered with kill criteria and the sha256 of the frozen instrument and campaign code before any campaign cell was run; a few smoke values had been seen before registration (listed in the preregistration file),
the control rule C2 of the dual-form lane was relaxed after a first failure (disclosed in its preregistration), and registration times are local-clock commits. Seeds were ledger-enforced and never reused. Deviations are logged in each lane's \texttt{deviations.md}.

\subsection{An independent search space: the dual kinetic form (H-LT5)}
At $\gamma=1$, \eqref{eq:LT} implies for orthonormal families $\psi_1,\dots,\psi_N$ in $L^2(\R;\mathbb C^m)$, with $\rho=\sum_j\psi_j\psi_j^\dagger$, that
$\sum_j\|\psi_j'\|^2\ge\frac{\pi^2}{4}\int\tr\rho^3\,dx$, because $4/(27\Lone(1)^2)=\pi^2/4$. The kinetic energy is exact in the sine basis and $\int\tr\rho^3$ is evaluated on a periodic grid and re-checked on a $2^{24}$ grid and by Gauss--Legendre. The implication goes one way only: a violation of the dual form would violate~\eqref{eq:LT}, but not conversely, so this null result is weaker evidence than the spectral search.
Table~\ref{tab:dual} gives, for $m\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $N\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, the smallest relative excess of the quotient over $\pi^2/4$ found in 12 restarts per cell; the smallest value over all $\DualCells$ cells is $\DualMinExcess$ above, so nothing went below.
In $\DualNeverClose$ cells (those with $(m,N)\in\{(1,3),(1,4),(2,4)\}$) no restart came within $10^{-5}$ of $\pi^2/4$ although for $m=1$ the quotient is bounded below by $\pi^2/4$ if upstream's scalar theorem is correct and its Lean statement faithful (we audited neither); the search resolution there is poor, and a violation smaller than about $10^{-4}$ would be invisible.

\subsection{Structure of near-maximisers (H-LT2): the preregistered prediction was killed}
The preregistered prediction was that near-maximisers are, up to a constant unitary, direct sums of scalar solitons: a commutator statistic $C<0.01$.
On a snapshot of the data taken during the night, $\TwoRows$ near-maximiser rows existed, of which $\TwoAnalysed$ had stored parameters ($\TwoNotStored$ did not and were not analysed). Of these, $\TwoKilled$ met the kill rule
($R\ge(1-10^{-4})\Lone$ with $C>0.05$), $\TwoKilledOutside$ of them outside the $\gamma=3/2$ control; $\TwoIntermediate$ were intermediate and $\TwoHolds$ held the prediction.
An exploratory, not preregistered, follow-up suggests that kills from embedded and twisted starts are mostly tail noise of a scalar soliton plus a zero channel (one rotated-pair row at $\gamma=1.4$ is a non-commuting single-channel exception);
about half of the random-family kills remain non-commuting at the stricter threshold and are multi-channel, and for the rest tail noise is not excluded. Near-maximisers here means within the instrument resolution of $\Lone$.
We offer an untested explanation: spatially separated pieces with different eigenvector directions decouple asymptotically (so $R\approx\Lone$) without commuting across $x,y$, so the wording ``one constant unitary'' may be too strong.
This concerns the form of an equality-case classification, not the inequality.

\section{Comparator check of the scalar Lean formalisation}
The repository \texttt{openai/math} contains a Lean 4 formalisation with a challenge file \path{ComparatorChallenges/LiebThirring.lean} (statement, ending in \texttt{sorry}) and a solution module \path{OAI.Analysis.LiebThirring.Main}.
We installed upstream's own pinned stack on a separate disk: Lean 4.34.1, Mathlib at upstream's commit \texttt{d13f23b7}, and upstream's \texttt{lakefile} (\texttt{autoImplicit} off), with no edits.
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{lake build} of the Lieb--Thirring closure (38 files) succeeds, and \texttt{\#print axioms} for the four final theorems lists exactly \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}.
\item The \texttt{leanprover/comparator}, run with \texttt{landrun} (sandbox) and \texttt{lean4export} and the Lean default kernel, reports \emph{``\CmpOutput''} for \path{OAI.SharpLiebThirring.sharp_lieb_thirring}
with permitted axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}, \texttt{Classical.choice}.
\item Deviations: \texttt{lean4export} and the Comparator were built from their tag v4.34.0 sources with the toolchain file set to 4.34.1 (no 4.34.1 tag exists); \texttt{landrun} is commit 811cfff; the sandboxed run needs the toolchain's \texttt{bin} on \texttt{PATH} and \texttt{lake env};
no external kernel (nanoda) was run; the three controls were run after the main run.
\item Controls for the Comparator, run on the same setup: a matching statement is accepted; a different statement is rejected (``theorem statement do not match''); a proof using an extra axiom is rejected (``Illegal axiom detected'').
\item For the triangular-lattice energy theorem of the same repository we have only the native build under the same pin (229 files, no errors) and
the axiom list of \path{OAI.AtomicTriangular.universal_energy_minimum}, again exactly the three standard axioms. A Comparator run on it was still in progress when this version was written,
so \emph{no Comparator verdict is claimed for it here}.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Process lessons.} Before the pinned stack was available, an adapted copy compiled on a different Lean/Mathlib version needed a declared one-line rename for the Lieb--Thirring closure (\texttt{Set.equivOfEq} to \texttt{Equiv.setCongr}),
while the triangular closure failed on a tactic whose behaviour changed (\texttt{convert}/\texttt{simp} left an unreduced \texttt{if}); both compiled unmodified under the pin. The lesson is that version drift is easy to mistake for a proof defect.
An independent verifier of the first pilot also found that we had compiled with \texttt{autoImplicit} on while upstream's lakefile turns it off; the final result above uses upstream's setting.

\paragraph{What the Comparator does and does not certify.} It certifies that the solution proves a theorem whose statement equals the challenge statement and uses only permitted axioms. It does not certify that the statement formalises the Lieb--Thirring conjecture
(domain of test functions, extended-real sums, definition of the negative moment), which requires a human audit that has not been done. If the statement is faithful, it would settle the scalar sharp inequality for $1/2<\gamma<3/2$,
which we understand to be an open conjecture in the literature (we did not search the literature exhaustively); this is precisely why the audit matters. It says nothing about the matrix-valued case.

\section{Limitations}
(i) The instrument resolves differences of about $10^{-6}$ only; a violation of relative size below that is invisible. (ii) The search is local and finite: $336$ restarts at $m=2$ cannot cover the space of potentials.
(iii) The dual-form search did not converge in three cells. (iv) The near-maximiser analysis rests on a partial snapshot. (v) The Lean part is scalar. (vi) The statement audit is outstanding.
