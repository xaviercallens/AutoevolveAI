\begin{abstract}
Let $E(a,t)$ be the harmonic zero-point energy per particle ($\hbar=m=1$) of the planar Bravais lattices $L_t=P_tL_\rho$,
$P_t=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\t&1+t^2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, sheared from the triangular lattice $L_\rho$, for the Gaussian pair
potential $V(r)=e^{-\pi a r^2}$, and let $F(a,t)=\partial_t^2E(a,t)$ be its \emph{exact} second variation along the shear
(not the majorant of the predecessor B2, which cannot reach the sign changes).
We enclose $F$ two-sided by ball arithmetic, for \emph{every} $a$ in an interval and every $|t|\le10^{-8}$, and chain 174 such intervals.
\textbf{Result.} $F<0$ on all of $[0.425,\,1.579]$ and $F>0$ on all of $[1.584,\,4]$. Hence along the shear the triangular lattice is
\emph{not a local minimum} of the zero-point energy for every $a\in[0.425,1.579]$ ($E(a,t)<E(a,0)$ for $0<|t|\le10^{-8}$), and
$E(a,0)<E(a,t)$ for every $a\in[1.584,4]$ (a \emph{strict minimum along the shear}; a strict local minimum over lattice shapes needs further
assumptions, stated). Each of $(0.3745,0.379)$ and $(1.579,1.583)$ contains a zero of $a\mapsto F(a,0)$ (intermediate value theorem); write $a_-$ and $a_+$ for such zeros.
Uniqueness, and the absence of other zeros in the uncertified gaps, are not certified.
\textbf{Not claimed.} The full equivalence ``local minimum $\iff a>a_+$'' for all $a>0$ is \emph{not} claimed and is probably false: the earlier
preprint P2 sees another sign change near $a\approx0.123$ (Tier \tX). On the left of $a_-$ only isolated points are certified
($F>0$ at $a=0.33,0.35,0.36,0.37,0.372,0.3745$); the intervals in $(0.383,0.425)$ and everything below $0.33$ are not certified (at $a=0.25$ and
$0.30$ the certifier could not prove $\det B>0$).
\textbf{Tier split.} \tA\ (Lean 4.34.1 + Mathlib, axioms within $\{$propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound$\}$): the logic, the signs of 197
rational records, the interval-chain coverage, the intermediate-value step and the window lemmas; the theorems take the enclosures as hypotheses.
\tCb: the Arb enclosures (1{,}276{,}022 certified cells over 202 distinct run records, 0 failed), not kernel-checked. \tCh: that $F$ is the second derivative of $E$ (so $E$ is $C^2$ in $t$),
the mirror symmetry, continuity in $a$, and, for the strict-minimum claim over shapes, criticality, isotropy and the second-derivative test.
The headline is therefore Tier C overall. Controls: just inside ($a=0.40$), a tampered certificate, straddling intervals and a planted false LeanFlow sign are all rejected;
a 609-check floating-point cross-check (3 per run record, duplicate included) has 0 misses. The kernel replay (leanchecker) of \lean{Cert}, \lean{Controls}, \lean{Honest} and \lean{Basic} passes with rc~0.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting, predecessors, and what is and is not claimed}\label{sec:setting}
\textbf{Predecessors.} P2 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23653833, \tX) conjectured (QU-1) that the triangular lattice minimises $\Ezp$ among unit-covolume lattices
for completely monotone potentials and refuted it numerically for the Gaussian $e^{-\pi a r^2}$ at some $a$: P2's curvature $c(a)=\mathrm{Lap}\,\Ezp/4$
changes sign near $a\approx0.3767$ and $a\approx1.5815$ and, in addition, near $a\approx0.123$ (it is negative at $a=0.1$), and its values at $a=6,8$ are flagged
for precision. B2 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23655392) certified, at the single value $a=1$, that $\rho$ is not a local minimum, via a linear-response majorant of $E$.
N2 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23663551) certified the Yukawa case via a moving comparison matrix with exact curvature. N1, this paper, goes back to the Gaussian family and asks
for the \emph{sign pattern in $a$}, which needs the exact second variation rather than a majorant (Step~1 below).

\textbf{Tiers.} \tA: kernel-checked Lean (pinned Lean 4.34.1 and Mathlib; every declaration prints axioms within propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound; \lean{sorryAx} and smuggled
axioms rejected). \tL: literature or own earlier preprints, cited. \tCb: rigorous Arb ball arithmetic (python-flint 0.6.0, 96 bits, outward rounded), not kernel-checked.
\tCh: a written argument, checked by no machine and no human. \tX: floating point only, never supporting a claim. A claim is filed no higher than what it rests on.

\textbf{What is claimed.} (i) $F<0$ on $[0.425,1.579]$ and $F>0$ on $[1.584,4]$, for all $|t|\le10^{-8}$ \,\tA{}+\tCb; (ii) the two root enclosures \tA{}+\tCb{}+\tCh; (iii) the
consequences along the shear path \tA{}+\tCb{}+\tCh. \textbf{What is not claimed.} (a) The iff for all $a>0$. (b) Anything for $a<0.33$ or $a>4$. (c) Uniqueness of the roots.
(d) A strict local minimum over shapes without the extra assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:shape}. (e) Any statement about the total (classical plus zero-point) energy, other lattices,
other potentials, or three dimensions. The window $|t|\le10^{-8}$ is very small; the statements are local to the shear path.

\section{Step-by-step human demonstration, with the matching Lean statements}\label{sec:human}
\paragraph{Step 1: why B2's majorant cannot work near the roots \tX{}+\tCh.} B2 bounds $E$ above by a fixed-matrix majorant $U$ and proves $E''(0)\le U''(0)<0$ when $U''(0)<0$.
The gap is a relaxation term: $U''(0)-E''(0)=\tfrac12\int\tr(X^{-1}Y^2)\,d\kappa\ge0$ (N2, \texttt{PZP-C-0008}, \tCh), where $X=D_0^{1/2}$ and $XY+YX=D_0'$. So $U''\ge E''$ always.
At a root of $F(\cdot,0)$ one has $E''=0$, hence $U''\ge0$ there, and a negative $U''$ is impossible. Numerically (\tX, $N=64$, \texttt{runs/explore\_tierX\_N64.txt}) it is worse than the inequality demands:
$U''=+2.79$ at the root $0.3767$ and $+8.77$ at the root $1.5815$ (both $>0$), and $U''(0)<0$ only at $a=0.5,0.7,1$ of the 17 grid values ($-1.013$ at $0.5$; $+3.877$ at $1.3$), so its sign changes lie in $(0.4,0.5)$ and $(1.0,1.3)$.
No majorant of this type can classify the signs near $a_\pm$, and none gives a positive lower bound at all (it is an upper bound). We therefore enclose $F$ itself.

\paragraph{Step 2: the exact second variation \tCh{} (\texttt{PZC-C-0009}).} Let $H_n(t)=\mathrm{Hess}\,e^{-\pi a|P_tR_n|^2}$, $D_t(\kappa)=\sum_{n\ne0}(1-\cos2\pi\kappa\!\cdot\!n)H_n(t)$ and $E(a,t)=\tfrac12\int\tr D_t^{1/2}\,d\kappa$ over the Brillouin zone (B2's setting).
The matrix $D_t$ is $2\times2$, symmetric, and positive definite for $\kappa\ne0$ when the lattice is dynamically stable. \emph{Identity.} For $S=D^{1/2}$, Cayley--Hamilton gives $(\tr S)^2=\tr S^2+2\det S=\tr D+2\sqrt{\det D}$, hence
\[\tr\sqrt D=\sqrt{\tr D+2\sqrt{\det D}}.\]
With the Duffy coordinates of B2 ($\kappa=s\,v(t')$, Jacobian $s/2$, $D_t=s^2B_t$, $B_t=\sum_n\theta_n^2f(s\theta_n)H_n(t)$, $f(x)=(1-\cos x)/x^2$) we get $\tr\sqrt{D_t}=s\,\tau$ with
$\tau=\sqrt{T+2\delta}$, $T=\tr B_t$, $\delta=\sqrt{\det B_t}$, and $F=\int g$ with the regular integrand $g=s^2\tau''/4$ (the prefactors and triangle bookkeeping are those of B2 and N2, and so is the Brillouin-zone coverage; both are inherited, filed \tCh, and checked here only at Tier~\tX{} by agreement with P2's independent $c(a)$ and roots. A positive overall prefactor cannot change any sign claimed here, whereas a missing Brillouin-zone region could). Differentiating twice in $t$ gives closed forms:
\[\delta''=\frac{(\det B)''}{2\delta}-\frac{((\det B)')^2}{4\delta^3},\qquad
\tau''=\frac{T''+2\delta''}{2\tau}-\frac{(T'+2\delta')^2}{4\tau^3},\]
with $B',B''$ from the closed forms of $H_n',H_n''$ (context only: B2's Lean \lean{hasDerivAt\_*} cover the scalar pieces, but no N1 theorem uses them). There is no inequality in this step: $F$ is the exact second derivative, and the only singularities are where $\det B=0$ or $\tr B=0$, which Step~3 excludes.
\tX{} check of the closed form against finite differences of $E$ (17 values of $a$): agreement to $\le4.92\times10^{-4}$ (at $a=2$), e.g.\ $E''(0)=-7.695875$ (closed form) against $-7.695997$ (FD) at $a=1$, and $c=E''/8$ reproduces P2's $c(a)$ ($0.18806$ against $0.188$ at $a=0.25$).
Lean: the identity and the closed forms are \emph{not} formalised; Lean sees $F$ only through its enclosure and the hypothesis \lean{hE'} of Step~6.

\paragraph{Step 3: an enclosure affine in $a$, which allows chaining \tCb{} (\texttt{PZC-C-0001..0004}).}
For an interval $[a_c-\Delta,a_c+\Delta]$ (a thin point has $\Delta=0$) and the window $|t|\le t_0$ (entering as the ball $[-t_0,t_0]$ in $H_n(t)$), the certifier \texttt{n1\_certify2.py} returns three balls $Q$, $D$, $R$ with
\[F(a,t)-(a-a_c)\,d\ \in\ Q+R\quad\text{for some }d\in D,\ \text{for every }a\text{ in the interval and every }|t|\le t_0 .\]
$Q$ and $D$ are midpoint cell sums of $g$ and $\partial_ag$ at the exact centre $a_c$; $R$ is the second-order Lagrange remainder in $(s,t',a)$ on cell~$\times$~interval (first-order terms in $s,t'$ integrate to zero on a square cell, the first-order term in $a$ is $(a-a_c)d$).
Why affine: a plain ball in $a$ of half-width $\Delta$ would cost a first-order error $\Delta\,\sup|\partial_aF|$, which swamps $F$ near the roots where $F\approx0$; the affine form leaves only the second-order remainder $O(\Delta^2)$ in $R$, so the sign certificate $q_{hi}+c_{\max}<0$ (resp.\ $q_{lo}+c_{\min}>0$) survives for intervals of width $0.001$ to $0.054$. Here $c_{\min},c_{\max}$ are the extreme corner values of $(a-a_c)d$ over $[a_{lo},a_{hi}]\times[d_{lo},d_{hi}]$.
A cell counts only if $\det B>0$ and $\tr B>0$ are certified on the whole cell ball~$\times$~$a$-interval~$\times$~$t$-window; a failed cell makes the record uncertified and it never enters Lean (Section~\ref{sec:tables}). In Lean the record and its meaning are:
\begin{lstlisting}
structure Rec where  lo hi ac qlo qhi dlo dhi : Q
def Rec.cmax r := max (max ((lo-ac)*dlo) ((lo-ac)*dhi)) (max ((hi-ac)*dlo) ((hi-ac)*dhi))   -- cmin: min
def allNeg (L : List Rec) : Bool := L.all (fun r => decide (r.qhi + r.cmax < 0))
def allPos (L : List Rec) : Bool := L.all (fun r => decide (0 < r.qlo + r.cmin))
def Encloses (F : R -> R -> R) (t0 : R) (L : List Rec) : Prop :=
  forall r in L, forall a, r.lo <= a -> a <= r.hi -> forall t, |t| <= t0 ->
    exists d, r.dlo <= d /\ d <= r.dhi /\ r.qlo <= F a t - (a - r.ac)*d /\ F a t - (a - r.ac)*d <= r.qhi
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{Encloses} \emph{is exactly the \tCb{} input}: it is a hypothesis of every assembled theorem, never proved. The lemmas \lean{mul\_le\_corners} and \lean{corners\_le\_mul} (\tA) bound $(a-a_c)d$ by the corner values; \lean{rec\_upper} and \lean{rec\_lower} (\tA) turn an \lean{Encloses} record into $F\le q_{hi}+c_{\max}$ and $F\ge q_{lo}+c_{\min}$.

\paragraph{Step 4: coverage of $[A,B]$ by a chain of intervals \tA{} (\texttt{PZC-A-0002, -0003}).} A list of records chains from $A$ to $B$ if the first starts at $A$, consecutive records satisfy $hi_i=lo_{i+1}$, and the last ends at $B$. Then every real $a\in[A,B]$ lies in some record. The kernel evaluates the Boolean on the actual lists by \lean{decide +kernel}:
\begin{lstlisting}
def chainFrom : Q -> Q -> List Rec -> Bool
  | _, _, [] => false
  | x, B, r :: rs => decide (r.lo = x) && decide (r.lo <= r.hi) && (decide (r.hi = B) || chainFrom r.hi B rs)
theorem covers_of_chain : forall L A B, chainFrom A B L = true ->
    forall a : R, A <= a -> a <= B -> exists r in L, r.lo <= a /\ a <= r.hi
theorem chain_neg0 : chainFrom (17/40) (1579/1000) Lnegseg0 = true := by decide +kernel
theorem chain_pos0 : chainFrom (198/125) 4 Lposseg0 = true := by decide +kernel
theorem sign_neg : allNeg Lneg = true := by decide +kernel
theorem sign_pos : allPos Lpos = true := by decide +kernel
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{Lnegseg0} has 71 records from $17/40=0.425$ to $1579/1000$, \lean{Lposseg0} has 103 from $198/125=1.584$ to $4$; \lean{Lneg} and \lean{Lpos} add the thin points and one more interval (197 records in all, 113 positive and 84 negative). The kernel checks that the records chain (no gap, no overlap beyond endpoints) and that every one has its sign.
Assembling with Step~3:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem segment_neg0 (F : R -> R -> R) (h : Encloses F t0 Lneg) :
    forall a, 17/40 <= a -> a <= 1579/1000 -> forall t, |t| <= t0 -> F a t < 0
theorem segment_pos0 (F : R -> R -> R) (h : Encloses F t0 Lpos) :
    forall a, 198/125 <= a -> a <= 4 -> forall t, |t| <= t0 -> 0 < F a t
\end{lstlisting}
This is T1 and T2, in the exact form: conditional on \lean{Encloses}.

\paragraph{Step 5: the roots by the intermediate value theorem \tA{}+\tCh{} (\texttt{PZC-A-0005}, \texttt{PZC-C-0011}).}
The thin-point records at $a=0.3745$ ($F\in[0.0177,0.0949]$) and $a=0.379$ ($F\in[-0.0978,-0.0235]$) have opposite certified signs, and so do $a=1.579$ ($F\in[-0.0423,-0.0195]$) and $a=1.583$ ($F\in[0.0075,0.0303]$) (values at $t\in[-t_0,t_0]$). If $a\mapsto F(a,0)$ is continuous on each bracket (\tCh: it is analytic in $a$, being a uniformly convergent lattice sum of smooth integrals; not formalised), the Mathlib intermediate value theorem gives a zero strictly inside:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem root_in_gap (f : R -> R) (hlt : aL < aR) (hf : ContinuousOn f (Icc aL aR)) (hL : 0 < f aL) (hR : f aR < 0) :
    exists a in Ioo aL aR, f a = 0                      -- root_in_gap' : the mirrored signs
theorem classification (F) (hp : Encloses F t0 Lpos) (hn : Encloses F t0 Lneg)
    (hcm : ContinuousOn (fun a => F a 0) (Icc 0.3745 0.379)) (hcp : ContinuousOn (fun a => F a 0) (Icc 1.579 1.583)) :
    (forall r in Lpos, ... 0 < F a t) /\ (forall r in Lneg, ... F a t < 0) /\
    (exists a in Ioo 0.3745 0.379, F a 0 = 0) /\ (exists a in Ioo 1.579 1.583, F a 0 = 0)
\end{lstlisting}
(\lean{brackets\_ordered}, a \lean{decide +kernel} fact, checks that the bracket points are the thin records with $lo=hi$ and are ordered.) P2's \tX{} roots $0.37668$ and $1.58148$ lie inside the two intervals. Uniqueness is \emph{not} certified: no sign of $\partial_aF$ was certified on the enclosures.
T7 follows from T1, T2 and the thin record at $1.583$ (so it rests on the \tCb{} enclosures; no Lean theorem states it): a zero of $F(\cdot,t)$ in $[0.425,4]$ cannot lie in $[0.425,1.579]$ or $[1.584,4]$, and not at $1.583$.

\paragraph{Step 6: from the sign of $F$ to the energy along the path \tA{} logic, \tCh{} inputs (\texttt{PZC-A-0001, -0006}).} \emph{Window lemma.} If $E$ is differentiable on $[-t_0,t_0]$ with derivative $E'$, $E'$ is differentiable with derivative $F$, and $F\le B$ there, then $E(t)-\tfrac B2t^2$ has second derivative $\le0$, hence is concave, and
\[E(t)+E(-t)-2E(0)\le B\,t^2\qquad(|t|\le t_0);\]
with $F\ge B$ the function is convex and the inequality reverses. (Mathlib \lean{concaveOn\_of\_hasDerivWithinAt2\_nonpos} and the convex twin.) With the mirror symmetry $E(-t)=E(t)$ this gives, for $B<0$: $E(t)<E(0)$ for $0<|t|\le t_0$, so $0$ is not a local minimum; for $B>0$: $E(0)<E(t)$.
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem window_upper {E E' E'' : R -> R} {t0 B : R} (hE : forall t in Icc (-t0) t0, HasDerivAt E (E' t) t)
    (hE' : forall t in Icc (-t0) t0, HasDerivAt E' (E'' t) t) (hB : forall t in Icc (-t0) t0, E'' t <= B)
    (ht : t in Icc (-t0) t0) : E t + E (-t) - 2 * E 0 <= B * t ^ 2      -- window_lower : >=
theorem not_localMin_of_curv_neg (ht0 : 0 < t0) (hBneg : B < 0) (hE) (hE') (hB) (hsym : forall t, E (-t) = E t) :
    Not (IsLocalMin E 0) /\ forall t, 0 < t -> t <= t0 -> E t < E 0
theorem strict_gt_of_curv_pos (hBpos : 0 < B) (hE) (hE') (hB) (hsym) :
    forall t, t != 0 -> |t| <= t0 -> E 0 < E t
theorem segment_neg0_path (E E' F) (h : Encloses F t0 Lneg) (hE : forall a t, t in Icc (-t0) t0 -> HasDerivAt (E a) (E' a t) t)
    (hE' : forall a t, ... -> HasDerivAt (E' a) (F a t) t) (hsym : forall a t, E a (-t) = E a t) :
    forall a, 17/40 <= a -> a <= 1579/1000 -> Not (IsLocalMin (E a) 0) /\ forall t, 0 < t -> t <= t0 -> E a t < E a 0
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{segment\_pos0\_path} is the same with $[198/125,4]$ and conclusion $E\,a\,0<E\,a\,t$ for $t\ne0$; \lean{along\_path} gives the per-record version, which includes the thin points. The hypotheses \lean{hE}, \lean{hE'}, \lean{hsym} are exactly the \tCh{} inputs:
(1) $E(a,\cdot)$ is $C^2$ with second derivative $F$ on the window (differentiation under the Brillouin-zone integral: the Duffy integrand is jointly smooth and bounded on the compact domain because $\det B>0$ is certified on every cell); (2) $E(a,-t)=E(a,t)$ (the reflection $\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ preserves $L_\rho$ and conjugates $P_t$ to $P_{-t}$, B2 \S2.4; true for every radial $V$).
At the window edges $t=\pm t_0$ the differentiability needs a neighbourhood of the closed window; the certified margins are strict, and the gap is closed by continuity (stated, not separately certified, as in N2).

\paragraph{Step 7: from the path to lattice shapes \tCh{}.} Since $\tau(t)=\rho+\sqrt3\,t+O(t^2)$ (B2 \S2.5), small $t\neq0$ give shapes $\ne\rho$ mod $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ arbitrarily close to $\rho$. Hence T4 (inside) says: arbitrarily near $\rho$ there are unit-covolume lattices with strictly smaller $\Ezp$, so $\rho$ is \emph{not a local minimum over shapes}; no criticality or isotropy is used. For the outside side see Section~\ref{sec:shape}.

\section{Scope of ``strict local minimum over shapes'': extra \tCh{} assumptions}\label{sec:shape}
T5 is a statement along one curve through $\rho$. To pass to ``$\rho$ is a strict local minimum of $\Ezp$ over unit-covolume lattice shapes'' for $a\in[1.584,4]$ (T6) one needs, \emph{all as written arguments}:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=1pt
\item $\Ezp$ is of class $C^2$ on a neighbourhood of $\rho$ in the shape space (the upper half plane modulo $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$), including $C^2$ in the shape coordinates, not only along the shear;
\item $\rho$ is a critical point of $\Ezp$ and its Hessian is isotropic. Both follow from the order-3 stabiliser of $\rho$ (P2 \S3.1, \tCh/\tX), which acts on the tangent plane by a rotation of angle $2\pi/3$, so an invariant quadratic form is a multiple of the metric and an invariant linear form is $0$; they use $C^2$;
\item the identification $F(a,0)=2\,\mathrm{Lap}\,\Ezp(\rho)$ (B2 \S2.6; it uses $|\tau'(0)|^2/y^2=4$ at $\rho$, i.e.\ the normalisation of the shear parameter);
\item the 2D second-derivative test: a positive definite Hessian at a critical point is a strict local minimum.
\end{enumerate}
Item~4 is classical analysis and not formalised here; the Lean of P2 only contains the algebra \lean{rot120\_comm\_symm} and \lean{rot120\_fixed\_vec}. With these four, $F(a,0)>0$ gives a positive definite Hessian, and T6 follows. Without them the proven statement is T5 (strict minimum \emph{along the shear}). Dually, T4 does not need any of them because a single descent direction suffices.
Nothing in this section is used by T1--T5, T7.

\section{The certifier, briefly}\label{sec:engine}
Everything here is \tCb{} and lives in \texttt{data/code/n1\_certify2.py} (sha256 below); it is not kernel-checked.
\textbf{Jets.} Every quantity is a second-order forward-mode jet in $(s,t',a)$ over the cell ball, the $a$-interval and the $t$-window, computed in lockstep with a first-order jet at the thin centre; after each operation the ball value and gradient are replaced by mean-value forms around the centre and intersected with the naive enclosure. This tightening is essential: without it $\det B>0$ cannot be certified at small $a$. Per-$n$ Gaussian Hessians over the $a$-interval use Taylor forms in $a$ around $a_c$.
\textbf{Lattice tail.} One bound is added to every jet component of every entry: shells of width $1/4$, polynomial factors at the outer radius and Gaussian factors at the inner radius (no monotonicity assumed), point count $\pi(r+1)^2$, weight bounds for all partials of $\theta^2f(s\theta)$ up to order $2$, an $a$-factor $1+L+L^2$, and a geometric remainder whose ratio is \emph{computed} and must be $<1$. (B2's audit had flagged two shortcuts there: evaluation at the inner shell radius and an asserted ratio; both are fixed.)
\textbf{Cost.} Small $a$ needs many lattice terms (99 half-terms at $a=0.37$, 147 at $0.25$) and a nearly singular $B$ near $\Gamma$; $R$ grows fast with the $a$-width. Total sweep CPU: $13{,}685$~s over the 210 run records, $13{,}599$~s over the distinct records, the duplicate excluded (cores 4,5; other users' jobs shared core 5 for part of the time). Settings: $N=32$ or $64$, depth $1$ or $2$, 96 bits, $t_0=10^{-8}$. Cells certified: $1{,}284{,}310$ (232{,}178 refined) over the 203 run records with ok status, one of which ($a=1.584$) is a duplicate; over the 202 distinct records $1{,}276{,}022$ (232{,}146 refined); $0$ failed. Of the 203 ok records, 198 carry a sign and 5 are inconclusive (2 straddling a root, 3 too wide).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Partial result.} Only $a\in[0.425,4]$ is classified, plus isolated points left of $a_-$. The full iff for all $a>0$ is not claimed, and P2's sign change near $a\approx0.123$ (and the precision-flagged values at $a=6,8$) show that no simple iff can be expected. Roots are enclosed, not shown unique.
\item \textbf{Mostly Tier~C.} The enclosures (\tCb) are executed code, not proofs; the identity of $F$ with $\partial_t^2E$, differentiation under the integral, mirror symmetry, continuity in $a$ and Section~\ref{sec:shape} are written arguments checked by no machine and no human referee.
\item \textbf{Tiny window and one path.} $|t|\le10^{-8}$, shear path only, harmonic approximation, Bravais lattices, two dimensions, Gaussian family only, $\hbar=m=1$. The result concerns $\Ezp$ alone; the classical energy contributes separately and nothing follows for the total energy.
\item \textbf{AI audit only.} One independent AI audit (ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS, no blocking item) exists; its corrections are applied here. No human has audited anything; no statement lock.
\item \textbf{Kernel replay.} \lean{Cert}, \lean{Controls}, \lean{Honest} and \lean{Basic} replay with rc~0 under leanchecker (auditor-built oleans, Section~\ref{sec:lean}); the replay is not an axiom audit.
\item \textbf{Over-strong Lean hypotheses (known, sound).} In \texttt{Cert.lean} the hypotheses \lean{hE}, \lean{hE'} and \lean{hsym} are quantified over every real $a$ (including $a\le0$, where $\Ezp$ is undefined) rather than over $a$ in the record's range. This is not unsound ($E$ and $F$ are free variables and can be extended arbitrarily outside the range) and the proofs use only $a$ in range, but it makes instantiation awkward and the hypotheses stronger than their gloss. The Lean files and generated certificates are unchanged in this revision; the fix would be to quantify over $\mathrm{lo}\le a\le \mathrm{hi}$ in \texttt{n1\_make\_cert.py}.
\item \textbf{Repository guards fail for pre-existing reasons.} \texttt{python antigravity\_guard.py} rc~1 and \texttt{python test\_rigor\_guard.py} rc~1 (hallucinated-import reports for packages absent from the venv, such as \texttt{datasets}, \texttt{peft}, \texttt{trl}, \texttt{flint}, and hollow tests in \texttt{tests/test\_k3\_anse\_improvements.py}, \texttt{tests/test\_scientific\_rigor\_v14.py}); neither output mentions any N1 file (\texttt{data/guard\_*.txt}). \texttt{pytest tests/} was not run for this paper. ``Guards pass'' is not claimed.
\item \textbf{Cross-check is consistency, not independence.} The 609-check comparison is floating point with the same formula (Section~\ref{sec:controls}). The N1 engine was developed iteratively (earlier versions in \texttt{data/code/}); the 19 Lean input files were all produced with one version, recorded in each run JSON by sha256 of \texttt{n1\_certify2.py} and of the imported helper \texttt{n1\_certify.py}.
\item \textbf{python-flint} 0.6.0 is not in the project venv; re-running needs that version. One record ($a=1.584$, \texttt{runs/sweep\_ap\_tight.json}) is a duplicate (114 positive records in the runs, 113 in Lean; the Lean list is de-duplicated: 197 records); counts of cells and CPU time are given with and without it.
\item \textbf{Not novelty-checked.} No literature search was made for prior certified results on zero-point lattice energies; no novelty is claimed.
\end{itemize}
