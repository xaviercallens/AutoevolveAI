\begin{abstract}
For the planar Yukawa pair potentials $V_\kappa(r)=e^{-\kappa r}/r$ with $\kappa=1$ and $\kappa=2$ and unit-covolume Bravais lattices, let $E(t)$ be the harmonic zero-point energy per particle ($\hbar=m=1$) along the symmetric shear $L_t=P_tL_\rho$, $P_t=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\t&1+t^2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, starting at the triangular lattice $L_\rho$.
We show $E(t)<E(0)$ for every $0<|t|\le10^{-5}$, so \textbf{the triangular lattice is not a local minimum of the zero-point energy over lattice shapes}, although it is the classical (static) energy minimiser for these potentials~\tL\ (H2, itself conditional on an upstream Lean theorem and not re-checked here). The linear-response majorant of the earlier Gaussian result (B2) cannot give this: its curvature $\Ulin''(0)$ is certified positive here ($[2.393,2.464]$ and $[2.688,2.723]$). We replace the fixed comparison matrix by a moving one, $X_t=D_0^{1/2}+tY$ with $XY+YX=D_0'$; the resulting majorant $U_2$ satisfies $U_2\ge E$, $U_2(0)=E(0)$ and $U_2''(0)=E''(0)$ exactly, and a window bound gives $U_2''\le M_{hi}<0$ with $M_{hi}=-2.03704$ ($\kappa=1$) and $-1.86854$ ($\kappa=2$).

\textbf{Per potential} (details in Section~\ref{sec:table}); two of the six preregistered potentials are certified.

\section{Setting, predecessors and what was preregistered}\label{sec:setting}
The lineage is: P2~\cite{p2} found numerically (\tX) that the triangular lattice is not the minimiser of the harmonic zero-point energy $\Ezp$ for several completely monotone potentials; H2~\cite{h2} proves (conditionally on an upstream Lean theorem, \tL\ here) that the triangular lattice \emph{is} the classical energy minimiser for planar Yukawa potentials at every density; B2~\cite{b2} certified ``not a local minimum'' for the single Gaussian $e^{-\pi r^2}$. The present item asks whether the physically relevant potentials (screened Coulomb, dipolar excitons, Riesz) can be treated the same way. For Yukawa the answer is yes, and the title's contrast is the point: \emph{classically optimal (H2, conditional) but not zero-point optimal}. ``Planar Yukawa'' means, as in H2, the 3D-screened Coulomb potential $e^{-\kappa r}/r$ between particles confined to a plane; it is \emph{not} the 2D Debye--H\"uckel Green function $K_0(\kappa r)$, which is not treated.

\paragraph{The functional.} Let $L_\rho=c\mathbb Z(1,0)+c\mathbb Z(\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2)$, $c^2=2/\sqrt3$, and $R_n(t)=P_tR_n(0)$ for integer labels $n=(m,l)$. Put $H_n(t)=\hess V(R_n(t))$ and, at reduced zone coordinate $\kappa\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^2$ (here $\kappa$ is the \emph{zone coordinate}; the Yukawa screening parameter is written $\kappa_Y\in\{1,2\}$ only where needed),
\[D_t(\kappa)=\sum_{n\ne0}\bigl(1-\cos2\pi\kappa\!\cdot\! n\bigr)H_n(t),\qquad E(t)=\tfrac12\int\tr D_t(\kappa)^{1/2}\,d\kappa .\]
Labels and $\kappa$ are held fixed as $t$ varies (strain frame, no rotation); $\det P_t=1$; the lattice shape is $\tau(t)=\rho+\sqrt3\,t+O(t^2)$. \tCh{} For a radial $V=g(|R|^2)$ one has $H=2g'(q)I+4g''(q)RR^{\mathsf T}$, $q=|R|^2$.

\paragraph{Preregistration.} Item N2 of \texttt{results/proposal3/2026-10-26/preregistration.json}: ``for each potential refuted numerically in P2, extend B2's linear-response majorant certificate (with rigorous lattice-sum tails for algebraic decay) to certify $\Ezp(L_t)<\Ezp(L_\rho)$ for small shears; negative control: a potential where P2 found the triangular lattice a local minimum (e.g.\ Riesz $s=6$ or Gaussian $a=2$) must not certify''. Priors: $0.5$ per potential, $0.3$ for all six. Kill rule: ``report per potential; slowly decaying tails (Riesz 3, dipolar) may defeat the certificate: report the obstacle''. Six potentials were listed: Yukawa $\kappa=1,2$, dipolar $d=0.1,0.5$, Riesz $s=3,4$.
\textbf{Deviations, stated up front.} (i)~The method is \emph{not} B2's linear-response majorant but a moving-matrix one, because B2's fails (Section~\ref{sec:human}, Step~1). (ii)~Riesz $s=4$ was not attempted because \tX{} evidence suggests the local statement is false (Section~\ref{sec:riesz4}). (iii)~The negative control is Gaussian $a=2$ (an allowed choice in the text), not Riesz $6$. (iv)~Two of six potentials are certified, so the ``all six'' prior ($0.3$) did not materialise; the per-potential prior ($0.5$) was met for two of the six.

\section{Step-by-step human demonstration, with the matching Lean statements}\label{sec:human}
Throughout, $X=D_0(\kappa)^{1/2}$, $D'=\partial_tD_t|_{t=0}$, $D''=\partial_t^2D_t|_{t=0}$, and $S_t=D_t^{1/2}$. Lean listings are transliterated to ASCII (full text in \texttt{data/lean/}).

\paragraph{Step 1: B2's linear-response majorant cannot work here \tCb{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0004}).} B2 bounds $E$ above by the \emph{fixed}-matrix majorant
\[\Ulin(t)=\tfrac14\int\bigl[\tr X+\tr(X^{-1}D_t)\bigr]d\kappa,\qquad \Ulin''(0)=\tfrac14\int\tr(X^{-1}D'')\,d\kappa .\]
It is affine in $D_t$, which made it computable, and it works when $\Ulin''(0)<0$ (the Gaussian $a=1$ case: $-1.92$). Here it does not: the Arb enclosures are $\Ulin''(0)\in[2.39298,2.46426]$ ($\kappa_Y=1$) and $[2.68830,2.72310]$ ($\kappa_Y=2$), strictly positive, while the floating-point $E''(0)$ is negative ($-3.107$, $-2.549$). Because $\Ulin''(0)>0$ is a property of $\Ulin$ itself, no choice of window constant can rescue it; it also holds for all six physical potentials at Tier~\tX{} (positive $+2.4$ to $+33.2$). The gap is exactly the relaxation term of Step~3: $\Ulin''(0)-E''(0)=\tfrac12\int\tr(X^{-1}Y^2)\,d\kappa\ge0$.

\paragraph{Step 2: the moving comparison matrix \tCh{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0007}).} The idea is to let the comparison matrix follow $S_t$ to first order. Differentiating $S_t^2=D_t$ gives $XS_0'+S_0'X=D'$, so $S_0'=Y$ is the unique symmetric solution of the $2\times2$ Sylvester equation
\[XY+YX=D',\]
a linear $3\times3$ system for the entries of $Y$, uniquely solvable because the eigenvalues of $Y\mapsto XY+YX$ are $\lambda_i+\lambda_j>0$ for $X\succ0$ (solved by Cramer's rule in the code).
Put $X_t=X+tY$. Where $X_t$ is positive definite (\tCb\ check on every cell and every $|u|\le10^{-5}$, \texttt{PZP-C-0003}; margins $q_{lo}=0.6633$ and $0.5739$), the pointwise inequality of B2's Lean lemma applies with $X:=X_t$, $S:=S_t$:
\[2\tr D_t^{1/2}\le \tr X_t+\tr(X_t^{-1}D_t)=:f_\kappa(t),\qquad\text{equality at }t=0 .\]
Integrating, $E(t)\le U_2(t):=\tfrac14\int f_\kappa(t)\,d\kappa$ and $U_2(0)=E(0)$. In Lean the pointwise inequality is B2's \lean{ProgZeroPointCert.two\_trace\_le\_of\_inverse}, a $2\times2$ trace inequality (\tA, \texttt{CZP-A-0001} of B2). The lemma is imported by N2's module, but \emph{no Lean theorem applies it here}: in \texttt{Basic.lean} the resulting inequality $E\le U_2$ on the window is only the hypothesis \lean{hEU}, discharged by this lemma plus the positivity checks and the integration, i.e.\ \tCb\ and \tCh, not \tA:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem two_trace_le_of_inverse (x11 x12 x22 p11 p12 p22 s11 s12 s22 : R) (hp : 0 < p11)
    (hdet : 0 <= p11*p22 - p12^2) (h11 : ...) (h12 : ...) (h21 : ...) (h22 : ...) :
    2*(s11+s22) <= (x11+x22) + (p11*(s11^2+s12^2) + 2*p12*(s12*(s11+s22)) + p22*(s12^2+s22^2))
\end{lstlisting}
The integration over $\kappa$ and the identification $S_t=D_t^{1/2}$ are glue.

\paragraph{Step 3: the curvature is exact, $U_2''(0)=E''(0)$ \tCh{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0008}).} Since $S_t=X+tY+O(t^2)$, we have $S_t-X_t=O(t^2)$. The pointwise gap is
\[f_\kappa(t)-2\tr S_t=\tr\bigl(X_t^{-1}(S_t-X_t)^2\bigr)=O(t^4)\]
(expand $\tr(P(S-X)^2)=\tr(PS^2)-2\tr S+\tr X$, $P=X_t^{-1}$, as in B2's proof of the lemma). So $f_\kappa$ and $2\tr S_t$ agree to third order at $t=0$. Explicitly,
\[f_\kappa''(0)=\tr(X^{-1}D'')-2\tr(X^{-1}Y^2)=2\tr S_0'',\]
where the second equality uses $D''=S''X+2Y^2+XS''$ and cyclicity of the trace. Hence $U_2''(0)=\tfrac12\int\tr S_0''\,d\kappa=E''(0)$. Compare the fixed-matrix majorant, whose second derivative lacks the term $-2\tr(X^{-1}Y^2)$: $\Ulin''(0)-U_2''(0)=\tfrac12\int\tr(X^{-1}Y^2)\,d\kappa$, the relaxation term of Step~1 (about $5.5$ for $\kappa_Y=1$ by Tier~\tX{} values: $2.43-(-3.107)$). \emph{The majorant loses nothing at second order.} Near $\Gamma$ all of this is done with the Duffy scaling $D=s^2B$ of B2 so that the integrands are regular; that integral version is part of the \tCh{} glue.

\paragraph{Step 4: isotropy, so the shear direction loses nothing \tCh{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0011}).} The Hessian of $\Ezp$ over lattice shapes at $\rho$ is isotropic because $\rho$ has an order-3 stabiliser in $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ (P2 section~3.1, itself Tier~C). Therefore $E''(0)$ does not depend on the strain direction, and since $U_2''(0)=E''(0)$ the shear path of B2 is as good as any other for every potential (assuming $\Ezp$ is $C^2$ at $\rho$, for paths with equal $|\tau'(0)|$; not used by the headline). B2's frame dependence was a property of $\Ulin$ only.

\paragraph{Step 5: the Taylor window lemma \tA{} (\texttt{PZP-A-0001}).} We need a bound on $U_2(t)+U_2(-t)-2U_2(0)$ over the whole window, not just at $0$. If $f$ is twice differentiable on $[-t,t]$ with $f''\le K$ there, then $f(t)+f(-t)-2f(0)\le t^2K$. \emph{Proof.} Put $\varphi(u)=f'(u)-f'(-u)-2Ku$; then $\varphi'(u)=f''(u)+f''(-u)-2K\le0$ and $\varphi(0)=0$, so $\varphi\le0$ on $[0,t]$. Put $\psi(u)=f(u)+f(-u)-Ku^2$; then $\psi'=\varphi\le0$, so $\psi(t)\le\psi(0)$, i.e.\ $f(t)+f(-t)-Kt^2\le2f(0)$. $\square$ In Lean (\texttt{Taylor.lean}, two applications of \lean{antitoneOn\_of\_deriv\_nonpos}):
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem second_diff_le_of_deriv2_le {f f1 f2 : R -> R} {t K : R} (ht : 0 <= t)
    (h1 : forall u in Set.Icc (-t) t, HasDerivAt f (f1 u) u)
    (h2 : forall u in Set.Icc (-t) t, HasDerivAt f1 (f2 u) u)
    (hK : forall u in Set.Icc (-t) t, f2 u <= K) :
    f t + f (-t) - 2 * f 0 <= t ^ 2 * K
\end{lstlisting}
This replaces B2's cruder esum perturbation bound by a single uniform second-derivative bound.

\paragraph{Step 6: the window enclosure \tCb{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0001, -0002}).} Let $M(u)=\tfrac14\int f_\kappa''(u)\,d\kappa=U_2''(u)$ \tCh{} (differentiation under the integral sign, \texttt{PZP-C-0009}: the integrand and its first two $t$-derivatives are bounded on cells times window, which the Arb run bounds). The certifier encloses $M(u)$ with $u$ a \emph{ball} $[-t_0,t_0]$, $t_0=10^{-5}$: for every fixed $|u|\le t_0$ the cell sum encloses $M(u)$, and the reported $M$ is an enclosure valid for all such $u$ at once (quantifier order: $\sup_u\int$, not $\int\sup_u$; this corrects the docstring of the code, Section~\ref{sec:lim}). Results: $M\in[-4.16483,-2.03704]$ ($\kappa_Y=1$), $M\in[-3.21751,-1.86854]$ ($\kappa_Y=2$). The Lean hypotheses \lean{hd1}, \lean{hd2} at the endpoints $u=\pm t_0$ need $U_2$ to be differentiable on a neighbourhood of $\pm t_0$, while the positivity checks cover only the closed window $|u|\le t_0$; the gap is closed by continuity because the margins are strict, an argument stated here and not separately certified (\texttt{PZP-C-0009}). Both upper endpoints are negative; $E''(0)=U_2''(0)$ lies in the same interval, and the Tier~\tX{} values $-3.107$ and $-2.549$ are inside.

\paragraph{Step 7: logic and assembly \tA{} (\texttt{PZP-A-0002 to -0004}).} With $M\le M_{hi}<0$: for $0<t\le t_0$, $E(t)+E(-t)\le U_2(t)+U_2(-t)\le2U_2(0)+t^2M<2E(0)$, so some one of $E(\pm t)$ is below $E(0)$, hence $0$ is not a local minimum; with the mirror symmetry $E(-t)=E(t)$ (any radial $V$: $\sigma=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ preserves $L_\rho$ and $\sigma P_t\sigma=P_{-t}$; \tCh{}, \texttt{PZP-C-0010}) both are. Lean (\texttt{Basic.lean}, which imports B2's \lean{Programmes.ZeroPointCert.Basic}):
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem window_of_deriv2 (U U1 U2pp : R -> R) (t0 M : R)

    (hneg : M_hi < 0) (hM : M <= M_hi) (hEU : forall t, |t| <= t0 -> E t <= U t) (h0 : U 0 = E 0)
    (hd1 : ...) (hd2 : ...) (hK : forall u, |u| <= t0 -> U2pp u <= M) (hsym : forall t, E (-t) = E t) :

    Not (IsLocalMin E 0) /\ forall t, 0 < t -> t <= t0 -> E t < E 0
\end{lstlisting}
The hypotheses of the two \lean{yukawa*\_not\_localMin\_of\_certificate} theorems are \emph{exactly} the non-kernel-checked inputs: $E\le U$ on the window (Step~2, \tCb+\tCh), $U(0)=E(0)$ (\lean{two\_trace\_eq\_of\_eq}, B2), the two derivatives of $U$ (Step~6), $U''\le M\le M_{hi}$ (\tCb) and the mirror symmetry (\tCh). The positivity rationals \lean{yuk*\_lmin\_lo}, \lean{yuk*\_lminu\_lo}, \lean{yuk*\_qpd\_lo} are proved positive in \lean{yuk*\_cert} but are \emph{unused}: the two \lean{*\_not\_localMin\_of\_certificate} theorems consume only \lean{yuk*\_cert.1} ($M_{hi}<0$), and positivity enters only through the hypothesis \lean{hEU}. The kernel proves the implication and the signs of the rational endpoints (\lean{yuk1\_M\_hi}$=-\tfrac{1018521206953}{500000000000}$, \lean{yuk2\_M\_hi}$=-\tfrac{1868541510831}{10^{12}}$), not the physics.

\paragraph{Step 8: why this excludes a local minimum of the shape problem \tCh{} (\texttt{PZP-C-0010}).} As in B2: $\tau(t)=\rho+\sqrt3\,t+O(t^2)$, so small $t\ne0$ give unit-covolume lattices of shape $\ne\rho$ (mod $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$) arbitrarily close to $\rho$ with strictly smaller $\Ezp$. No criticality or isotropy is needed for this step (isotropy enters only in Step~4's remark and in the side result of Section~\ref{sec:lap}).

\paragraph{Headline \tier{C} (\texttt{PZP-C-0012}).} Assembling Steps 2--8: for $V_\kappa=e^{-\kappa_Yr}/r$, $\kappa_Y\in\{1,2\}$, $E(t)<E(0)$ for $0<|t|\le10^{-5}$; $0$ is not a local minimum of $E$; the triangular lattice is not a local minimum of $\Ezp$ over lattice shapes (harmonic approximation, Bravais, 2D, one path, local window).

\section{The certifier, briefly}\label{sec:engine}
Everything in this section is \tCb{} and lives in \texttt{data/code/n2\_certify.py} (sha256 below); it is not kernel-checked.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Potential and derivatives.} $V=g(q)$, $q=|R|^2$; $H$, $H'$, $H''$ along $R(t)=P_tR_0$ from $g',\dots,g''''$ (sympy, evaluated in Arb); closed-form $H'$, $H''$ against finite differences: max error $3\times10^{-8}$, $2\times10^{-7}$ \tX{} (\texttt{data/code/n2\_selfcheck.py}).
\item \textbf{Duffy coordinates.} The reduced square is split into four triangles with apex $\Gamma$, $\kappa=s\,v(t')$, and $D_t=s^2B_t$, so that $B_t$, $B'_t$, $B''_t$ are regular at $s=0$. With $Q=B_0^{1/2}$, $QY_t+Y_tQ=B_0'$ and $Q_i=(Q+uY_t)^{-1}$, the integrand is $s^2\varphi/8$ with $\varphi=2\tr(Q_iY_tQ_iY_tQ_iB_u)-2\tr(Q_iY_tQ_iB_u')+\tr(Q_iB_u'')$, the formula for $f_\kappa''(u)$ above.
\item \textbf{Second-order cell rule.} $\int_{\rm cell}g=|{\rm cell}|(g(c)+R)$ with $|R|\le\tfrac{h^2}{24}(\sup|g_{ss}|+\sup|g_{tt}|)+\tfrac{h^2}{16}\sup|g_{st}|$ (Taylor with Lagrange remainder; the linear term integrates to zero), with second-order jets in $(s,t')$ computed in forward mode. B2's first-order rule gave widths $10$--$100\times$ larger and would not close.
\item \textbf{The function $f(x)=(1-\cos x)/x^2$} (the cell phases) is enclosed by monotonicity on $[0,2\pi]$ ($f'<0$ there), with $f',f''$ by series, closed form, or global bounds $\sup|f'|\le0.13804$; a float sample check found 0 violations (\texttt{n2\_dbg2.py}).
\item \textbf{Tails.} The lattice sum is exact over $m^2+ml+l^2\le Q_{\max}$ ($650$ for $\kappa_Y=1$, $150$ for $\kappa_Y=2$). Beyond it, shells of width $\tfrac12$ with point count $\le\pi(r+\tfrac12+\rho_V)^2-\pi(r-\rho_V)^2$ (Voronoi hexagon radius $\rho_V$) and $|g^{(j)}|$ decreasing in $q$ (complete monotonicity); beyond $r_K=r_{\rm tail}+200$ an explicit decay majorant and a geometric series with computed ratio ($0.639$, $0.389$) are used. Tails enter as balls on every entry and every partial derivative (values in the \texttt{tails} field of each run file).
\item \textbf{Positivity.} On every cell and every $|u|\le t_0$: $\det B_u>0$, $\tr B_u>0$ ($\lambda_{\min}$ lower bound $\mathrm{lminu}_{lo}$) and $\det(Q+uY_t)>0$, $\tr>0$ ($\mathrm{qpd}_{lo}$): the strained lattices are dynamically stable and $X_u$ is positive definite. These three lower bounds pass through floating-point \texttt{float()} in the certifier (\texttt{n2\_certify.py}, lines 695 and 759--761), so they are rounded to nearest, not outward; with margins $\ge0.57$ this is harmless,; as noted in Step~7, no Lean theorem uses them.
\end{itemize}

\section{The obstacle for Riesz \texorpdfstring{$r^{-3}$}{1/r**3} and the dipolar potentials}\label{sec:obstacle}
Tier~\tX{} says a certificate should exist: $E''(0)=-2.439$ (Riesz 3), $-2.616$ ($d=0.1$), $-4.767$ ($d=0.5$), unchanged from real-space cutoff 40 to 100, and $U_2''(0)=E''(0)$ by Step~3. The obstruction is rigorous control of $D$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item $\hess r^{-3}\sim r^{-5}$. Near $\Gamma$, $B=D/s^2$ is the elastic sum $\sum\theta_n^2fH_n\sim\sum r^2r^{-5}$, whose real-space tail decays only like $1/R_c$ because $D$ has a non-analytic $|k|^3$ term. The shell bound that works for Yukawa, applied to $r^{-3}$ (\texttt{n2\_riesz3\_tail.py}, \texttt{PZP-X-0002}), gives for the annulus $[R_c,2R_c)$ \emph{alone}:
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lccccc}\toprule
$R_c$ & 10 & 25 & 50 & 100 & 200\\\midrule
tail bound on entries of $B$ & 818 & 291 & 140 & 68.6 & 34.0\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
i.e.\ about $6800/R_c$, against $\lambda_{\min}(B)\gtrsim(2\pi)^2\times0.39\times\tfrac14\approx3.8$ (\tX). A certificate would need $R_c\sim10^5$, about $3\times10^{10}$ lattice points per cell evaluation: infeasible. (These numbers are floating-point evaluations of a bound, \tX, so they show an obstacle, they do not prove impossibility of any method.)
\item The partial $s$-derivatives that the cell rule uses are worse: $\sum\theta^3f'H\sim\sum r^3r^{-5}$ diverges logarithmically termwise near $s=0$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Scoped remedy (not attempted, no claim).} In P2's normalisation the dipolar potential is $r^{-3}+O(d^2r^{-5})$, so one Ewald / Gaussian-mixture treatment of $r^{-3}$, $r^{-3}=\tfrac{\pi^{3/2}}{\Gamma(3/2)}\int_0^\infty a^{1/2}e^{-\pi ar^2}da$, split at $a^*$ into a short-range part $\Gamma(\tfrac32,\pi a^*r^2)/(\Gamma(\tfrac32)r^3)$ in real space and a long-range part in reciprocal space with incomplete-gamma transforms, with the remainder (Hessian $\sim r^{-7}$) in real space, would cover Riesz~3 and both dipolar cases. That needs reciprocal-space jets in $(s,t',t)$ and a cancellation-free form of $[F(G-\xi)+F(G+\xi)-2F(G)]/s^2$ for $G\ne0$. It was estimated at 2--3 hours of development and not attempted within the budget. Nothing is claimed for these three potentials, in either direction.

\section{Deviation from the preregistration: Riesz \texorpdfstring{$r^{-4}$}{1/r**4}, Tier X evidence of a local minimum}\label{sec:riesz4}
The preregistered list contains Riesz $s=4$ because P2 reports the claim ``the triangular lattice is not the zero-point minimiser'' as refuted for $s=4$. That refutation, however, is \emph{global}: it uses the finite competitor $\tau=0.3+0.954i$, not a local direction. \tX{} evidence (finite-difference $E''(0)=+2.754$ along the shear, real-space cutoff 40 and 100; and $\Ulin''(0)=+33.2$) is that $\rho$ is a local minimum of $\Ezp$ for $s=4$. Riesz~6 ($E''(0)=+27.2$, $\Ulin''=+89.2$) was therefore not run through the certifier as a negative control; Gaussian $a=2$ (an allowed control) was used instead. \section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Two of six.} Only the two Yukawa potentials are certified. Riesz~3 and the dipolar cases are blocked by the tail obstacle (Section~\ref{sec:obstacle}); for Riesz~4 there is \tX{} evidence of a local minimum, uncertified (Section~\ref{sec:riesz4}). Nothing is claimed for those four.
\item \textbf{Mostly Tier~C.} Only the logic, the window lemma and the rational arithmetic are kernel-checked. The cell bounds, Duffy map, tails and positivity are executed code, not proofs; the interchange of differentiation and integration, $E\le U_2$ after integration, the identification with P2's $\Ezp$, and the shape statement are written arguments checked by no machine and no human referee.
\item \textbf{Window and scope.} $|t|\le10^{-5}$, one path, harmonic approximation, Bravais lattices, two dimensions, $\hbar=m=1$. The theorem is local. P2's finite competitors remain \tX. ``Classically optimal'' is H2's conditional result, cited and not re-checked here (\tL). The result concerns $\Ezp$ alone. The classical energy has positive curvature at $\rho$, so nothing follows for $E_{\mathrm{cl}}+\hbar\Ezp$ at small $\hbar$, and no structural transition is claimed.
\item \textbf{AI-audited, not human-audited; not statement-locked.} An independent AI audit (\texttt{AUDIT.md}, ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS, no blocking item) was run and its corrections are applied in this version. No human has audited anything, and no statement lock has been made.
\item \textbf{Repository guards fail for pre-existing reasons.} \texttt{python antigravity\_guard.py} rc~1 and \texttt{python test\_rigor\_guard.py} rc~1 (hollow tests in \texttt{tests/test\_k3\_anse\_improvements.py}, \texttt{tests/test\_scientific\_rigor\_v14.py}; hallucinated-import reports for packages not installed in the venv, and for \texttt{flint}); \texttt{pytest tests/} rc~2 with 28 collection errors. All in files this paper does not touch; verbatim in \texttt{data/guard\_*.txt}, \texttt{data/pytest\_tests.txt}. ``Guards pass'' is not claimed.
\item \textbf{Errata of the code comments (found while writing, from \texttt{CERTIFICATES.md}).} (a) The docstring of \texttt{n2\_certify.py} writes $\int\sup_uf''$; what is computed is $\sup_u\int f''$ enclosed uniformly in $u$, and the certificate uses the latter (Step~6). (b) The docstring gives the polynomial degree of the tail factor as $\le9$; it is $\le1+4+6=11$, and the code uses 12, so results are unaffected. (c) The audit adds that the docstring of \texttt{tail\_bounds} (\texttt{n2\_certify.py}, line 455, ``1 + 4 + 4 = 9'') carries the same erratum as (b). The code is pinned by its hash and was not edited after the runs (hash below).
\item \textbf{python-flint location.} python-flint~0.6.0 is not in the project venv (installed with \texttt{pip -{}-target} under \texttt{.scratchpad/b2\_pylib}); re-running needs that version.
\item \textbf{Exploratory runs} (\texttt{explore\_*}, \texttt{o2\_g1\_N32*}, \texttt{o2\_g1\_N64.*}, \texttt{o2\_y2\_N32.log}) used earlier code versions (first-order cell rule or before the $f$-enclosure fixes) and support nothing; they are not part of this bundle except the first Yukawa~1 attempt, which is reported as a non-certification.
\item \textbf{Not novelty-checked.} No literature search was made for prior certified results on zero-point lattice energies of screened potentials; no novelty is claimed.
\end{itemize}
