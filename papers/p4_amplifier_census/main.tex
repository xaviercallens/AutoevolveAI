\begin{abstract}
For death-Birth (dB) updating on a connected, simple, unweighted graph $G$ with $N$ vertices, Allen et al.\ showed that
$\rho_G(1+\delta)=\tfrac1N+\delta\,\tfrac{\Neff-2}{2N}+O(\delta^2)$, with $\Neff$ an effective population size computed
from coalescence times of random walks, and called $G$ an \emph{amplifier of weak selection} if $\Neff>N$. Richter (2020)
exhibited an eleven-vertex example. We enumerated \emph{every} connected graph with $3\le N\le10$ (2, 6, 21, 112, 853, 11\,117,
261\,080 and 11\,716\,571 graphs) and found \textbf{none} with $\Neff>N$: every regular graph has $\Neff=N$ exactly and
every other graph has $\Neff<N$, the closest non-regular graph at $N=10$ lying at least $0.0207$ below $N$. In Lean~4 we check
that on Richter's graph \emph{every} rational solution of Allen's coalescence system has
$\Neff=2668552698098/242579025123\approx11.000756>11$. Together, \emph{within the weak-selection criterion for unweighted
graphs}, the smallest dB amplifier has exactly $11$ vertices.

\section{Setting, predecessor and preregistration}\label{sec:setting}
This is item C1 of a computational programme (Proposal~4, preregistered 2026-10-27 in \texttt{results/proposal4/2026-10-27/preregistration.json}).
It continues an earlier kernel-checked paper on the \emph{isothermal theorem} for birth-death updating~\cite{iso}: there, a
population on an isothermal graph (equal column and row sums of the replacement matrix) fixes mutants exactly as the well-mixed population does, for
every fitness $r$. For death-Birth updating the analogous statement is only first order: regular graphs tie with the complete
graph at order $\delta$, and the question becomes which graphs do \emph{better} (amplifiers) or \emph{worse} (suppressors) than a
well-mixed population when selection is weak. Allen et al.~\cite{allen} gave an exact first-order criterion; Richter~\cite{rich20,rich22}
and Svoboda et al.~\cite{svo} describe the eleven-vertex example. Svoboda et al.\ (p.~5) call it ``the smallest known unweighted dB-amplifier'';
Richter (2020, p.~6) writes that ``the smallest graph for which $N_{\mathrm{eff}}>N$ was found is the quartic graph with size $N=11$'', meaning within his
regular-input search, and Richter (2022, p.~8) reports that no transient amplifier was found for regular graphs with $N\le10$ using his method. None of these is an
exhaustive statement over all graphs. The preregistered item C1b asked: \emph{either no connected unweighted graph with $N\le10$ has $\Neff>N$, or the
exact list of those that do}. The kill rules were: if the $N=10$ census exceeds two budgets, report $N\le9$; if the criterion's hypotheses
cannot be pinned from the primary source, the census stays Tier~X. Neither fired: the $N=10$ enclosure took $1237$ s of one core (plus the candidate solves), and
the criterion was read in Allen et al.\ (arXiv v1, definitions on pp.~6 and 8; eqs.~(5)--(9) and the definitions are identical in the published version, checked by the auditor). Prior $0.45$ for a definite census; the result is a definite
(negative) census.

\section{A step-by-step account for non-specialists}\label{sec:human}
\paragraph{Step 1: the death-Birth process.}\tL{} A graph $G$ has $N$ vertices, each occupied by one individual: a \emph{resident}
(fitness $1$) or a \emph{mutant} (fitness $r=1+\delta$). In one step a vertex $i$ is chosen \emph{uniformly at random to die}; each
neighbour $j$ of $i$ then competes to fill the vacancy with probability proportional to its fitness, $f_j/\sum_{k\sim i}f_k$, and the
winner places a copy of itself on $i$. Start with one mutant at a uniformly random vertex. The process ends when the mutant type has
taken over (\emph{fixation}) or died out. The \emph{fixation probability} $\rho_G(r)$ is the chance of fixation. For the complete graph
$K_N$ it is an explicit formula; when $r=1$ every graph has $\rho=1/N$. (Lean: the process is \emph{not} formalised; only the linear
system of Step~3 is.)

\paragraph{Step 2: weak selection, and what ``amplifier'' means here.}\tL{} Write $r=1+\delta$. Allen et al.\ show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion}
\rho_G(1+\delta)=\frac1N+\delta\,\frac{\Neff-2}{2N}+O(\delta^2)\qquad(\text{their eq.~(8)}),
\end{equation}
so for $K_N$ (where $\Neff=N$) the slope is $(N-2)/2N$, which agrees with the exact formula $\tfrac{N-1}{N}\tfrac{1-1/r}{1-r^{1-N}}$
(a check we made). Hence $\rho_G(1+\delta)-\rho_{K_N}(1+\delta)=\delta\,(\Neff-N)/(2N)+O(\delta^2)$. If $\Neff>N$ then $G$ beats $K_N$ for all small
$\delta>0$ and loses for small $\delta<0$: $G$ is an \emph{amplifier of weak selection}. If $\Neff<N$ it is a \emph{suppressor}. If
$\Neff=N$ the first order decides nothing. Notice what this is not: the statement concerns $r$ close to $1$ and an unknown threshold
$b>1$ beyond which the sign may flip; it does not say $G$ amplifies for every $r>1$.

\paragraph{Step 3: coalescence times and $\Neff$.}\tL{} Follow two lineages backward in time. Each step, one of the two (chosen with
probability $\tfrac12$) jumps to a uniformly random neighbour: from $i$ it goes to $k$ with probability $p_{ik}=1/k_i$ for $k\sim i$,
where $k_i$ is the degree. Let $\tau_{ij}$ be the expected time until they meet, with $\tau_{ii}=0$. Then (Allen eq.~(5), \lean{AmplifierCensus.Eq5})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five}
\tau_{ij}=1+\tfrac12\sum_k\bigl(p_{ik}\tau_{jk}+p_{jk}\tau_{ik}\bigr)\quad(i\ne j).
\end{equation}
The \emph{remeeting time} is $\tau_i=1+\sum_jp_{ij}\tau_{ij}$ (eq.~(6), \lean{remeet}), and with $\pi_i=k_i/\sum_jk_j$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neff}
\Neff=\sum_i\pi_i\tau_i\qquad(\text{eq.~(9)},\ \lean{neff}).
\end{equation}
\emph{Worked example} (exact, \texttt{data/worked\_example.py}). Path on three vertices $0\!-\!\{1,2\}$ (a star $S_2$, centre $0$): by symmetry
$\tau_{01}=\tau_{02}$ and \eqref{eq:five} gives $\tau_{01}=1+\tfrac14\tau_{12}$, $\tau_{12}=1+\tau_{01}$, hence $\tau_{01}=\tfrac53$,
$\tau_{12}=\tfrac83$, all $\tau_i=\tfrac83$ and $\Neff=\tfrac83<3$: a suppressor (it equals $4n/(n+1)$ at $n=2$, Allen's star). The triangle $K_3$
gives all $\tau_{ij}=2$, $\tau_i=3$, $\Neff=3=N$. The graph $K_4$ minus an edge gives $\Neff=173/45\approx3.844<4$.
Two identities are used by the code (checked exactly on all 994 connected graphs with $3\le N\le7$, \texttt{AMC-C-0006}): $\sum_i\pi_i^2\tau_i=1$ (eq.~(7)), and, because $\pi_ip_{ij}=1/2m$ for $m$ edges,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
\Neff=1+\frac1m\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\tau_{ij}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 4: why regular graphs tie.}\tL{}\,\tC{} If every degree equals $k$, then $\pi_i=1/N$, and eq.~(7) reads $\tfrac1{N^2}\sum_i\tau_i=1$, so
$\Neff=\tfrac1N\sum_i\tau_i=N$ exactly. This is the first-order isothermal theorem. The census confirms it for all
$1+2+2+5+4+17+22+167$ connected regular graphs with $3\le N\le10$ and shows no non-regular graph attains equality (\texttt{AMC-C-0004}).
In Lean, the same fact on the quartic parent of Richter's graph is \lean{P11\_isothermal} (\tA, \texttt{AMC-A-0003}): $\Neff=11$ exactly.

\paragraph{Step 5: deciding $\Neff>N$ for 11.7 million graphs without trusting a float.}\tC{} Solving \eqref{eq:five} exactly for every graph with $N=10$ would cost
hours per $10^5$ graphs. Instead, a floating-point solve only \emph{proposes}; an exact integer computation \emph{decides}.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=1pt
\item Eliminate $\tau_{ii}=0$ and multiply \eqref{eq:five} by $2k_ik_j$. The unknown pair-vector $\tau$ then satisfies an integer system $A_s\tau=b$, equivalently $(I-M)\tau=\mathbf 1$ with $M\ge0$ entrywise
 (entries $\tfrac12p$) and row sums at most $1$ (mass that lands on a diagonal pair is lost: the lineages have met).
\item Because the lineages of a connected graph do meet, $M^n\to0$ and $(I-M)^{-1}=\sum_nM^n\ge0$ entrywise; and $(I-M)^{-1}\mathbf 1=\tau$ is the expected meeting-time vector.
\item Let $\tilde\tau$ be any approximate solution (here the float solution rounded to a multiple of $2^{-30}$). Its \emph{residual} $\rho=(I-M)\tilde\tau-\mathbf1$ is computed
 exactly in 64-bit integers, giving $\varepsilon=\|\rho\|_\infty\le E/2^{31}$. Then $\tau-\tilde\tau=-(I-M)^{-1}\rho$, and since $(I-M)^{-1}\ge0$,
 \[|\tau_{ij}-\tilde\tau_{ij}|\le(I-M)^{-1}|\rho|\le\varepsilon\,(I-M)^{-1}\mathbf1=\varepsilon\,\tau_{\max}\ ,\qquad\tau_{\max}\le\frac{\tilde\tau_{\max}}{1-\varepsilon}.\]
 Non-negativity of the inverse is the whole point: it turns a residual into a \emph{componentwise} error bound without computing any norm of the inverse.
\item By \eqref{eq:edge}, $|\Neff-\hat N_{\mathrm{eff}}|\le\varepsilon\tau_{\max}$. A graph is \emph{excluded} (certified $\Neff<N$) only if $\hat N_{\mathrm{eff}}+\varepsilon\tau_{\max}<N$, tested in exact integers.
\item Every graph that is not excluded is solved exactly in rational arithmetic. In the census these were precisely the regular graphs (22 at $N=9$, 167 at $N=10$).
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Precondition.} The residual is formed in 64-bit integers, so the argument needs $\tilde\tau$ finite and $|t|=|\mathrm{rint}(2^{30}\tilde\tau)|$ small enough that no int64 overflow occurs (the
code now asserts \texttt{isfinite} and $\max|t|<2^{50}$; for realistic data $\tau\lesssim10^2$ gives $|t|\le2^{37}$ and row sums of $|A_st|\lesssim2^{50}$). A poor but finite proposal only enlarges $E$ and blocks an exclusion; it cannot cause a wrong one. The assert was added after the census run (\texttt{REVISION.md}); the independent audit recheck covers the decisions.
The largest $\varepsilon$ seen was $5.9\times10^{-8}$ (at $N=10$), while the smallest certified margin $N-\Neff$ among non-regular graphs was at least $0.0173$ ($N=9$; exactly $13/748$) and $0.0207$ ($N=10$; exactly $13/627$): the
error budget is about six orders of magnitude below the decision margin. This argument is a hand proof implemented in Python; it is \tC{}, not kernel-checked.

\paragraph{Step 6: the Lean statement beside the data.}\tA{} Lean cannot reasonably enumerate $10^7$ graphs, but it can check one graph exactly. Let \lean{Eq5 A tau} be \eqref{eq:five} over $\mathbb Q$ for the adjacency $A$ on \lean{Fin n}.
\lean{eq5\_unique} says: \emph{if $v$ solves \lean{Eq5} and $v_{ij}>0$ for $i\ne j$, then every rational solution $\tau$ equals $v$}
(a maximum principle: the largest ratio $\delta_{ij}/v_{ij}$ of the difference $\delta=\tau-v$ would otherwise satisfy a strict inequality). So a single rational vector, produced by untrusted code and
re-checked by the kernel with \lean{decide +kernel}, certifies $\Neff$ for \emph{all} solutions of the system, not for a guessed one. \lean{R11\_amplifier} then states
\[\forall\tau,\ \text{Eq5}\ G_{R11}\ \tau\Rightarrow\Neff(G_{R11},\tau)=\tfrac{2668552698098}{242579025123}\ \wedge\ 11<\Neff(G_{R11},\tau).\]
The kernel also checks that the adjacency is symmetric, loopless, and has degrees $3$ at vertices $0,9$ and $4$ elsewhere. What Lean does \emph{not} check: that this linear system is the right one for the death-Birth
process, that eq.~\eqref{eq:expansion} holds, or that the census is complete.

\paragraph{Step 7: from $\Neff>N$ to finite $r$.}\tL{}\,\tC{} By \eqref{eq:expansion} the step from $\Neff>N$ to ``$\rho_G>\rho_{K_N}$ for $r\in(1,b)$'' is literature (\texttt{AMC-L-0002}). To check that this is not an artefact of our reading of the criterion, we
solved the exact $2^N-2$-state chain for every connected graph with $N\le6$ (141 graphs) and 22 graphs (every 40th) at $N=7$ ($163$ graphs in all) and found slope $(\Neff-2)/2N$ in every case (\texttt{AMC-C-0006}). For the single graph with $N=11$ we also
certified signs of $\rho_G-\rho_{K_{11}}$ at chosen $r$ with the same device as Step~5: the transient-state matrix $Q$ of the 2046-state chain has $(I-Q)^{-1}\ge0$ with row sums equal to expected absorption times $T$, so an exact residual bound gives $|x-\tilde x|\le\|\rho_x\|_\infty T_{\max}$ with $T_{\max}\le242.43$ (\texttt{AMC-C-0011}).

\section{Controls}\label{sec:controls}
Every control was run before any census count was reported (\texttt{data/controls.json}, \texttt{data/logs/controls.log}; \texttt{AMC-C-0006..0009}, \texttt{AMC-A-0003/0004}).
\paragraph{Positive controls (all met).}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=0pt
\item $K_N$ and $C_N$, $N=3..10$: $\Neff=N$ exactly (Allen's weak-selection isothermal theorem).
\item All regular graphs have $\Neff=N$ exactly; their counts $1,2,2,5,4,17,22,167$ equal OEIS A005177, so none was missing.
\item Star $S_n$, $n=2..9$: $\Neff=4n/(n+1)$ exactly.
\item Slope control: $d\rho/dr|_{r=1}$ of the exact $2^N$-state chain equals $(\Neff-2)/(2N)$ on all $141$ connected graphs with $N=3..6$ and on $22$ graphs (every $40$th) at $N=7$, $163$ in total.
\item Closed forms against the exact chain at $r\in\{\tfrac34,\tfrac54,\tfrac32,\tfrac74,\tfrac{1001}{1000}\}$, $N=3..7$: dB and Bd on $K_N$; the dB cycle as given by Kaveh et al.\ eq.~(20) at $d=1$, Hindersin--Traulsen SI eq.~(18) and Richter eq.~(12), namely $2(r-1)/\bigl(3r-1+(r-3)r^{2-N}\bigr)$; the dB star (Allen eq.~(2)).
\item Hindersin \& Traulsen, $N=4$: their $C_4$ dB eqs.~(13), (14) reproduced exactly; the four degree-heterogeneous graphs are Bd amplifiers and dB suppressors; $C_4$ is the only dB-``unclassified'' graph; $K_4$ equals the well-mixed population. Stars $S_4..S_6$ are Bd amplifiers and dB suppressors on the $r$ grid.
\item Richter: among the $265$ quartic graphs on $11$ vertices exactly one parent is an amplifier constructor, all four removals at its argmax vertex give $\Neff=11.000756$ (printed $11.0008$); among the $85$ cubic graphs on $12$ vertices exactly one constructor, two removals give $12.026644$ (printed $12.0266$; the third removal disconnects).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Negative controls (all rejected as intended).}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=0pt
\item \textbf{Allen eq.~(3) as printed in arXiv v1.} The cycle formula with $r^{-(N-1)}$ \emph{disagrees with the exact chain in 25 of 25 cases}; three other sources and the exact chain agree with $r^{2-N}(r-3)$. arXiv v1 misprints the exponent; the published version (PLoS Comput.\ Biol.\ 2020, Eq.~(3)) has $r^{-(N-3)}$ and agrees with the exact chain (the auditor confirmed this on $C_4,C_5,C_6$ at $r=5/4,3/4$, and that the published form agrees while the v1 form does not). So this is not an open discrepancy; the criterion used here (eqs.~(5)--(9)) does not depend on eq.~(3) (\texttt{AMC-C-0007}).
\item A planted wrong random walk, $p_{ij}=1/k_j$ in place of $1/k_i$, breaks the star value $10/3$.
\item $K_N$ never passes the strict test $\Neff>N$.
\item The Antal approximation $N\mu_1^2/\mu_2$ is $\le N$ on every connected graph with $N\le7$, so it can never detect an amplifier; it ``fails'' the Richter control by construction.
\item Edges removed away from the argmax vertex: $\Neff\le10.8805<11$ on Richter's parent, $\le11.9337<12$ on the cubic parent.
\item Lean (kernel decides the right way, \texttt{AMC-A-0004}): \lean{ctl\_perturbed\_vector\_rejected} (one $\tau$ entry changed by $1/D$ gives $\neg$Eq5), \lean{ctl\_perturbed\_graph\_rejected} (the R11 certificate is $\neg$Eq5 on the parent: a wrong transition matrix is caught), \lean{ctl\_parent\_not\_amplifier}.
\item Lean (caught by the axiom audit): the planted \lean{ctl\_sorry} reports \lean{sorryAx} and \lean{ctl\_axiom} reports \lean{AmplifierCensusControls.ctl\_cheat}, although \texttt{Controls.lean} compiles with rc~0 (a compile exit code alone accepts both).

The decisive evidence is the kernel-checked, replayed \lean{ctl\_perturbed\_vector\_rejected}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Novelty of the exhaustive statement is established only to our knowledge (a literature search, not a proof of absence).} We read Allen et al.\ (arXiv 1906.01036v1), Richter (2008.01446v1; 2204.12861v1, in pages), Tkadlec et al.\ (1906.02785v1), Hindersin \& Traulsen (1504.03832v2), Kaveh et al.\ (1411.4556v1), and, for novelty only, Svoboda et al.\ (2401.14914v1) and Brewster et al.\ (2605.26411v3, Bd only), plus OEIS A001349 and A005177. Richter~\cite{rich22} (p.~8) reports only that his iterative \emph{regular-input} procedure found none with $N\le10$; Svoboda et al.\ (p.~5) call Richter's graph ``the smallest known''. The independent auditor identified Richter's ref.~[6] as Allen et al.~\cite{allen21} (PLoS Comput.\ Biol.\ 17:e1008695, 2021), whose exhaustive analysis of all connected graphs up to size $10$ is for \emph{Birth-death} updating only (read via the article page, not in full); the related Alcalde Cuesta et al.~\cite{alc17} database of all graphs of order $\le10$ and its companion ``Suppressors of selection'' (PLoS ONE 12:e0180549, 2017) are likewise Birth-death only (the chapter could not be opened). To our knowledge there is no prior exhaustive $N\le10$ death-Birth weak-selection statement: the auditor's alphaXiv searches and its reading of ten papers (Allen 2019/2020 and 2021, Richter 2020 and 2022, Tkadlec 2019, Hindersin--Traulsen, Kaveh et al., Svoboda et al., Brewster et al.\ 2026 and arXiv:2512.11164) found none. Hindersin--Traulsen classified random graphs on an $r$ grid, which is not an exhaustive weak-selection census. This is a search result, not a proof of absence, and nothing here should be read as a priority claim.
\item \textbf{The census is Tier~C, not Lean.} Completeness depends on nauty \texttt{geng} (2.7r3, Debian, Apache-2.0) and on the match with OEIS A001349; the enclosure argument is a hand proof implemented in Python; the Lean theorems cover only the $N=11$ linear system.
\item \textbf{Scope of the statement.} Unweighted, undirected, simple, connected graphs; death-Birth updating; uniform initialisation (one mutant at a uniformly random vertex); the weak-selection criterion $\Neff>N$ at first order in $\delta$. Temperature or mutation-at-birth initialisation can change the classification (Hindersin \& Traulsen, Richter 2022) and nothing transfers. Weighted transient amplifiers (Allen's Fans, Separated Hubs) are out of scope. A weak-selection amplifier is not an amplifier at finite $r$ and not a universal amplifier (Tkadlec et al., Thm.~1, as recorded in the run note, forbids that for non-complete graphs).
\item \textbf{Finite-$r$ checks are not proofs for all $r$ near 1.} The four signs, the bisection and the 212 regular graphs are certified only at the listed $r$; uniqueness of the crossing is not shown; whether regular graphs are reducers for all $r$ near $1$ remains open.
\item \textbf{No human audit; no statement lock.} An independent AI audit agent (ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS; census rechecked by a different exact method for $N=3..9$ in full and a 20\,177-graph sample at $N=10$, Lean rebuilt and replayed, finite-$r$ signs reproduced) has reviewed this paper, but no person has audited the Lean statements or the hand arguments, and no statement lock was registered for the new module. The $N=10$ recheck is a sample, not a full independent census.
\item \textbf{Repository guards were not run} (\texttt{antigravity\_guard.py}, \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py}, \texttt{pytest tests/}); no guard output is claimed.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration.} (1) The $N=9,10$ census is a certified enclosure plus exact solves of the candidates, not an exact rational solve of every graph. (2) The preregistration expected a Tier~A certificate per listed amplifier; there is none at $N\le10$, so Tier~A went to Richter's graph and its parent. (3) Finite-$r$ certificates use Python \texttt{Fraction}s, not python-flint (not installed), so they are Tier~C, as preregistered for that route. (4) C1a (the $K_N$ and cycle closed forms) was checked as exact rational comparisons with the exact chain, not as Lean theorems. (5) C1x (birth/death-rate planes on tissue-like graphs) was \textbf{not attempted}. (6) The Lean gate used \texttt{lean\_pinned.py}. (7) CPU accounting: at most two busy processes, but idle wrapper processes at times made three; some shell-guard lapses occurred (wait loops, one empty heredoc, one stray variable); none touched git.
\item \textbf{Open discrepancies with Richter.} (i) Richter's printed $\tau_{\max}$ values (Section~\ref{sec:results}) are unexplained; the auditor ruled out the perturbed-graph hypothesis. (ii) Richter's $r_{\max}\approx1.00075$ is inconsistent with our certified signs at $1.0007$--$1.0008125$ (bracket $[1.00082333,1.00082349]$); not investigated. Neither has been raised with the author. Allen eq.~(3) is not open: arXiv v1 misprints it and the published version is correct.
\item \textbf{Data note.} The run note quotes the cubic $\tau_{\max}$ as $18.29406$; the data file gives $18.29410$, used here.
\end{itemize}
