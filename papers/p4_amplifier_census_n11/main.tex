\begin{abstract}
For death-Birth (dB) updating on a connected, simple, unweighted graph $G$ with $N$ vertices, Allen et al.\ showed that the fixation probability
of a single mutant of fitness $1+\delta$ is $\tfrac1N+\delta\,\tfrac{\Neff-2}{2N}+O(\delta^2)$, and called $G$ an \emph{amplifier of weak selection} if $\Neff>N$.
An earlier preprint of ours found by exhaustive enumeration that no connected graph with $N\le10$ is one~\cite{c1}. Here we enumerate \emph{all}
$1\,006\,700\,565$ connected graphs on $N=11$ vertices with nauty \texttt{geng} (the count equals OEIS A001349(11)) and decide each by an exact rule.
\textbf{Exactly four graphs have $\Neff>11$}, with exact rational $\Neff\approx11.095159,\ 11.012785,\ 11.005561$ and $11.000756$; \textbf{539 graphs tie} at $\Neff=11$, all regular, and $539$ equals OEIS A005177(11), so they are all the connected regular graphs on $11$ vertices;
the remaining $1\,006\,700\,022$ graphs have $\Neff<11$, each certified.
\textbf{The four graphs are not new.} They are, by printed $\Neff$, mean degree, minimum degree and normalized-Laplacian $\lambda_2$ (we did not compare the drawings), the four $N=11$ graphs shown by
Richter in arXiv:2204.12861v1, p.~8, Fig.~1. What is new, to our knowledge, is that \emph{they are all of them} at $N=11$: no other connected unweighted graph on $11$ vertices amplifies weak selection under dB.
Our previous preprint presented Richter's graph as the $N=11$ example; the corresponding corrections are listed in \texttt{ERRATUM\_C1.md}.

\textbf{Tier split.}
\tA{} (Lean~4.34.1, axioms within $\{$\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}$\}$): for three of the four graphs, \lean{A1\_amplifier}, \lean{A2\_amplifier}, \lean{A3\_amplifier}: every rational solution of Allen's coalescence system has the stated exact $\Neff>11$ (the fourth graph is Richter's, certified in \lean{R11\_amplifier} of the earlier preprint).
\tL{} (literature, not re-proved): the criterion $\Neff>N$ and the step from $\Neff>N$ to $\rho_G>\rho_{K_N}$ for $r$ slightly above~$1$.
\tC{}: the census count (enumeration plus a certified exact-integer decision rule, run with a C port validated against the Python code of the earlier preprint) and exact-rational sign certificates of $\rho_G-\rho_{K_{11}}$ at three values of $r$ for each of the three new graphs. The census is \emph{not} kernel-checked.
Every claim carries a ledger identifier (\texttt{AMN-$\cdot$-nnnn}).

\section{Setting and question}\label{sec:setting}
The model, the coalescence-time system and the effective size $\Neff$ are those of Allen et al.~\cite{allen}; a step-by-step account for non-specialists (Steps~1--7) is in Section~2 of our earlier preprint~\cite{c1} and is not repeated.
In short: $\tau_{ij}$ solves $\tau_{ij}=1+\tfrac12\sum_k(p_{ik}\tau_{jk}+p_{jk}\tau_{ik})$ for $i\ne j$ with $\tau_{ii}=0$ and $p_{ik}=1/k_i$ on edges (eq.~(5)), $\tau_i=1+\sum_jp_{ij}\tau_{ij}$ (eq.~(6)), and $\Neff=\sum_i\pi_i\tau_i$ with $\pi_i=k_i/2m$ (eq.~(9)); $\rho_G(1+\delta)-\rho_{K_N}(1+\delta)=\delta(\Neff-N)/(2N)+O(\delta^2)$ (eq.~(8)); regular graphs tie ($\Neff=N$).

The earlier preprint~\cite{c1} showed that no connected graph with $N\le10$ has $\Neff>N$, and checked in Lean that Richter's eleven-vertex graph does. It did not ask whether that graph is the only one at $N=11$. The preregistered question of this item (C1b, addendum~2 to Proposal~4, \texttt{docs/proposals/PROPOSAL\_4\_ADDENDUM\_2\_C1B\_2026-10-27.md}, sha256 \texttt{a5e5909d\dots}, equal to the value recorded in the run files; the file was not edited) was: among the $1\,006\,700\,565$ connected graphs on $11$ vertices, (i) exactly how many have $\Neff>N$, and (ii) is Richter's graph the unique one up to isomorphism, and what are the others, if any? The recorded priors were $0.5$ that the census completes within budget, $0.25$ that it finds a graph other than Richter's, and $0.6$ that Richter's graph is the only one; the first two held (three graphs other than Richter's were found), the third did not. The kill criterion (stop if the measured throughput projects to more than $24$~h on two cores) did not fire.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Enumeration.}\tC{} \texttt{nauty-geng -c 11 res/256}, $\mathrm{res}=0..255$, nauty 2.7r3, one part per run; each part was piped straight into the checker, so no graph6 data are stored. geng was run without \texttt{-q}, so its line ``\texttt{>Z k graphs generated}'' is in each part log (\texttt{data/logs/parts/}); the driver checks that $k$ equals the number of graphs decided and that no input line was malformed. The $256$ parts sum to $1\,006\,700\,565$, equal to OEIS A001349(11) (\texttt{AMN-C-0001}). geng emits one graph per isomorphism class.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}\tC{} The rule is that of the earlier preprint~\cite{c1}: floating point only proposes, exact integer arithmetic decides. Multiply eq.~(5) by $2k_ik_j$ to obtain an integer system $(I-M)\tau=\mathbf 1$ with $M\ge0$ and $(I-M)^{-1}\ge0$. A float64 solution $x$ is rounded to $t=\mathrm{rint}(2^{30}x)$ (and only accepted if it is finite and $|x|2^{30}<2^{50}$; otherwise the graph is a candidate); its residual $E$ is formed exactly in 128-bit integers, $\varepsilon=E/2^{31}$; since the inverse is non-negative, $|\Neff-\hat N_{\mathrm{eff}}|\le\varepsilon\,\tau_{\max}\le\varepsilon\tilde\tau_{\max}/(1-\varepsilon)$ by the edge identity $\Neff=1+\frac1m\sum_{ij\in E}\tau_{ij}$. A graph is certified \emph{excluded} ($\Neff<11$) only if the resulting upper bound is below $11$ in exact integers and the graph is non-regular. Every graph not excluded (the $539$ regular graphs and the $4$ amplifiers, $543$ in all) is solved exactly with Python \texttt{Fraction}s using C1's \texttt{dbcore.neff\_exact}; the four amplifiers were solved a second time by C1's \texttt{exact\_census.py}, a pure-\texttt{Fraction} Gauss--Jordan on eqs.~(5), (6), (9) that does not use the edge identity, giving the same four graphs and the same maximum. A bad float proposal can only block an exclusion; it cannot produce a wrong one. Over the whole census the guards never fired (\texttt{guard\_fail}$\,=0$, \texttt{chol\_fail}$\,=0$) and the largest $\varepsilon$ was $7.45\times10^{-8}$. The smallest certified gap $11-\Neff$ over all excluded graphs is $0.0020643$; no excluded graph had a certified upper bound within $10^{-3}$ of $11$ (\texttt{AMN-C-0002}, \texttt{-0006}). The correctness of the bound is the hand proof of C1 (audited there); here it is again \tC{}.

\paragraph{A C port, validated before use, and a bug it had.}\tC{} The Python implementation of C1 projected $21.4$~h on two cores for $N=11$ (measured on a $10^7$-graph sample: $6\,527$ graphs/s per core). We therefore wrote \texttt{census\_fast.c}, a C port of the same rule (Cholesky instead of LAPACK LU for the proposal, which is allowed since the scaled matrix is symmetric positive definite and correctness does not depend on the proposer; the inequality is C1's multiplied by $2$): $21\,925$ graphs/s per core, projected $6.4$~h; the actual run took $5$~h $10$~min of wall time on two workers (CPU sum $35\,950$~s). It was validated before any $N=11$ result was used (\texttt{AMN-C-0003}):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=0pt
\item At $N=3..10$, on C1's own geng files (sha256 equal to C1's recorded hashes), the C code gives identical graph counts, identical excluded counts and identical candidate sets as C1's outputs. Per graph, against the guarded Python functions: all graphs at $N=3..9$ and a $10^6$-graph stride sample at $N=10$ (every $11$th line): same graph order, $0$ verdict mismatches, the two certified intervals intersect on every graph, $\max|\Delta\hat N_{\mathrm{eff}}|=1.1\times10^{-10}$.
\item After the exact finish, $N=3..10$ reproduce C1: $0$ amplifiers; ties $1,2,2,5,4,17,22,167$ (all regular, equal to A005177); counts equal A001349.
\item On a $10^7$-graph $N=11$ sample the Python and C codes agree on every count ($9\,999\,998$ excluded) and on the candidate set (two regular graphs), and name the same nearest non-amplifier.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{A bug was found and fixed before this validation.} graph6 uses the characters with codes $63..126$, which include the backslash; the first build of the C code did not escape it when writing JSON. The fix touches output formatting only. The $10^7$-graph throughput run used that first build; its output happened to parse and its decisions are unaffected. Validation, controls and the census used the fixed build (sha256 \texttt{6f658a78\dots}, \texttt{data/result.json}). The C implementation was validated against C1 but has not been independently audited.

\paragraph{Isomorphism.}\tC{} Canonical forms from \texttt{nauty-labelg}, cross-checked by networkx \texttt{is\_isomorphic}: the four amplifiers have four distinct canonical forms and are pairwise non-isomorphic; only \texttt{JCQRRQodIU?} is isomorphic to Richter's \texttt{J?ouPjI[@i?}.

\paragraph{Lean.}\tA{} As for \lean{R11\_amplifier} in C1: \lean{eq5\_unique} (a positive rational solution of eq.~(5) is the only solution) and \lean{neff\_of\_cert} from \texttt{Core.lean}, with integer certificate data \texttt{DataA1/A2/A3.lean} proposed by the untrusted \texttt{gen\_lean.py} and re-checked by the kernel with \lean{decide +kernel}; \lean{Amplifiers11.lean} holds the three theorems and the symmetry, looplessness and degree lemmas. See Section~\ref{sec:lean}.

The earlier preprint cited Svoboda et al.~\cite{svo} calling Richter's graph ``the smallest known'' amplifier; the present result does not contradict that wording but shows that the picture at $N=11$ is four graphs, not one.

\section{Observations on Richter's Fig.~1}\label{sec:rich}
\tC{} Three inconsistencies, recorded neutrally as observations, within the figure, caption and text of~\cite{rich22} as read in arXiv:2204.12861v1 and in the J.\ Math.\ Biol.\ version (unchanged there) (\texttt{AMN-C-0010}, \texttt{AMN-L-0003}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=0pt
\item \emph{Maximum degree.} The caption and the text of p.~8 (``All 4 graphs have a maximum degree $\Delta(G)=4$'') state that all four graphs have maximum degree $4$. Our A1 (his (d), $\Neff=11.0952$) has maximum degree $3$. It is A3 with the edge between A3's two degree-$4$ vertices removed (the two graph6 strings differ in one bit), which fits his own remark that (d) is (c) minus the red edge. Removing that edge lowers both endpoints from degree $4$ to degree $3$, leaving maximum degree $3$.
\item \emph{Which is highest.} The text (p.~8, again p.~10 and in the caption of Fig.~2(b)) says that (b), with $11.0128$, has the highest $\Neff$, whereas (d) has $11.0952$ in Fig.~1.
\item \emph{Mean degree.} On p.~10 the mean degree of (b) and (d) is printed as $2.0991$; the caption of Fig.~1 has $2.9091=32/11$.
\end{enumerate}
None affects the census or the identification by $\Neff$, edge count, minimum degree and $\lambda_2$. None has been raised with the author; they may be typographical. (The two further open discrepancies with Richter recorded in the earlier preprint, his printed $\tau_{\max}$ values and his $r_{\max}\approx1.00075$, are not examined here.)

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{AI audit only; statement lock and human audit owed.} A different AI agent (AUDIT.md, ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS; not a person) recompiled the Lean modules through a clone of the \texttt{lean\_pinned.py} compile path (not \texttt{lean\_pinned.py} itself), replayed the kernel on them, re-derived the decision rule, re-censused $N\le10$ and about $7.3$ million $N=11$ graphs with its own code and reran the C port on two parts, with identical verdicts. No statement lock exists, and no human has audited the Lean statements or the hand argument. A statement lock and a human audit are owed before any stronger wording.
\item \textbf{The census is Tier~C, not Lean.} Completeness rests on nauty \texttt{geng} (2.7r3), the exact A001349(11) count, the hand proof of the enclosure bound (audited in C1, not in Lean) and the C implementation, validated against C1, reviewed and rerun on two parts by the AI auditor, but not given a line-by-line independent audit. The Lean theorems cover only the linear system on four explicit adjacencies and say nothing about ``there are no others''.
\item \textbf{Novelty, to our knowledge.} ``The first exhaustive statement that these four are all of them at $N=11$'' is the result of a literature search made for the earlier preprint (Allen et al., Richter 2020 and 2022, Tkadlec et al., Hindersin--Traulsen, Kaveh et al., Svoboda et al., Brewster et al., the exhaustive Birth-death analyses of Allen et al.~2021 and Alcalde Cuesta et al.\ for $N\le10$ which are not dB) and of the reading of Richter~2022 and Allen et al.\ in the C1b session; it is a search result, not a proof of absence, and nothing here is a priority claim. Richter's $N=11$ graphs are the output of his Algorithm~1 (an approximative greedy search with a filter, cospectrality used as a proxy for isomorphism) started from all regular graphs with $k=4,6,8$; only for the $265$ quartic inputs does he report a complete enumeration of the edge-removal search (pp.~5, 8--9). He makes no statement about all connected graphs on $11$ vertices. An additional literature search by the auditor (alphaXiv, web) found no exhaustive $N=11$ death-Birth enumeration.
\item \textbf{The four graphs are not new}, and the identification with Richter's Fig.~1 is by printed $\Neff$, edge count, minimum degree and $\lambda_2$, not by the drawings.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Unweighted, undirected, simple, connected graphs; death-Birth updating; uniform initialisation; the first-order criterion $\Neff>N$. Temperature or mutation-at-birth initialisation can change the classification; weighted transient amplifiers are out of scope. A weak-selection amplifier is not an amplifier at finite~$r$.
\item \textbf{Finite-$r$ statements} hold only at the three listed $r$ for A1--A3 (Python \texttt{Fraction}s, Tier~C); no $r_{\max}$ is computed.
\item \textbf{Repository guards were not run} (\texttt{antigravity\_guard.py}, \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py}, \texttt{pytest tests/}); no guard output is claimed.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration and the brief.} (1) A C port of the decision rule was used instead of the Python code, with the Cholesky proposer; validated as above. (2) The first C build had the backslash-escape bug (fixed, decisions unaffected). (3) The throughput sample is the first $10^7$ graphs of geng parts $0$ and $1$ of $100$, not a random sample. (4) During the throughput measurement an unrelated process shared a core, so that timing includes contention. (5) The producer's Lean gate used \texttt{lean\_pinned.py}; the producer $\ne$ verifier recompile was done by the AI auditor (through a clone of that compile path); no statement lock. (6) Process accounting: at most two CPU-busy processes except idle wrappers; a few shell-guard lapses occurred (chained commands, two \texttt{sed -i} edits, one read-only git status); nothing was committed, staged or pushed by the run.
\item \textbf{Open discrepancies with Richter} listed in Section~\ref{sec:rich}; not raised with the author.
\item Not a medical or biological claim of any kind.
\end{itemize}
