\begin{abstract}
An unstressed crystal is elastically stable only if its $6\times6$ stiffness matrix $C$ is positive definite (the Born criterion, as stated by Mouhat and Coudert~\cite{mc}). We tested every full $6\times6$ elastic tensor stored in the
JARVIS-DFT \texttt{dft\_3d} dataset~\cite{jarvis} (CC BY 4.0; figshare article 6815699; unzipped json sha256 \texttt{9dacb55a\dots5907}) with exact rational arithmetic. Of $93\,902$ records, $59\,400$ carry no tensor (\texttt{"na"}), $179$ contain a non-finite number, and
$N=34\,323$ are analysed. Reading each stored float as the exact rational of its shortest decimal, and testing the symmetric part $S=(C+C^T)/2$: $22\,842$ tensors are positive definite and
\textbf{$11\,481$ ($33.45\%$) are not}, each with an exact rational witness $x\ne0$, $x^TSx\le0$. Three exact methods agree on every entry (the producer's $LDL^T$ and Bareiss elimination, and an independent auditor's characteristic-polynomial sign pattern), and the auditor re-checked all $11\,481$ witnesses. $234$ tensors are numerically zero; without them $11\,247$ of $34\,089$ ($32.99\%$) are not positive definite.
Under an exact worst-case rounding test (each printed value within half a unit of its last printed place) $22\,809$ verdicts are robustly positive definite, $11\,244$ robustly not, $55$ certified ambiguous and $215$ undecided ($210$ of them null tensors). $342$ tensors pass the incorrect linear orthorhombic criterion but are not positive definite.
\textbf{This is a statement about stored DFT tensors under one stated reading.} Many entries are raw DFT outputs, including hypothetical, unrelaxed and dynamically unstable structures, so a large fraction is expected. It does \textbf{not} say that JARVIS-DFT is wrong and says nothing about any material being unstable in a laboratory. The kernel-checked Lean part (Tier~\tA{}, provisional: a statement-lock entry and the \texttt{lean\_runner.py} gate are still owed) covers $18$ explicit matrices and $2$ control statements only.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small\textbf{What this is NOT.} Not a claim that any material is stable, unstable or synthesisable; not a claim that JARVIS-DFT or its authors are wrong; not a formalisation of the classification (Lean covers 18 matrices and 2 controls); not a statement under applied load (the zero-stress criterion is tested). The owner has not forwarded the dataset observations of Section~\ref{sec:obs} to the dataset authors.}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and question}\label{sec:setting}
\paragraph{Criterion (Tier \tL{}).} Mouhat and Coudert~\cite{mc} state that an unstressed crystal is elastically stable iff the quadratic form $E=\tfrac12V_0\sum C_{ij}\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j$ is positive for all $\varepsilon\ne0$, i.e.\ iff the $6\times6$ matrix $C$ is positive definite (all eigenvalues $>0$, equivalently all leading principal minors $>0$). They cite Born and Huang~\cite{bh} for this; we did not read that book. Under load the relevant matrix is $B\ne C$ (their Eq.~20), so what is tested here is the \emph{zero-stress} criterion (correction C4). Their Eq.~19 is the linear orthorhombic criterion of Wu et al., which they call incorrect, with the counterexample of their footnote~26. Only the symmetric part $S=(C+C^T)/2$ enters $x^TCx$, so verdicts are for $S$; the number of non-symmetric stored tensors is reported separately.

\paragraph{Data and licence (Tier \tL{}).} JARVIS-DFT \texttt{dft\_3d}~\cite{jarvis,jarvis_elastic}, figshare article 6815699 (DOI \texttt{10.6084/m9.figshare.6815699}; the file used is \texttt{jdft\_3d-9-24-2025.json.zip}, figshare version~11). The unzipped json has sha256 \texttt{9dacb55ac8371c7c2bf0332933b55c98e9cca6201e92eb9d6fcd03aab0975907}; the zip's md5 \texttt{c3179161\dots} equals the figshare API's \texttt{computed\_md5}. The licence is \textbf{CC BY 4.0}, read from the figshare API by the producer before download and again by the auditor; attribution: Choudhary et al., npj Comput.\ Mater.\ 6, 173 (2020). The dataset is not redistributed with this paper (no bulk data, no per-entry table; per-entry results are kept outside the repository, with their sha256 in \texttt{data/result.json}). Exactly $22$ dataset tensors are shipped: $18$ in \texttt{Witnesses.lean} and $4$ in \texttt{data/summaries/f1\_inspect.json}, each file carrying the CC BY 4.0 attribution line (correction C9). Stored units are GPa (the auditor matched five published values of Table~S3 of~\cite{jarvis_elastic} to the printed digits).

\paragraph{Questions (preregistered).} \texttt{data/prereg\_M2.json} (sha256 \texttt{2c3127f9\dots5d0fb}) asked: (Q1) how many tensors are certifiably not positive definite when the stored decimals are read as exact rationals; (Q2) how many verdicts are ambiguous under half-unit rounding; (Q3) how many pass the incorrect linear criterion but fail exact positive definiteness; (Q4) where float pipelines disagree with the exact verdict; (Q5) where the class-specific closed-form conditions of~\cite{mc} disagree with the exact verdict. The outcome rule was KEEP if all controls pass, the whole dataset is classified, two implementations agree and the certified not-positive-definite fraction is at least $1\%$. Outcome: KEEP.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Reading.} Each stored float is converted to the exact rational of its shortest round-trip decimal representation. This is an interpretation: the stored decimals are treated as exact numbers. A DFT tensor is a calculation, not ``the material's'' tensor. The auditor checked that this equals reading the literal json text for every finite entry ($1\,235\,628$ entries, $0$ differences) and that no entry is a json integer literal.

\paragraph{Exact test.}\tC{} $LDL^T$ without pivoting over $\Q$ on $S$: $S$ is positive definite iff all pivots are $>0$ (Sylvester). If the first non-positive pivot is $d_k\le0$, solving $L^Tx=e_k$ gives an exact rational witness with $x^TSx=d_k\le0$ (a ``boundary'' case when $d_k=0$). The producer ran this ($LDL^T$), an integer Bareiss elimination on all entries, and sympy leading minors on a $14\,615$-entry subset (all not-positive-definite entries, all rounding-undecided entries and $3\,000$ random positive definite ones; a logged deviation from ``all''). The auditor wrote separate code deciding positive definiteness from strict sign alternation of the exact characteristic polynomial over $\Q$ (an ``iff'' because $S$ is symmetric, hence real-rooted) on all $34\,323$ entries.

\paragraph{Ordering.} JARVIS stores the VASP order $(xx,yy,zz,xy,yz,zx)$. Positive definiteness is invariant under simultaneous permutation and positive diagonal congruence, so the order cannot change a full-tensor verdict; it matters only for the class conditions, where the producer maps to Voigt by $[0,1,2,4,5,3]$. The auditor confirmed the mapping (tetragonal-I entries: stored index 4 equals index 5 exactly in $9\,596$ of $10\,343$, against index 3 equals index 4 in $35$; the identity map would give $1\,046$ instead of $555$ class disagreements).

\paragraph{Rounding robustness.}\tC{} Each printed value is allowed $\pm\tfrac12$ unit of its last printed place (half-widths $\Delta_{ij}$, symmetrised). The producer used sufficient tests (a Frobenius-norm bound for ``robustly positive definite'', $x^TSx+|x|^T\Delta|x|<0$ for ``robustly not''). The auditor replaced the first by the exact Rohn vertex test: every symmetric $S+E$ with $|E_{ij}|\le\Delta_{ij}$ is positive definite iff all $32$ matrices $S-T_z\Delta T_z$, $z\in\{\pm1\}^6$, $z_1=1$, are (here $T_z=\operatorname{diag}(z)$). A not-positive-definite entry is ``certified ambiguous'' when some $S+T_z\Delta T_z$ or $S+\operatorname{diag}\Delta$ is positive definite; ``undecided'' means no certificate was found.

\paragraph{Float pipelines (Tier \tX).} \texttt{numpy.linalg.eigvalsh} on $S$ with three conventions: F0 $\lambda_{\min}>0$; F1 $\lambda_{\min}>-10^{-8}$; F2 $\lambda_{\min}>-10^{-6}\lambda_{\max}$. These are context, not evidence for the exact verdict.

\paragraph{Code.} \texttt{data/code/}: \texttt{born\_exact.py} (exact tests, class conditions, intervals), \texttt{controls.py}, \texttt{run\_dataset.py}, \texttt{analysis2.py}, \texttt{final\_summary.py}, \texttt{inspect\_f1.py}, \texttt{gen\_lean.py}, \texttt{make\_result\_json.py}. The downloaded json was only parsed with \texttt{json.load}; the producer's own scripts were not run with \texttt{-I} (it would drop the script directory), a logged deviation.

\subsection{The incorrect linear criterion and the class conditions}
\paragraph{Q3.} $23\,184$ tensors pass the linear orthorhombic criterion of Wu et al.\ \cite{wu} (\cite{mc} Eq.~19) applied to all entries regardless of class; $342$ ($1.00\%$ of $N$) of them are exactly not positive definite ($328$ robustly); $0$ fail it while positive definite, consistent with necessity. By class, pass-but-not-PD: cubic $87$, tetragonal~I $63$, trigonal~I $57$, monoclinic $47$, triclinic $41$, hexagonal $29$, orthorhombic $15$, trigonal~II $3$, tetragonal~II $0$.

\paragraph{Q5 (correction C3).} Closed-form class conditions (cubic, hexagonal, tetragonal~I/II, trigonal~I/II, orthorhombic; Eqs.~6, 9, 11, 13, 15, 18 of~\cite{mc}) on the stored entries of $30\,203$ tensors disagree with exact positive definiteness of the full tensor in $555$ cases: $544$ class-stable but not positive definite, $11$ class-unstable but positive definite. Most are expected: the class formulae read only representative entries (e.g.\ $C_{11}$ for $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$, $C_{44}$ for $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$), and the stored tensors do not satisfy those class equalities exactly. The auditor's split of the $544$ by block: $184$ have a Voigt diagonal entry $\le0$, $299$ a non-positive-definite normal $3\times3$ block, $55$ a non-positive-definite shear block, and $6$ fail only through normal--shear couplings. So $483$ are driven by violated class equalities and $61$ by off-diagonal couplings (mostly outside the class zero pattern). This is not an error in either method. Monoclinic and triclinic reduce to the generic test and are not cross-checked.

\subsection{Float pipelines (Tier \tX)}
F0 disagrees with the exact verdict on $0$ tensors. F1 ($-10^{-8}$ absolute) disagrees on $236$, all calling a not-positive-definite tensor stable; $234$ are the null tensors and $2$ are not. F2 (relative $-10^{-6}\lambda_{\max}$) disagrees on $3$ ($2$ not null). The disagreements are essentially tolerance verdicts on null-like tensors plus two near-singular tensors. The auditor's own numpy call reproduces the figures.

\subsection{DFT-uncertainty context (illustrative)}
Exact test of $S-\theta\|S\|_F I$ among the $22\,842$ positive definite tensors: $\theta=1\%$: $22\,074$; $2\%$: $21\,224$; $5\%$: $17\,983$; $10\%$: $10\,703$; $20\%$: $611$. The dataset stores no uncertainty; these thresholds are illustrative stand-ins and not statements about JARVIS accuracy.

\subsection{Dataset observations (neutral)}\label{sec:obs}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item $59\,400$ of $93\,902$ records have \texttt{elastic\_tensor} $=$ \texttt{"na"}; $179$ tensors contain NaN or infinity.
\item $234$ stored tensors are numerically zero; $12$ tensors have a literal zero row.

\section{Interpretation}\label{sec:interp}
The percentage is the fraction of stored DFT tensors that are not positive definite under one stated reading (exact decimals, symmetrised, zero-stress criterion). A tensor with a negative eigenvalue is a calculated tensor that is not positive definite; finite-difference noise, unrelaxed or hypothetical structures, residual stress, magnetism and convergence all remain possible reasons, and finite-difference tensors of dynamically unstable cells are expected to be non-positive-definite at times. The criterion tested is the zero-stress Born criterion (Mouhat--Coudert Eq.~20 replaces $C$ by $B$ under load); residual stress in a computed cell changes the relevant matrix. The JARVIS elastic-property paper~\cite{jarvis_elastic} invites users to apply Born's criterion but, to the extent the auditor read it, reports no unstable fraction. \textbf{Novelty:} to our knowledge no exact-rational Born audit of the whole JARVIS elastic dataset has been published; this is not an exhaustive search and not a priority claim. The result neither validates nor invalidates any individual entry or the dataset.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{AI audit only; human audit and statement lock owed.} No person has certified that the Lean statements mean what their gloss says; the \texttt{lean\_runner.py} gate was not run by producer or auditor.
\item \textbf{Unverified, from the audit.} (a) The physical meaning of the JARVIS fields \texttt{ehull} and \texttt{min\_ir\_mode}, and the convergence state of individual calculations. (b) Born and Huang (1954) was not read; the criterion is taken from Mouhat--Coudert. (c) The producer's own \texttt{controls.py} was not re-run by the auditor, which ran its own controls. (d) The auditor's code and per-entry output are scratch files not shipped here; its reproduction is documented in \texttt{AUDIT.md} but cannot be re-run from this bundle. (e) No Lean certificates exist beyond the $18$ entries plus $2$ controls.
\item \textbf{Reading.} Everything depends on taking each stored float as the exact rational of its shortest decimal and on testing the symmetric part. A different reading (e.g.\ the tensor as the rounded value of an unknown exact tensor) is what the rounding-robustness analysis addresses, with the limits given there.
\item \textbf{Zero-stress criterion.} Not valid as a stability statement for a cell under residual stress.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration} (logged in \texttt{data/RESULT.md}): sympy on a $14\,615$-entry subset (Bareiss on all, and the auditor's characteristic polynomial on all, close the gap); post hoc additions (half-width floor, null-tensor exclusion, $K$/$G$ sanity, context counts, asymmetry test); two float tolerance conventions fixed in the prereg and used as written; Lean limited to controls and an $18$-entry sample (the prereg called it a stretch); producer scripts not run with \texttt{-I}; the wrong internal timestamp of the prereg (C7).
\item \textbf{Not run:} the repository guards \texttt{antigravity\_guard.py}, \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py} and \texttt{pytest tests/} were not run for this paper.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Only stored 6x6 tensors of one dataset version (9-24-2025). No claim about any material, any laboratory, any other database, or JARVIS's accuracy.
\end{itemize}
