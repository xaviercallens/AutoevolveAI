\begin{abstract}
McDannald et al.\ (arXiv 2501.04825) define an intrinsic capture efficiency $\xi=\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}/E_{\rm total}$ for a sorbent in an
idealised piston cycle and conclude that it is ``greatly beneficial to start cold'': 11 of the 14 Pareto rows of their Table~2 start
at $T_1\le 217.7$ K, and all start at or below $236.6$ K. Their $E_{\rm total}$ is a face-value sum of heats and works: the cooling of Step~1 is counted as a heat of adsorption at $T_1$,
the return Step~3 is set to zero (eq.~5), and no ambient temperature appears. We asked whether the conclusion
survives when every heat of the sorbent and its adsorbate is weighted by its Carnot factor against an explicit ambient $T_0$, their sub-ambient cooling is priced, and the
return step is charged; version~2 also prices the chilling of the contacted \emph{feed air} ($1/x=2{,}500$ mol of air per mol CO$_2$ at capture fraction~1), which v1 left out.
\textbf{Our preregistered hypothesis H1 was that the optimum would move to $T_1\ge T_0$ (prior $0.35$). At the preregistered setting ($T_0=298.15$~K, no heat recovery) it is refuted with and without feed-air pricing.}
(i) \emph{Base accounting (feed air not priced, ideal machines):} for all 14 Table~2 rows the exergy-optimal start, found on a joint grid over $T_1$, $\Delta T$ and $P_{\rm end}$, lies at $200$--$236.75$~K
in all 9 unconstrained variants ($T_0\in\{273.15,298.15,313.15\}$~K, $r\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ heat recovery); the 9 purity-constrained variants are vacuous for every row (no warm path on our grid reaches the stored purity), so 126 of the 252 row-variants are informative, not 252.
The cold-start benefit ratio (best $\xi$ cold over best $\xi$ with $T_1\ge298.15$~K) is $10$--$818$ at face value, $3.5$--$129$ at $r=0$ and $1.29$--$6.6$ at $r=1$.
(ii) \emph{Feed air priced, no recuperation ($\varepsilon=0$), ideal machines, $T_0=298.15$~K:} at $r=0$ a cold start still wins in 14/14 rows, but much less cold ($T_1^\ast\approx254$--$290$~K instead of $200$--$237$~K; grid-dependent by one or two cells, Section~\ref{sec:air}) and by a much smaller factor ($1.46$--$8.0$); at $r=0.5$, $T_1^\ast=268$--$290$~K; at $r=1$, \textbf{in 11 of 14 rows the optimum moves to ambient}: $T_1^\ast=297.25$--$297.75$~K on a $0.25$~K grid, and the best start below ambient beats the best start at or above it by only $0.07$--$0.35\%$, less than each row's own printed $\xi$ uncertainty (not a paired test); a start $\ge10$~K below ambient then loses unless a recuperator recovers more than $91$--$96\%$ of the air's cold (auditor's coarser split: $77$--$90\%$). Only VANNIK (both rows) and LETQAE01 keep a cold start ($284$--$288$~K) at every $\varepsilon$.
So \emph{the cold-start conclusion survives exergy accounting of the heats and works as published, but its size, and at full recovery its existence, depend on how the cost of chilling the feed air is priced.}
Exergy-weighted efficiencies on the stored paths are $25.4$--$36.8$~\umolJ{} against $5.98$--$11.26$ at face value (nominal). \textbf{H2:} no published $\xi$ exceeds $1/W_{\min}$ of the ideal mixture (dataset maximum $11.259$~\umolJ{} against $51.6$).
\textbf{Retraction:} v1 also reported five dataset entries with heat flows inconsistent with the second law; those flags were produced by our own Step-3 quadrature and vanish, over all 15,161 paths, when we use the published Step-2 rule. The revised numbers were computed by the producer and the feed-air scenario family was independently re-checked by a separate AI agent instance (ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS, corrections applied; no human review).

\section{A step-by-step account for non-specialists}\label{sec:human}
\paragraph{Step 1: the cycle as published.}\tL{} A sorbent (here a metal-organic framework, MOF) sits in a piston. \emph{Step~1:} it is exposed to ``air'' (400 ppm CO$_2$, balance N$_2$) at
temperature $T_1$ and pressure $P_1$ and reaches equilibrium, releasing heat of adsorption that is carried away to keep $T_1$ fixed. \emph{Step~2:} the chamber is closed and the temperature is raised and the
pressure lowered along a path to $(T_{\rm end},P_{\rm end})$; gas desorbs and leaves through a check-valve. \emph{Step~3:} the system returns to $(T_1,P_1)$. The captured amounts
are $\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}$, $\Delta n_{\rm N_2}$ (mol/kg), the purity is $x_{\rm end}=\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}/(\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}+\Delta n_{\rm N_2})$ (eq.~7) and the metric is
\[\xi=\frac{\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}}{E_{\rm total}}\quad(\text{eq.~8}),\qquad E_{\rm total}=E_1+E_2+E_3\quad(\text{eq.~6}).\]

\paragraph{Step 2: the energy balance as read.}\tL{} Eq.~1: $E_1=Q_{\rm ads}-W_1$, which in the text becomes
$E_1=\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}\bigl(q_{\rm ads,CO_2}(T_1)+RT_1\bigr)+\Delta n_{\rm N_2}\bigl(q_{\rm ads,N_2}(T_1)+RT_1\bigr)$: a heat removed at $T_1$ plus a compression work.
Eq.~2: $E_2=Q_{\rm ads,CO_2}+Q_{\rm ads,N_2}-W_{2,{\rm CO_2}}-W_{2,{\rm N_2}}+E_{\rm heat,CO_2}+E_{\rm heat,N_2}+E_{\rm heat,sorb}+E_{\Delta P}$, and eq.~4 writes it as an integral over a path $s$ with
$dT/ds\ge0$, $dP/ds\le0$ (eq.~3), using heat capacities $\tfrac92R$ and $\tfrac72R$ for adsorbed CO$_2$ and N$_2$ and a fitted sorbent heat capacity $C_{V,{\rm sorb}}(T)$.
The Julia code differs from eq.~4 as typeset in two places: it \emph{adds} the desorption work term ($+\,d\,RT_{\rm mid}$ where eq.~4 prints $-RT\,n$), and it takes the absolute value
$\lvert nRT\ln(P_i/P_{i-1})\rvert$ of the pressure term. The published numbers follow the code (Table~2 is reproduced only with the code's sign; with eq.~4's sign BEVQID's nominal $\xi$ would be $9.59$ instead of $9.15$~\umolJ{}, the auditor's figure),
and our ledger keeps the code's convention. Eq.~5: $E_3=0$ (``we assume no energy recovery''; the text adds that real systems would recover some). Eq.~9 is $C=1/\xi$.
Eq.~10 is the optimisation prior: $T_1\sim U(200,400)$ K (the optimiser bound in \texttt{Path\_optimizer.jl} is also $[200,400]$ K), $\Delta T\sim U(0,200)$, $P_1\sim U(101325,\,1.1\times101325)$ Pa, $\Delta P\sim U(-101225,0)$ Pa. Our Python
re-implementation follows the Julia functions line by line (\texttt{data/code/k1/dac\_model.py}); the Julia code is not redistributed here (Section~\ref{sec:licence}).

\paragraph{Step 3: why heats at face value differ from exergy.}\tL{}\,\tC{} A joule of heat is not a joule of work. Heat $Q$ delivered at temperature $T>T_0$ (with $T_0$ the ambient temperature) is worth at most
$Q(1-T_0/T)$ of work, the Carnot factor; this is the cost of producing that heat reversibly from work. Removing heat $Q$ \emph{below} ambient (e.g.\ cooling the sorbent to $T_1\approx200$ K while it adsorbs) needs a refrigerator, which costs at least $Q(T_0/T-1)$ of work;
heat rejected above ambient can be thrown away (no-credit accounting, $r=0$) or partly turned back into work (full reversible recovery, $r=1$). The paper's face-value sum treats all three alike
and sets the return step to zero, which is the point of our audit. We therefore (i) fix an explicit $T_0\in\{273.15,298.15,313.15\}$ K; (ii) record every heat of the \emph{sorbent and its adsorbate} once, with its direction and temperature (Step~1: $Q_{\rm ads}$ removed at $T_1$; Step~2: the per-step
heat at the mid-step temperature; Step~3: sensible cooling of the sorbent and of the adsorbed gas from $T_{\rm end}$ back to $T_1$, an \emph{explicit return step}, discretised with the same right-point rule $c_i\,\Delta T_i$ that the published Step~2 uses, so that the sorbent's heating and cooling close exactly; v1 used a trapezoid here, see Section~\ref{sec:h2} and the errata); (iii) weight each by $\lvert1-T_0/T\rvert$ with the sign convention above; (iv) keep the works as published. The cost is
$B(r)=B_{\rm NC}-r\cdot({\rm credits})$ per mole of CO$_2$ and $\xi_{\rm ex}=1/B$. \textbf{Two assumptions of this ``base'' accounting carry weight} (audit item B3): the \emph{contacted feed air} is not in the ledger (adsorbing at $T_1<T_0$ means the air passing the sorbent must be at $T_1$ too, about $2{,}500$~mol per mol CO$_2$), and the machines are ideal (Carnot refrigerator and heat pump). Section~\ref{sec:air} prices the feed air and degrades the machines. Lean states the two weights (\lean{carnot\_in\_mem\_unit}: $0<1-T_0/T<1$ for $T>T_0$, \id{A-0001}; \lean{refrig\_weight\_lt\_one}: $0<T_0/T-1<1$ for $T_0/2<T<T_0$) \tA; that these are the right weights is the textbook argument, not a Lean statement.

\paragraph{Step 4: the reversible floor.}\tA{}\,\tX{} Even a perfect machine must pay the ideal-mixture minimum work to extract CO$_2$ from the mixture. For a pure product at mole fraction $x$ this is
$W_{\min}=RT_0\ln(1/x)$, which is $19.4$ kJ/mol at $x=400$ ppm and $T_0=298.15$ K (hand arithmetic; $1/W_{\min}\approx51.6$~\umolJ{}). Lean proves that this function is positive for $0<x<1$ and strictly decreasing in $x$ (\lean{wmin\_pure\_pos}, \lean{wmin\_pure\_strictAnti}, \id{A-0004}); for less pure products our code uses
the corresponding mixture expression (Tier~X, not in Lean). A model whose reversible exergy cost $B_{\rm rev}$ (all credits taken) falls \emph{below} $W_{\min}$ would violate the second law, so it would signal inconsistent inputs, provided our own Step-3 quadrature is consistent with the published Step~2 (v1's was not; Section~\ref{sec:h2}). This is H2.

\paragraph{Step 5: the closed-form thresholds in a stylised surrogate.}\tC{} To see \emph{why} a cold start can still win, we use a two-component surrogate of the cycle: van~'t~Hoff Henry loadings for CO$_2$ and N$_2$ with heats of adsorption $q_C,q_N$, a sensible-heat
term $c=\gamma Rn_0$ and a recovery fraction $r$ on the sensible heat, started at $T_c<T_0$ instead of $T_0$ and heated to the same $T_h$. The benefit is
\[\beta=C(T_0)-C(T_c)=A+\lambda B+(1-r)\,\gamma R\,D,\qquad A_{\rm ex}=-(T_0-T_c)\Bigl(\frac{q_C}{T_c}-R\Bigr),\quad A_{\rm fv}=R(T_0-T_c),\]
where $\lambda$ is the N$_2$-to-CO$_2$ loading ratio at the inlet and $B,D$ are differences of loading-ratio-weighted terms (\id{C-0001}; derivation by hand, algebra in Lean). Beside each step the Lean statement is:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item the CO$_2$ term alone penalises cold under exergy: $A_{\rm ex}<0$ whenever $q_C>RT_c$ (\lean{numerator\_factor}, \lean{co2\_term\_penalises\_cold}, \id{A-0002}); at face value it favours cold ($A_{\rm fv}>0$, \lean{co2\_term\_facevalue\_favours\_cold}). This is \emph{not} the sign of the whole benefit;
\item $\beta$ is affine in $r$ (\lean{benefit\_affine\_in\_r}), and when $\gamma D\ge0$ the case $r=1$ is the worst case for a cold start (\lean{worst\_case\_full\_recovery}, \id{A-0003}); the sign of $D$ is \emph{not} certified over the box (\texttt{cert\_sign.log}: $D_{\rm ex}>0$ certified on $2.9\%$ of it, $97.1\%$ undecided), so ``$r=1$ is the worst case'' is conditional;
\item \textbf{N$_2$ threshold} (at $r=1$): for $B>0$, cold wins iff $\lambda>\lambda^\ast=-A_{\rm ex}/B=(T_0-T_c)(q_C/T_c-R)/B$ (\lean{lambda\_threshold\_iff});
\item \textbf{heat-capacity threshold} (for $r<1$): with $N=-(A+\lambda B)$ and $RD$ replaced by $(1-r)RD>0$, cold wins iff $\gamma>N/((1-r)RD)$ (\lean{threshold\_iff});
\item a rational instance ($R=8.314$, $q=40$ kJ/mol, $T_c=200$ K, $T_0=298.15$ K) with negative CO$_2$-only term, checked by \lean{instance\_r1} (this is \lean{norm\_num}, an identity check and not a theorem about the world).
\end{itemize}
Reading: under exergy accounting the refrigeration of the CO$_2$ adsorption heat is a real penalty, and a cold start wins only because lowering $T$ changes the \emph{ratio} of N$_2$ to CO$_2$ uptake (the N$_2$ term $\lambda B$ and the sensible term). This is the paper's own mechanism (CO$_2$ uptake diverges from N$_2$ at low $T$); the purity at the optimum is $0.98$ at 200 K but $0.20$ at 240 K for LETQAE01 on its slice.
At $r=1$ this surrogate predicts the wrong sign for most Table~2 rows (Section~\ref{sec:surr}), so the mechanism is \emph{illustrated}, not established.

\subsection{New in v2: pricing the chilling of the feed air}\label{sec:air}
\tX{} (\id{X-0013}) \emph{The omitted term.} Adsorbing at $T_1<T_0$ means the contacted air must be at $T_1$. In the model the inlet CO$_2$ mole fraction is $x=400/10^6$ (\texttt{run\_optimize\_intrinsic\_dac.jl}, line~15), so at capture fraction $f=1$ (every CO$_2$ molecule of the contacted air captured; the case most favourable to a cold start) the air contacted per cycle is $n_{\rm air}=\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}/x=2{,}500\,\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}$. Bringing it from $T_0$ to $T_1$ with an ideal refrigerator costs $n_{\rm air}c_p\,[T_0\ln(T_0/T_1)-(T_0-T_1)]$ ($c_p=\tfrac72R$; for $T_1>T_0$, the heat-pump analogue $n_{\rm air}c_p[(T_1-T_0)-T_0\ln(T_1/T_0)]$). A recuperator of effectiveness $\varepsilon\in[0,1]$ recovers the share $\varepsilon$ of it; we add $(1-\varepsilon)$ times this exergy to $B(r)$. $\varepsilon=1$ is the base accounting. For $f<1$, replace $(1-\varepsilon)$ by $(1-\varepsilon)/f$. Note that $r=1$ with $\varepsilon<1$ is a mixed bookend: reversible for the sorbent's heats, irreversible for the air. The scenario family is computed on the same stored per-path components as the base accounting (\texttt{code/k1/rev\_scan.py}, \texttt{rev\_analyse.py}; the components reproduce the independent \texttt{fast\_costs.py} to $1.0\times10^{-15}$).

\subsection{H2: no published \texorpdfstring{$\xi$}{xi} above \texorpdfstring{$1/W_{\min}$}{1/Wmin}; no second-law violation in the dataset}\label{sec:h2}
\tX{} (\id{X-0005}) \textbf{The preregistered part stands.} Over the 8,765 materials with valid paths (15,161 paths; the public record holds 11,656 optimised files) the largest published face-value $\xi$ is $11.259$~\umolJ{} (VANNIK), against $1/W_{\min}\approx51.6$~\umolJ{}: no published $\xi$ exceeds $1/W_{\min}$.

\textbf{Retraction (v2).} Version~1 reported that five dataset entries (CSMNOX, GUSNEN, PACYIE, SABWAU01, UJOKIP05) had heat flows inconsistent with the second law, which it read as inconsistent inputs. \textbf{That claim is withdrawn: the apparent violations were produced by our own quadrature rule and vanish under the published rule.} Our G3 floor check first flagged 18 path/$T_0$ pairs at $298.15$~K in five materials (14 at $273.15$~K, 21 at $313.15$~K, against the lowest-pressure floor), and 16, 22 and 25 pairs (63 in all, ten materials) against the $P_0$ floor. All of them come from the v1 Step-3 return, which we discretised with a trapezoid while the published Step~2 uses the right-point rule $c_i\Delta T_i$; the mismatch is of order $(dC_V/dT)\,\Delta T_{\rm step}$ per step. Every flagged path has a tiny working capacity ($\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}=3\times10^{-9}$ to $3.4\times10^{-4}$~mol/kg, face-value $\xi=6\times10^{-8}$ to $2.9\times10^{-3}$~\umolJ{}), so the mismatch, divided by $\Delta n_{\rm CO_2}$, gave $B_{\rm rev}$ down to $-1.5\times10^{6}$~kJ/mol. We reran the whole record with both rules (\texttt{code/k1/rev\_h2\_rules.py}, all 15,161 paths, both floors, all three $T_0$, with the v1 counter bug fixed): the trapezoid reproduces the v1 flags exactly (14/18/21 and 16/22/25), and \textbf{the right-point rule gives 0 violations at every $T_0$ against both floors}. With the right-point rule the G2 sensible closure holds to $4.4\times10^{-16}$ on every path (exact by construction). Two further errors of v1 are corrected with the retraction: SABWAU01's value was $B_{\rm rev}=-41.9$~\emph{kJ}/mol (v1 printed ``J/mol''), and v1 called SABWAU01 and UJOKIP05 ``not degenerate''; they have purities $0.91$ and $0.67$ but working capacities of only $4.2\times10^{-5}$ and $1.4\times10^{-4}$~mol/kg ($\xi_{\rm fv}=2.4\times10^{-4}$ and $8.8\times10^{-4}$~\umolJ{}), so they are degenerate in working capacity. These flags were evidence about our floor, not about the dataset entries.
Changing the Step-3 rule moves the Table~1 exergy numbers by at most $2.4\times10^{-4}$ relative (all 14 rows, three $T_0$, three $r$); no printed digit of Table~1 changes, and the 14 rows still pass the gate. The v1 counter note (the first script skipped cases with $W_{\min}(\text{low }P)<0$) is superseded by the full rerun.

We plant an accounting in which a cold start must lose: every refrigeration cost (sorbent and feed air, $\varepsilon=0$) is multiplied by $M$. A path with $T_1\ge T_0$ rejects no heat below $T_0$, so its cost does not depend on $M$, while every path with $T_1<T_0$ pays $M$ times its Step-1 heat removal below ambient; for $M$ large enough the warm side must win on any finite grid. \section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Everything is inside a published simplified model}: Henry/IAST loadings, absolute adsorption, adsorbed-gas heat capacities $\tfrac92R$ and $\tfrac72R$, GP-extrapolated heat capacity, binary CO$_2$/N$_2$ air, no water, ideal piston. A different model could give a different answer; nothing transfers to real plants.
\item \textbf{Exergy weighting uses ideal reversible machines against one ambient $T_0$} in the base accounting and, in v2, a feed-air term at capture fraction~1 with recuperator effectiveness $\varepsilon$; works are kept at face value (dropping the $PV$ terms or charging the product recompression does not change the direction in the base accounting, auditor's check); no process-level recovery was modelled; $r$, $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$ are bookends, not designs. \textbf{The size and, at $r=1$, the existence of the cold-start benefit depend on the feed-air pricing} (Section~\ref{sec:air}); the degraded-machine cases are a sensitivity only.
\item \textbf{The joint search is a grid with refinement, not a proof of global optimality} ($T_{\rm end}\le400$~K, a restriction of the published domain); the cell-mass substitute shifts the saturation edge; the vacuity of the purity-constrained variants is a statement on this grid.
\item \textbf{The interval results are about the surrogate} and the surrogate is wrong on 11 of 14 Table~2 rows at $r=1$ and $T_0=273.15$ K (Section~3.7); the Lean results certify algebra only.
\item \textbf{The supplement was not obtained and not read}; the sub-ambient pricing question is settled only for the code and the main text.
\item \textbf{Audit status}: v1 was audited by an independent AI agent (not a person), verdict ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS (\texttt{AUDIT.md}); the auditor recompiled the Lean core and replayed it with \texttt{leanchecker} (rc~0, 118~s). This v2 is the producer's revision (\texttt{REVISION.md}) and was re-audited on 2026-10-28 by a separate AI agent instance (\texttt{AUDIT\_v2.md}; ACCEPT\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS, corrections applied; it reproduced the feed-air family, the vacuity count, the H1 negative control and the full-record H2 rerun, with the grid-dependent differences noted in Section~\ref{sec:air}); no human audit; no statement lock; GATE-1 not run. The advisor review of the K1 lane timed out and produced no output.
\item \textbf{Repository guards were not run} (\texttt{antigravity\_guard.py}, \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py}, \texttt{pytest tests/}); no guard output is claimed.
\item \textbf{No author contact} and no statement about the authors' intent. Wording that implies the NIST bound is wrong is not supported and is not intended; the metric is a face-value convention by the source's own description.
\item \textbf{Novelty is unchecked} beyond the source paper read; this is not a priority claim.
\item \textbf{Data note}: the run note quotes ``11/14'' surrogate disagreement without a temperature; the per-row recount shows it holds at $T_0=273.15$ K, with $10/13$ at $298.15$ K.
\end{itemize}
