\begin{abstract}
In the phenomenological low-dissipation Carnot engine model (heat $Q_x=Q_x^{(r)}-M_x/t_x$ per stroke), Ma, Xu, Dong and Sun~\cite{ma18} define an upper bound $\ep(\tilde P,\zeta,\eC)$ on the efficiency at given normalised power $\tilde P$ and dissipation asymmetry $\zeta\in[-1,1]$ (their Eq.~(13)). They state that it increases with $\zeta$ and that the analytic proof ``is tedious and difficult'', so they check it numerically (Appendix~B). We prove in Lean~4 that the \emph{root form} of the bound---the positive root of the quadratic obtained from their tangency condition---is strictly increasing in $\zeta$ on the whole box $0<\eC<1$, $\tilde P\in(0,1]$, $\zeta\in[-1,1]$ (Tier~\tA{}, statement lock owed). The proof is elementary: a root of a quadratic moves monotonically with a parameter in which the quadratic is monotone. We also machine-check their universal bound Eq.~(1) for every engine of their Eqs.~(3)--(4); \textbf{that bound is a known result with analytic proofs} (Ma and Fu~\cite{mafu}, Zhai~\cite{zhai}), so only the machine check is new. To our knowledge there is no earlier machine-checked proof of either statement; only the $\zeta$-monotonicity lacks a published analytic proof (search not systematic). The identification of the root form with Eq.~(13) is \emph{not} in Lean (symbolic identity up to the sign branch, plus high-precision numerics, Tier~\tC{}). The bound is a valid upper bound, \emph{not} attained in general. A model result; no device or power-plant claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, definitions and scope}\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{Model (Tier \tL{}).} Following Ma et al.~\cite{ma18} (Eqs.~(2)--(4)): a Carnot-like cycle with hot and cold isothermal strokes of durations $t_h,t_c>0$ exchanges heat $Q_x=Q_x^{(r)}-M_x/t_x$ ($x=h,c$), where $M_x>0$ is the dissipation coefficient. Put $a=\sqrt{M_h}$, $b=\sqrt{M_c}$, $M=(a+b)^2$, $\zeta=(a-b)/(a+b)\in(-1,1)$ and $q=\eC Q_h^{(r)}$ with $c=\eC\in(0,1)$ the Carnot efficiency. Power and efficiency are
\[
P=\frac{q-a^2/t_h-b^2/t_c}{t_h+t_c},\qquad \eta=\frac{q-a^2/t_h-b^2/t_c}{q/c-a^2/t_h},
\]
(their Eqs.~(3)--(4)). The maximal power is $P_{\max}=q^2/(4M)$ (Eq.~(6)) and $\tilde P=P/P_{\max}\in(0,1]$. Everything below is \emph{model-conditional}: it concerns this idealised model, not a device.
\paragraph{The bound $\ep$.} The source maximises $\eta$ over $t_h+t_c=\tau_+$, the largest cycle time compatible with power $P$ (their Eqs.~(9)--(11)), using that $\eta$ increases in $t_h$ and $t_c$. The result, Eq.~(13), is a closed form in $(\tilde P,\zeta,\eC)$. It is an \emph{upper bound} on the efficiency of every engine at normalised power $\tilde P$ and asymmetry $\zeta$: it relaxes the exact power constraint. It is \textbf{a valid upper bound and is not attained in general}; the source itself remarks that it ``is not a supremum for all the $\tilde P$'' (p.~3), and Zhai, Yue and Sun~\cite{zys} give an exact, tighter bound. In this paper $\ep$ means this quantity and ``the bound'' never means a tight or optimal one.
\paragraph{Root form.} With $s=\sqrt{1-\tilde P}\in[0,1)$ and $K=2/(1-s)>1$ define
\[
G(w)=K\bigl(w^2-(1-c)\bigr)-\tfrac{c}{4}\bigl((1-\zeta)+(1+\zeta)w\bigr)^2=Aw^2-Bw-C,
\]
$A=K-c(1+\zeta)^2/4>0$, $B=c(1-\zeta^2)/2\ge0$, $C=K(1-c)+c(1-\zeta)^2/4>0$, let $w_+=\bigl(B+\sqrt{B^2+4AC}\bigr)/(2A)$ be its unique positive root, and $\ep/\eC=(1-w_+^2)/c$ (the root form, \lean{etaPlus}). The Lean theorems are stated for the root form; the identification with Eq.~(13) is Section~\ref{sec:link}.

\section{Prior art and what is added}\label{sec:prior}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Eq.~(1) already has analytic proofs (Tier \tL{}).} Ma and Fu~\cite{mafu} (arXiv:2411.03849, Phys.\ Rev.\ E 112, 054130 (2025)), Appendix~B, prove analytically (sign of $d\tilde\eta_+/d\delta$) that their upper bound increases in a dissipation ratio $\delta\in[0,1]$, and at $\delta=1$ obtain their Eq.~(14), identical to Eq.~(1) of~\cite{ma18}. Zhai~\cite{zhai} (arXiv:2605.02732) re-derives Eq.~(1) by a short geometric argument. Zhai, Yue and Sun~\cite{zys} (arXiv:2509.07721) state that~\cite{ma18} already ``rigorously proved'' the Holubec--Ryabov conjecture and give an exact, tighter bound. Holubec and Ryabov~\cite{hr} (2016; read by the auditor) derived the bound analytically only near zero and maximum power, with numerical evidence in between. Ye and Holubec (2021) concern heat pumps and are not relevant. Esposito et al.\ (2010) were not re-read; they enter only as a positive control (Section~\ref{sec:controls}).
\item \textbf{$\zeta$-monotonicity of Eq.~(13): no analytic proof found.} The source checks it numerically (Appendix~B: ``the analytical proof of its monotonicity is tedious and difficult. Instead numerical calculate the derivative''). The Ma--Fu family is a \emph{different} interpolating function: it agrees with the root form at $\zeta=\pm1$ ($\delta=0,1$) but differs in between by up to $0.038$ under $\delta=(1+\zeta)/2$ (and by $0.40$ and $0.055$ under two other natural maps; auditor script \texttt{mafu\_cmp.py}); their proof is the closest analogue and is cited, not reused.
\item \textbf{What is added.} (i) A Lean proof of the $\zeta$-monotonicity of the root form: elementary, with a claim to novelty only in being a proof (none found analytically) and a machine-checked one. (ii) A Lean machine check of Eq.~(1) for every engine of Eqs.~(3)--(4): a \emph{known} result, so only the machine check is new. \textbf{To our knowledge this is the first machine-checked proof of either statement}; the literature search was not systematic. We do not claim a first proof of Eq.~(1).
\end{itemize}

\section{Derivation of the root form (Tier \tC{})}\label{sec:deriv}
Write $u=\eta/c$, $w=\sqrt{1-\eta}=\sqrt{1-cu}$. This section corrects step~3 of the producer's \texttt{code/derivation.md} (audit correction~6); the producer's file is shipped unchanged and is superseded here.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=1pt
\item From Eq.~(4): $q(1-u)=w^2a^2/t_h+b^2/t_c$.
\item Cauchy--Schwarz, $A^2/x+B^2/y\ge(A+B)^2/(x+y)$, with $A=wa$, $B=b$, $x+y\le\tau_+$ and $q\tau_+=KM$ (since $\tau_+=q(1+s)/(2P)$, $K=2/(1-s)$): $K(1-u)\ge\bigl((1-\zeta)+(1+\zeta)w\bigr)^2/4$, using $a/(a+b)=(1+\zeta)/2$.
\item $\ep/\eC$ is the largest $u$ (equivalently the smallest $w$) for which this holds, i.e.\ the \emph{maximum of $\eta$ over $t_h+t_c=\tau_+$}. This is an upper bound at power $P$ and is \emph{not} attained in general: the equality in the power constraint needs $t_h/t_c=a/b$, whereas the efficiency Cauchy--Schwarz equality needs $t_h/t_c=wa/b$. The boundary of the feasible set is the tangency condition of the source's Eq.~(12). Multiplying by $c$ and using $c(1-u)=w^2-(1-c)$ gives $G(w)\ge0$.
\item $G$ is a convex quadratic with $G(0)=-C<0$, so $G(w)\ge0$ for $w\ge0$ iff $w\ge w_+$, and $\ep/\eC=(1-w_+^2)/c$. At fixed $w\in[0,1)$ the quantity $T=(1-\zeta)+(1+\zeta)w=(1+w)-\zeta(1-w)$ is strictly decreasing in $\zeta$ and $\ge 2w\ge0$, so $G$ is strictly increasing in $\zeta$; hence $w_+$ strictly decreases and $\ep$ strictly increases.
\end{enumerate}
At $\zeta=1$: $(K-c)w^2=K(1-c)$ and $\ep/\eC=(K-1)/(K-c)=(1+s)^2/(2(1+s)-c\tilde P)$, which is Eq.~(1) solved for $\eta/\eC$. At $\tilde P=1$ ($s=0$, $K=2$): $\zeta=-1$ gives $1/2$ and $\zeta=1$ gives $1/(2-c)$ (Esposito sandwich).

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\begin{thm}[Tier \tA{}, statement lock owed; \lean{etaPlus\_strictMono}]\label{thm:mono}
For $0<c<1$, $K>1$ and $-1\le\zeta_1<\zeta_2\le1$: $\ep(c,K,\zeta_1)<\ep(c,K,\zeta_2)$, with $\ep$ the root form above. (Here $K>1$ contains the physical range $K=2/(1-s)\ge2$.)
\end{thm}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea (the Lean proof follows it).} Nonnegative roots of $G(\cdot;\zeta)$ are unique (\lean{root\_unique}), $<1$ (\lean{root\_lt\_one}), and $G$ is strictly increasing in $\zeta$ on $[0,1)$ (\lean{G\_strict\_mono\_zeta}); with $G_{\zeta}(w_1)-G_{\zeta}(w_2)=(w_1-w_2)(A(w_1+w_2)-B)$ and $A w>B$ at a positive root, a larger $\zeta$ forces a smaller root (\lean{root\_strict\_anti}). Quantifiers range over the whole box; the physical $K$ is a subset.

\begin{cor}[\lean{etaPlus\_one}, \lean{etaPlus\_le\_one\_value}, \lean{eq1\_of\_P}; Tier \tA{}]
$\ep(c,K,1)=(K-1)/(K-c)$, hence $\ep(\zeta)\le(K-1)/(K-c)$, and for $s\in[0,1)$, $\tilde P=1-s^2$: $\ep(c,2/(1-s),\zeta)+(1-c)\tilde P/(2(1+s)-c\tilde P)\le1$ (Eq.~(1) for the root form; $\tilde P=0$ is trivial and not formalised).
\end{cor}

\begin{thm}[engine-level Eq.~(1), \lean{engine\_bound}; Tier \tA{}; a known result]\label{thm:eng}
For all $a,b,q,t_h,t_c>0$, $0<c<1$, $P>0$, $\tilde P$ with $\tilde Pq^2=4(a+b)^2P$, $P$ and $\eta$ given by Eqs.~(3)--(4) and positive hot-bath heat $q/c-a^2/t_h>0$:
$\eta/c+(1-c)\tilde P/\bigl(2(1+\sqrt{1-\tilde P})-c\tilde P\bigr)\le1$.
\end{thm}
\noindent This uses Cauchy--Schwarz (\lean{cs2}), the quadratic $P\tau^2-q\tau+M\le0$ and $w\le1$; no $\zeta$ and no Eq.~(13) is used. It is a machine check of a result proved analytically in~\cite{mafu,zhai}; \textbf{only the machine check is new}. $\tilde P\le1$ is derived, not assumed. Engines with $M_h=0$ or $M_c=0$ ($\zeta=\mp1$) are excluded (limits only). One declaration carries \lean{set\_option maxHeartbeats 3000000} (an elaboration budget; the term is re-checked by \texttt{leanchecker}).

\paragraph{Scope: what the Lean theorems do and do not say.} The Lean statements are about the root form, i.e.\ they \emph{state the paper's model through the root form}. They prove \textbf{neither} that $\ep$ is attained \textbf{nor} that every engine with asymmetry $\zeta$ has $\eta/\eC\le\ep(\zeta)$: \lean{engine\_bound} avoids $\zeta$ (it uses $w\le1$, the worst case $\zeta=1$). The latter inequality rests on the derivation of Section~\ref{sec:deriv} (checked by the auditor) and on numerics (0 of 53,021 random engines violate it). The missing Lean lemma is short (Cauchy--Schwarz $\Rightarrow G(w;\zeta)\ge0\Rightarrow w\ge w_+$ via \lean{root\_unique} and convexity) and is owed.

\section{Link to Eq.~(13) as printed}\label{sec:link}
\paragraph{Transcription.} Read from the page images (arXiv:1802.09806v4, Eq.~(13)), the square-root vinculum ends after ``$+1-\eC$''; the term $-\tfrac{1+\zeta}{4}\eC\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-\tilde P}\bigr)$ lies \emph{outside} the root, inside the square bracket. \textbf{The producer's preregistered transcription was wrong}: it put this term inside the square root. The disagreement with the root form was reported first (up to $0.23$; \texttt{logs/link\_first\_transcription.log.txt}), diagnosed through the symbolic structure (\texttt{code/sym.py}) and the page image, and the transcription corrected; the original disagreement is kept in the logs and the preregistration file is not edited. The corrected reading agrees with the root form to $1.6\cdot10^{-58}$ at $3000$ random $(c,\tilde P,\zeta)$ at 60 digits (\texttt{logs/link.log.txt}, Tier \tC{}).
\paragraph{Symbolic branch identity (auditor; Tier \tC{}, none in Lean).} Eliminating $w$ from the tangency equality by squaring away the square roots gives a quadratic $R(u)=0$. Writing Eq.~(13) as $u_{13}(x)$ with $x$ the square root, the remainder of $R(u_{13}(x))$ modulo $x^2-(\text{radicand})$ is $0$ (sympy, \texttt{audit\_scripts/sym\_audit.py}): \emph{both sign branches of Eq.~(13) are exact roots of the tangency quadratic} once square roots are squared away. The branch is then selected numerically (mpmath, 50 digits, 5000 random points; minimum radicand $2.0\cdot10^{-3}>0$): the $+$ branch matches the root form to $5.0\cdot10^{-48}$; the $-$ branch is off by up to $0.47$; the ``term inside the root'' misreading by up to $0.23$. So the identification is symbolic up to the sign branch, with the $+$ branch chosen numerically, and is not in Lean.
