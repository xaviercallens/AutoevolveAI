\begin{abstract}
We study the ideal-ignition criterion $\Pc\ge\PB$ (fusion power against bremsstrahlung, no transport loss) of the $0$-D power-balance model of Wurzel and Hsu~\cite{wh} for proton--boron-11, with two reactivity fits: Nevins--Swain~\cite{ns}, as transcribed from the Wurzel--Hsu public code (MIT licence, commit \texttt{4ae563bf}), and the updated S-factor of Wang et al.~\cite{wang}. In the box $\Ti\in[50,500]$~keV, boron fraction $x\in[0.05,0.5]$, interval-arithmetic enclosures (Arb balls, Tier~\tC{}, not replayed in Lean) give:
\textbf{there is no ignition window at $\Te=\Ti$}: the certified maximum of $\Pc/\PB$ is in $[0.4214,0.4256]$ (Nevins--Swain) and $[0.4287,0.4326]$ (Wang). A window requires $\Te/\Ti$ below a threshold $\theta^\ast$, certified only for $\theta\le1$: Nevins--Swain $[0.4223,0.4308]$ over the box and $[0.3753,0.3828]$ at $x=1/6$; Wang $[0.4381,0.4470]$ and $[0.396,0.402]$. These thresholds hold for \emph{these fits and this bremsstrahlung only}: a uniform factor $1.1/1.2/1.32$ on the reactivity moves the threshold to $0.477/0.524/0.579$ (auditor, float). The ``no window at $\Te=\Ti$'' statement is more robust: it survives any reactivity up to $2.349$ times the Nevins--Swain fit (certified).
As a positive control the D-T ideal-ignition temperature and the Table~II/III minima of~\cite{wh} are reproduced to the printed digits \emph{for the model the authors' code ran}; the gate model was chosen after a float run, and the most literal text reading of the paper prints $3.0\times10^{21}$ instead of $2.9\times10^{21}$ for the triple-product minimum.
The Bosch--Hale and Nevins--Swain coefficients \textbf{could not be obtained from the primary papers by the producer or by the auditor} and are \textbf{not verified}; this matters most for $\theta^\ast$.
Two small Lean facts ($20x(1-x)\le(1+4x)^2$ and $1+2.2t+3t^2-4.375t^{2.5}>0$ on $[0,1]$) are kernel-checked, independently recompiled and replayed by the auditor, and are Tier~\tA{} once a statement-lock entry exists (none yet).

\section{Model and question}\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{The model (Tier \tL{}; \texttt{PBI-L-0001}).} As in Wurzel and Hsu~\cite{wh} (eqs.~(6)--(8), (16), (22), (41), (D1)--(D5)): the fusion power density is $S_c=f_c\,n_1n_2\langle\sigma v\rangle E_F$ and the bremsstrahlung loss is $S_B=C_B\,n_e^2\sqrt{\Te}\,\gamma(Z_{\rm eff})$ with $C_B=5.34\times10^{-37}$~W\,m$^3$\,keV$^{-1/2}$ and the relativistic bracket
$$\gamma=Z_{\rm eff}\bigl(1+1.78\,t^{1.34}\bigr)+2.12\,t\bigl(1+1.1\,t+t^2-1.25\,t^{2.5}\bigr),\qquad t=\Te/m_ec^2,$$
(their eq.~(D2), equal to eq.~(14) of Ochs and Fisch~\cite{of}, which includes the $x^2$ term). Ideal ignition means $\Pc\ge\PB$, with ideal confinement and no conduction loss. For $\pB$: $f_c=1$, $E=8.68$--$8.70$~MeV, boron fraction $x=n_B/n_i$, $n_e=(1+4x)n_i$, $Z_{\rm eff}=(1+24x)/(1+4x)$. The ratio $R=\Pc/\PB$ does not depend on density. The Wurzel--Hsu optimal mix is $x=1/6$ ($Z_{\rm eff}=3$; their eqs.~(D3)--(D4), footnote~138). The temperature ratio $\theta=\Te/\Ti$ is a \emph{free parameter}; no mechanism that would keep $\Te<\Ti$ is modelled.

\paragraph{The question (preregistered; \texttt{docs/proposals/NEXT\_STEPS\_PROPOSAL\_4\_2026-10-27.md} and the addendum of the same date, item E1).} (a) Reproduce the D-T ideal-ignition numbers of~\cite{wh} as a positive control, with a gate fixed before the certified run. (b) For $\pB$ in the box $\Ti\in[50,500]$~keV, $x\in[0.05,0.5]$: certify the threshold $\theta^\ast$ above which $\Pc<\PB$ everywhere. (c) Certify whether any window exists at $\Te=\Ti$, under the older fit and under an updated fit. The preregistration asked for Tier~\tA{} inequalities and for coefficients pinned from the primary papers; neither was achieved (Section~\ref{sec:lim}, Deviations~1 and~3).

\section{Fits and what could not be verified}\label{sec:fits}
\paragraph{Reactivity fits.} (i) \emph{Nevins--Swain~\cite{ns}}: the high-temperature fit, valid $50$--$500$~keV, with coefficients $P_1,\dots,P_7$, $E_G$, $M_rc^2$ and a resonant term, as transcribed in the Wurzel--Hsu public code at commit \texttt{4ae563bf} (MIT licence; the commit is dated after the 2022 paper). (ii) \emph{Wang et al.~\cite{wang}}: an updated S-factor in three segments (Table~I of that paper), made a reactivity by the Maxwellian integral $\langle\sigma v\rangle=(8/\pi)^{1/2}\mu^{-1/2}T^{-3/2}\!\int_0^\infty\sigma E\,e^{-E/T}dE$, with $\sigma:=0$ above $10$~MeV (their stated fit range). The first Wang segment reprints the Nevins--Swain low-energy coefficients, which independently corroborates that part of the transcription. All coefficients are decimal strings in \texttt{data/fits.json} with per-block provenance.

\paragraph{Not verified against the primary papers (Tier \tL{}, \texttt{PBI-L-0003}; audit correction C9).} \textbf{Neither the producer nor the independent auditor could obtain Bosch--Hale (1992)~\cite{bh} or Nevins--Swain (2000)~\cite{ns}} (closed access or bot-challenged). The Bosch--Hale (D-T) and Nevins--Swain ($\pB$) coefficients are therefore \emph{secondary}: they are the values in the authors' code. The only checks are indirect: the transcribed Nevins--Swain fit divided by the Maxwellian integral of the transcribed Nevins--Swain S-factor is $0.985$--$0.996$ for $\Ti=50$--$200$~keV, $0.976$ and $0.965$ at $250$ and $300$~keV, $0.940$ at $400$ and $0.917$ at $500$~keV (producer; reproduced by the auditor; Tier~\tX{}). This excludes gross transcription errors but cannot detect an error of a few per cent, and the high-temperature drift is unexplained. The auditor's agreement with recalled Bosch--Hale values is memory, not a pinned source. \textbf{Because $\theta^\ast$ moves roughly in proportion to the reactivity, it inherits any error in the unverified digits directly}; the $\Te=\Ti$ exclusion has a margin of about $2.35$ and does not.

\paragraph{Other sources.} Read in full: Wang et al.\ (2601.00241) and Ochs--Fisch (2310.18508), via alphaXiv. Read in part: Wurzel--Hsu (physics sections and Appendices C/D; data tables not read). Sikora--Weller~\cite{sw} (J.\ Fusion Energy 35, 538) was read but gives no reactivity fit and is not used as one. Putvinski et al.\ (2019) was seen only through OCR summaries (bremsstrahlung eq.~(7) is consistent with the two texts read). Tentori--Belloni (2023) could not be accessed. Third-party full texts, the Wurzel--Hsu code and the paywall pages are \emph{not} in this bundle (Section~\ref{sec:corr}, C7).

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Arb enclosures (Tier \tC{}; \texttt{PBI-C-0001}).} \texttt{data/code/e1\_model.py} implements the model with python-flint~0.6.0 Arb balls at $128$ bits; every coefficient is a decimal string. For a cross-section fit the Maxwellian integral is a rigorous \texttt{acb.integral} on each analytic segment plus a bound on $(0,1]$~keV; a reactivity enclosure over a temperature interval $[a,b]$ follows from point integrals by $\langle\sigma v\rangle(a)(a/b)^{3/2}\le\langle\sigma v\rangle(T)\le\langle\sigma v\rangle(b)(b/a)^{3/2}$, valid because $\sigma\ge0$ (certified). The $\pB$ reduced mass is enclosed in $\mu c^2\in[859500,859650]$~keV. The ignition temperature is a bisection plus certified signs on the outer intervals; minima use an upper bound at the float minimiser and certified lower bounds elsewhere; $\max R$ over $(\Ti,x)$ boxes is a best-first branch and bound with outward rounding; $\theta^\ast$ is a bisection with a two-sided certificate (certified $\max R<1$ at $\theta_{\rm hi}$, and a point witness $R>1$ at $\theta_{\rm lo}$).
\paragraph{From the face $\theta_{\rm hi}$ to all $\theta\in[\theta_{\rm hi},1]$.} $\PB\propto\sqrt{\Te}\,\gamma(\Te/m_ec^2)$ is increasing in $\Te$ for $\Te\le500$~keV$<m_ec^2$: the $Z_{\rm eff}$ term is increasing, and the derivative of the electron--electron term $t(1+1.1t+t^2-1.25t^{2.5})$ is $1+2.2t+3t^2-4.375t^{2.5}>0$ on $[0,1]$, certified twice, by Arb subdivision and by the Lean lemma of Section~\ref{sec:lean}. The step ``positive derivative implies monotone'' is not formalised (Tier~\tC{}). Hence a certified $\max R<1$ at $\theta_{\rm hi}$ gives $R<1$ for every $\theta\in[\theta_{\rm hi},1]$. \textbf{We certify nothing for $\theta>1$} (there $\Te$ can leave the $500$~keV box toward $m_ec^2$ where the fit and the argument stop; Deviation~4).
\paragraph{Independent float code.} \texttt{e1\_float\_indep.py} is a separate float implementation (scipy \texttt{quad}, \texttt{brentq}, text constants); it lies inside every certified enclosure (Tier~\tX{}, \texttt{PBI-X-0001}).
\paragraph{Run history.} Runs~1 and~2 of the D-T stage and two $\pB$ stage runs were aborted (hang, too wide, a grid edge rounding below $50$~keV); run~3 failed G4 only, for the constant-hull reason. The final hashed \texttt{e1\_certify.py} was rerun end to end (\texttt{logs/certify.json}, \texttt{logs/certify\_final\_stdout.txt}) and reproduces every gate verdict and enclosure quoted here; earlier logs are kept as history. In \emph{decide} mode an early-exit bug could return ``undecided'' but not a false certificate; Wang at $x=1/6$ is certified by the explicit scan \texttt{e1\_wang\_mix.py}, because the stopping order of \texttt{theta\_threshold} (rtol test before the decide test) is still unfixed in the hashed code, so that the hashed code is exactly what ran.

So $\Pc<\PB$ everywhere on the box under both fits: no ideal-ignition window at $\Te=\Ti$ in this ideal $0$-D model (no synchrotron, no transport, Maxwellian, no mechanism maintaining $\Te<\Ti$), for $\Ti\in[50,500]$~keV and $x\in[0.05,0.5]$ (audit correction C5). The multiplier $m^\ast$ is a certified statement that needs no updated fit: $R$ is linear in $\langle\sigma v\rangle$, so any reactivity with $\langle\sigma v\rangle(T)\le2.349\times$ (Nevins--Swain)$(T)$ on $[50,500]$~keV gives $\Pc<\PB$ on the whole box at $\Te=\Ti$.

\paragraph{Caveat on comparing the two fits.} Wang is an \emph{integrated} S-factor; Nevins--Swain is a \emph{fitted} reactivity. The Nevins--Swain fit lies $2.4$--$3.5\%$ below the Maxwellian integral of the Nevins--Swain S-factor at $250$--$300$~keV, where $\theta^\ast$ is set, and $8\%$ below at $500$~keV, so part of the Wang/Nevins--Swain difference in $\theta^\ast$ is method mismatch, not new data. The certified ratio Wang/NS of the reactivity is $1.016,\ 1.000,\ 1.005,\ 1.016,\ 1.070,\ 1.154,\ 1.262$ at $50,100,150,200,300,400,500$~keV (point enclosures of relative radius below $10^{-4}$); at $500$~keV the Wang fit includes the new $4.7$~MeV resonance. The $\Te=\Ti$ exclusion does not depend on this (margin about $2.3$ either way). Wang et al.\ themselves state, with Rider's bremsstrahlung, that with $\Te=T$ the bremsstrahlung exceeds the fusion power over the whole temperature range (Tier~\tL{}, \texttt{PBI-L-0002}); this agrees with, and is not an independent check of, the Putvinski-bremsstrahlung result here.

\paragraph{Scope, with the audit's qualification (C4, C5).} Under these two fits and Putvinski's bremsstrahlung, a window needs $\Te/\Ti$ below about $0.42$--$0.45$ (box) or $0.375$--$0.402$ ($x=1/6$). \textbf{$\theta^\ast$ is not a property of $\pB$; it is a property of the chosen reactivity and bremsstrahlung fits.} Auditor sensitivities (float, Tier~\tX{}): uniform reactivity $\times1.1/1.2/1.32$ gives $\theta_c=0.477/0.524/0.579$ (the factor $1.32$ is an illustrative product of ``$+20\%$ cross section, $+10\%$ kinetic'', not a pinned value); Rider's bremsstrahlung moves $\theta_c$ by about $+1\%$ (to $0.434$ NS, $0.452$ Wang) and raises $\max R$ at $\theta=1$ to $0.438$ and $0.447$; $E=8.682$~MeV gives $0.4277$; removing Wang's $4.7$~MeV resonance moves $0.4451$ to $0.4450$. The maximum over the box for $\theta<1$ lies at $\Ti\approx235$--$259$~keV, $x\approx0.093$.

\subsection{Remark on Fig.~28 of Wurzel--Hsu}
The caption of Fig.~28 of~\cite{wh} reads ``$\PB$ always exceeds $\Pc$ when $\Te\gtrsim\Ti/3$'', with the parameterized Nevins--Swain reactivity and the $x=1/6$ mix. In the same model the certified threshold is $[0.3753,0.3828]$, not $1/3$; so for $\Te/\Ti$ between $1/3$ and about $0.375$ there are temperatures, near $\Ti\approx243$--$248$~keV, with $\Pc>\PB$. By float check at $\Te=\Ti/3$, $x=1/6$: $\max\Pc/\PB=1.113$ at $248.3$~keV (Nevins--Swain) and $1.161$ at $268.5$~keV (Wang) (\texttt{logs/third\_check.txt}; the auditor finds $1.1132$ and $1.1606$; Tier~\tX{}). This \textbf{sharpens a figure reading and does not contradict their conclusions}: the caption's ``$\gtrsim$'' makes it a rough reading of a plotted curve, and their statement that $\pB$ is not ignitable at $\Te=\Ti$ is what we also find.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Model, fits and box.} Every statement is about an idealised $0$-D power balance with the stated fits in $\Ti\in[50,500]$~keV, $x\in[0.05,0.5]$. Ideal ignition only: no conduction and no confinement time; it is not reactor breakeven, which is a different and weaker condition (Ochs--Fisch~\cite{of}; their breakeven and ignition-time figures come from a four-species optimisation not reproduced here).
\item \textbf{Not in the model:} synchrotron radiation (it would only make every window smaller); transport and conduction losses; any mechanism maintaining $\Te<\Ti$, that is the ion--electron collisional coupling a self-consistent $\Te$ would need; $\alpha$ heating of electrons; ash and impurities; non-Maxwellian or kinetic enhancement (bounded only through $m^\ast$); the uncertainties of the fits (Wang quote $\pm3$--$10\%$ on the $0.6$~MeV resonance; the Nevins--Swain errors are not propagated).
\item \textbf{The $\theta^\ast$ results do not say that $\Te<\Ti$ is achievable}, and are a property of the chosen fits (Section~\ref{sec:pb}).
\item \textbf{Coefficients not verified against primary papers} (Bosch--Hale 1992, Nevins--Swain 2000; Section~\ref{sec:fits}); the Putvinski bremsstrahlung equation was seen only through OCR; Tentori--Belloni and Sikora--Weller were not used as fits. ``Updated fit'' means Wang 2026 only.
\item \textbf{Tier~\tC{}, not Tier~\tA{}.} The enclosures are Arb ball arithmetic not replayed in Lean (the preregistration asked for Tier~\tA{}); the monotonicity step is not formalised. No human audit; AI audit only. The auditor's Wang-side numbers are float, not rigorous; its D-T minimisers of the gate model are float; the producer's Arb integrals were not re-run by the auditor.
\item \textbf{Lean:} two small facts only; recompilation and replay by the auditor; no statement lock; the auditor's leanchecker transcript is not shipped.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration} (\texttt{preregistration.json} was not edited): (1) sources: coefficients from the Wurzel--Hsu code (the listed fallback), so the kill criterion's wording ``pinned from primary sources'' is not met; (2) the updated fit is Wang 2026, not Sikora--Weller or Putvinski; (3) Tier~\tC{} instead of~\tA{} for the inequalities; (4) $\theta$ is certified on $[\theta_{\rm hi},1]$ only, not for $\theta>1$; (5) E1c is answered twice, as an exclusion under both fits and as the multiplier $m^\ast$; (6) the gate was written after a first float run and tests the code path that produced Table~III; (7) certification-method changes between runs, with run logs kept, and the early-exit bug and unfixed stopping order described in Section~\ref{sec:method}; (8) not done: the CVODE transient (optional) and a synchrotron or kinetic sensitivity.
\item \textbf{Repository guards.} \texttt{antigravity\_guard.py} (38 findings, none in E1 files) and \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py} exit~1 on pre-existing files elsewhere; \texttt{pytest tests/} was not run (E1 adds no tests) (\texttt{data/logs/guard\_*.txt}).
\item \textbf{Novelty.} No priority claim; to our knowledge nobody has published these enclosures, but this was not searched systematically.
\item Public data only; no inertial-confinement, burn or yield content. Not a statement about any device, plant, climate or economics.
\end{itemize}
