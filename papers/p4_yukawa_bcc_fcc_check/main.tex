\begin{abstract}
For unit-density point charges that repel through the screened Coulomb (Yukawa) potential $e^{-\kappa r}/r$, Luo and Wei prove that the
body-centred cubic (BCC) lattice has the lowest energy among all three-dimensional lattices for $\kappa<\ks$, the face-centred cubic (FCC)
lattice for $\kappa>\ks$, and that $\ks\approx 1.7179218684699$. Their proof leans on finite, computer-assisted inequalities. We checked
part of them independently. \textbf{We found no discrepancy; every printed number and inequality that we checked holds.}
Tier split.
\tA{} (Lean~4.34.1, pinned Mathlib; axioms within $\{$\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}$\}$; \texttt{leanchecker}
replay rc~0 on \lean{Basic}, \lean{Shells}, \lean{Chains}, \lean{LemmaA5}, \lean{Lemma61}, run by the producer and again by the independent AI audit on privately rebuilt,
byte-identical oleans; all 138 declarations of the five modules print only these axioms; no human audit; no statement lock):
\emph{seventeen Lean theorems} (ten substantive; seven are one-line rational facts or bridge lemmas, and one of them,
\lean{lemma61\_prop41\_bracket}, is a for-all-$x$ inequality, not a finite statement): the FCC shell multiplicities $N_1,\dots,N_{12}$ and the BCC multiplicities $M_\ell$ at the
17 nonzero indices $\ell\le48$, proved equal to the count over all of $\mathbb Z^3$; Lemma~A.5 (A.6), (A.7) and (A.8) as real-number statements over \lean{Real.pi}
and \lean{Real.exp}; the constants of the proof of Lemma~6.1; the tail rational of the proof of Lemma~A.6; and the rational step of Lemma~A.1.
Several Lean statements are weaker than the printed ones (Table~\ref{tab:weaker}).
\tL{}: the paper's own statements, taken as stated.
\tC{}: Arb ball arithmetic (Lemma~A.6 for $G=2(E_B-E_F)$ at $\kappa\in\{1.717921868469903,\,1.717921868469904\}$ and at neighbouring controls;
the Lemma~A.5 minimum slacks $7.52\times10^{-4}$ and $7.65\times10^{-5}$; Lemmas~A.1 and A.3) and \emph{three independent reproductions of $\ks$}
(Ewald sums in Arb at 320 bits; a direct real-space Arb sum; mpmath quadrature of the paper's integral equation (1.4) at 40 digits): (E) and (Q) agree to all 25 printed digits and the
interval of (D), of width $4\times10^{-24}$, contains both. The audit added a fourth, structurally different value (classical Ewald split with a free splitting parameter, mpmath, 70 digits,
independent of that parameter), $\ks=1.71792186846990386425087416660628171879468538$, and an independent ball-arithmetic run (python-flint 0.9.0) that reproduces both signs of Lemma~A.6.
(Our IEEE-double direct sum reaches 11 digits; double precision cannot resolve the $10^{-15}$-wide enclosure (A.9), since rounding at $10^{-16}$ in sums of order one moves $\ks$ by about $10^{-12}$.)
\textbf{Not done:} the assembly of Lemma~6.1 (theta functions as lattice sums, Poisson summation for $D(1)=0$, $N_n<30n$ for all $n$);
Lemma~A.4 (5\,828 rectangles: the partition data is not public and arXiv lists no ancillary files; we intend to ask the authors for it) and Lemma~A.2; and Lemma~A.6 in Lean (the pinned Mathlib has no \lean{erfc}).

\section{A step-by-step account for non-specialists}\label{sec:human}
Each step ends with the Lean statement that corresponds to it, when there is one. Lean statements are transcribed in ASCII; the sources are in
\texttt{data/lean/}.

\paragraph{Step 1: the question.}\tL{} Put point charges at the points of a lattice (a regular 3D grid generated by three vectors) with one point per unit volume. If
the charges repel as $1/r$ (Coulomb), the lowest-energy lattice is BCC: a cube with a point at each corner and one at the centre. If the repulsion is
\emph{screened}, $e^{-\kappa r}/r$ (Yukawa), with screening $\kappa>0$, strong screening makes the interaction short-ranged, and nearest-neighbour geometry wins: FCC, a
cube with points at the corners and face centres, packs the nearest neighbours better. The energy per particle is $E_\kappa(L)=\tfrac12\sum_{v\in L\setminus\{0\}}e^{-\kappa|v|}/|v|$.
Luo and Wei prove that among \emph{all} lattices of unit covolume the minimiser is BCC for $\kappa<\ks$ and FCC for $\kappa>\ks$.

\paragraph{Step 2: what a ``certified threshold'' means.}\tL{} The threshold $\ks$ is the unique $\kappa$ with $E_\kappa(\LB)=E_\kappa(\LF)$. A
\emph{certified} enclosure of $\ks$ means this: evaluate $G(\kappa)=2(E_\kappa(\LB)-E_\kappa(\LF))$ at two rational values $\kappa_1<\kappa_2$ and show rigorously that
$G(\kappa_1)<0<G(\kappa_2)$. Because $G$ has a single sign change, $\ks\in(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)$. Lemma~A.6 does this with
$\kappa_1=1717921868469903/10^{15}$ and $\kappa_2=1717921868469904/10^{15}$, claiming $G(\kappa_1)<-10^{-19}$ and $G(\kappa_2)>10^{-20}$.
The numbers are small because $G'(\ks)\approx1.57\times10^{-4}$: one unit in the fifteenth decimal moves $G$ by about $1.6\times10^{-19}$. \tC{}

\paragraph{Step 3: shell multiplicities.}\tL{} The FCC lattice $\LF$ and its dual BCC lattice $\LB=\LF^*$ have squared lengths $2^{1/3}n$ (with $N_n$ vectors) and
$2^{-4/3}\ell$ (with $M_\ell$ vectors). Concretely $N_n=\#\{m\in\mathbb Z^3:|m|^2=2n\}$ and $M_\ell=\#\{m\in\mathbb Z^3:|m|^2=\ell,\ m_1\equiv m_2\equiv m_3 \bmod 2\}$.
Everything below uses the printed tables. We recounted them in the kernel, over a finite box, \emph{and} proved that the box count equals the count over all of $\mathbb Z^3$:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem Nc_eq_ncard (n : N) (hn : 2*n < 36) :
    Nc n = Set.ncard {m : Z x Z x Z | normSq m = 2*n}
theorem Mc_eq_ncard (l : N) (hl : l < 49) :
    Mc l = Set.ncard {m : Z x Z x Z | normSq m = l /\ sameParity m}
theorem fcc_multiplicities :
    (List.range 12).map (fun i => Nc (i+1)) = [12,6,24,12,24,8,48,6,36,24,24,24]
theorem bcc_multiplicities :  -- 17 nonzero M_l, l <= 48
    filter (Mc l /= 0) [1..48] = [3,4,8,11,12,16,19,20,24,27,32,35,36,40,43,44,48] /\
    map Mc [3,4,8,...,48] = [8,6,12,24,8,6,24,24,24,32,12,48,30,24,24,24,8]
\end{lstlisting}
\tA{} (\texttt{YBC-A-0001}, \texttt{YBC-A-0002}). The box sizes (radius 5 and 6) are justified by the first lemma: any vector of squared length below $(R+1)^2$ has all coordinates in $[-R,R]$.

\paragraph{Step 4: a finite inequality becomes a statement about real numbers.}\tL{} Lemma~A.5 compares two sums of exponentials on a grid of $1000+2000$ cells. Here is (A.7):
for every integer $0\le j<2000$, $\sum_{\ell\le48}M_\ell e^{-\pi 2^{-4/3}\ell\,(11/10+(j+1)/2000)}-\sum_{n\le12}N_n e^{-\pi2^{1/3}n\,(11/10+j/2000)}-10^{-18}>10^{-4}$.
In Lean, over the reals with \lean{Real.pi}, \lean{Real.exp}, $a_2=2^{1/3}$ and $b_2=2^{-4/3}$:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem lemmaA5_A7 (j : N) (hj : j < 2000) :
  sum l in Icc 1 48, (Mc l : R) * exp (-(pi*b2) * l * (11/10 + (j+1)/2000))
  - sum n in Icc 1 12, (Nc n : R) * exp (-(pi*a2) * n * (11/10 + j/2000))
  - 1/10^18 > 1/10000
\end{lstlisting}
\tA{} (\texttt{YBC-A-0004}); (A.6) is \lean{lemmaA5\_A6} for $j<1000$ with margin $3/1000$ (\texttt{YBC-A-0003}), and (A.8) is
\lean{8 * exp((pi*a2 - 3*(pi*b2)) * (21/10)) > 25/2} (\lean{lemmaA5\_A8}, \texttt{YBC-A-0005}).

\paragraph{Step 5: how the kernel checks 3\,000 exponential inequalities.}\tA{} The kernel cannot evaluate $e^x$, so we reduce each cell to rational arithmetic. (i) $\pi$ is
bracketed by Mathlib's \lean{Real.pi\_gt\_d20} and \lean{pi\_lt\_d20}; $2^{1/3}$ and $2^{-4/3}$ are bracketed through their cubes. (ii) For a rational $z$, $e^z=(e^{z/8})^8$ and
\lean{Real.exp\_bound} gives a rational enclosure from a Taylor polynomial. (iii) The values $e^{-\pi a_2(1+k/10^4)}$ and three similar sequences are supplied as lists of
dyadic rationals (denominator $2^{52}$) by an \emph{untrusted} script; the kernel checks that each entry follows the previous one within a certified enclosure
(\lean{chainLo}, \lean{chainHi}), and the chain is lifted by induction to the real sequence. Because shell sums are monotone in the exponential, one table entry per cell suffices. (iv) Each of the 3\,000 cell inequalities is a rational
comparison decided by \lean{decide +kernel}, in blocks of 100 cells. An emitter script mirrors every kernel check in exact \lean{Fraction} arithmetic before Lean runs; its slacks, $7.5214\times10^{-4}$ for (A.6) and $7.6459\times10^{-5}$ for (A.7), match the Arb values. The untrusted tables
cannot make a false statement pass: a tampered entry fails (Section~\ref{sec:controls}).

\paragraph{Step 6: the proof of Lemma~6.1, as far as it is finite.}\tL{} Lemma~6.1 says $D(t)=\Theta(t,\LB)-\Theta(t,\LF)$ satisfies $D(1)=0$ and $D(t)>0$ for $t>1$, where
$\Theta(t,L)=\sum_{v\in L}e^{-\pi t|v|^2}$. The printed proof uses Poisson summation (for $D(1)=0$, since $\LB=\LF^*$), then, for $t\ge1$, bounds the shells beyond $n=13$ and $\ell=49$ using $N_n<30n$, $M_\ell<30\ell$ and a few
numerical constants. Those constants are \tA{} (\texttt{YBC-A-0006}), for example
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem lemma61_exp_fcc : exp (-(pi*a2)) < 1/49
theorem lemma61_exp_bcc : exp (-(pi*b2)) < 3/10
theorem lemma61_tail_fcc (K : N) :
  30 * sum k in range K, (13+k) * (1/49)^(13+k) < 1/10^18
\end{lstlisting}
The statement for \emph{every partial sum} is what we proved; passing to the infinite series is not written in Lean. The assembly is Section~\ref{sec:open}.

\paragraph{Step 7: Lemma~A.6 and why it is in Ball arithmetic, not Lean.}\tC{} $E_\kappa$ is not a finite sum. The paper (eqs.\ 2.3, 2.4) rewrites it by an Ewald split into rapidly convergent sums over $\LF$ and $\LB$ (truncated at $n\le12$, $\ell\le48$) whose terms involve an incomplete integral $I(q,c)=\int_1^\infty t^{-1/2}e^{-\pi qt-c/t}dt$, a complementary error function in closed form. The pinned Mathlib has no \lean{erfc}, hence no certified enclosure for $I$ in Lean without building that theory. We therefore verify Lemma~A.6 in Arb (Section~\ref{sec:kappa}). The rational tail, $60(1/49)^{13}/(1-1/49)+60(3/10)^{49}/(7/10)<7\times10^{-21}$ (exact value $6.5267\times10^{-21}$), is \tA{} (\lean{lemmaA6\_tail}, \texttt{YBC-A-0007}); so is the rational step $55995/10^6<7/125$ of Lemma~A.1 (\lean{lemmaA1\_rational}).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Partial.} See Section~\ref{sec:open}. We do not claim that Theorem~1.1, 5.1 or 6.1 is verified. Tier~A certifies Lean statements about the stated model quantities (unit-covolume shell sums with \lean{Real.pi}, \lean{Real.exp}), nothing more.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration.} (1) A.6 is Tier~C for a reason other than the kill text gives (no \lean{erfc}). (2) Lemma~6.1 is not kernel-checked, so even the fallback ``kernel-check Lemma~6.1 only'' was not fully met. (3) The two omitted-tail series are bounded on every partial sum; the monotone limit is not in Lean. (4) The producer's IEEE-double direct-sum control reaches 11 digits (a float64 Ewald reaches about 12), too few for the 13-digit printed value, so the 13-digit float control became mpmath at 40 digits. (5) M3c was blocked, so the tampered-rectangle control is N/A.
\item \textbf{Compute and process.} The Lean runs were on core~6, which another agent's process also used. Two earlier monolithic compiles of \lean{LemmaA5} were killed with signal~9, unattributed, probably memory pressure from about four 5.7~GB Lean processes of other lanes; the file was then split into four modules with 100-cell blocks. The emitter was re-run once after the oleans were built, to regenerate \lean{Lemma61} only (it is deterministic); \texttt{Statements.lean} prints the olean types of the five main theorems and they match the sources. \texttt{m3\_arb.py} was re-annotated after its first run and re-run with identical key numbers.
\item \textbf{Verifier status.} The producer's \texttt{leanchecker} replay is not independent; the audit's is (private olean directory, the producer's shared olean directory removed from the library path, oleans byte-identical to the producer's, rc~0 on all five modules). The audit is an AI audit; no human has audited the paper or the Lean statements, and no statement lock was registered.
\item \textbf{Repository guards fail, for pre-existing reasons in other lanes' files.} \texttt{antigravity\_guard.py} rc~1 (103 findings, hallucinated-import findings in other lanes); \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py} rc~1 (tests with no assertions in other modules); \texttt{pytest tests/} rc~2 (28 collection errors; no test ran). None lies in this lane's files; this lane added no tests. We report the outputs as they came (\texttt{data/logs/guard\_*.txt}, \texttt{pytest\_tests.txt}) and do not call the repository gate passed.
\item Interpreter and shell lapses of the producer: the Lean wrapper and guards ran under system \texttt{python3} without \texttt{-I}; a few wait loops were used in background shells. Neither touched git.
\item \textbf{The paper text is not redistributed.} The 27-page text extraction used for reading is not included here, since we did not establish its licence; the paper is cited.
\end{itemize}
