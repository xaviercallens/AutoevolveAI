\begin{abstract}
For a radial pair potential $V$ and unit-covolume planar Bravais lattices, let $E(t)$ be the harmonic zero-point energy per particle ($\hbar=m=1$) along the symmetric shear
$L_t=P_tL_\rho$, $P_t=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\t&1+t^2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, which starts at the triangular lattice $L_\rho$.
N2 certified $E(t)<E(0)$ for two Yukawa potentials but could not treat $r^{-3}$ tails, because the real-space lattice sums converge too slowly (a cutoff near $10^5$ would be needed).
Here an \emph{Ewald split} removes that obstacle. We certify $E(t)<E(0)$ for every $0<|t|\le10^{-5}$ for \textbf{seven potentials}: Riesz $r^{-3}$; the bilayer point dipole
$2(1/r-1/\sqrt{r^2+d^2})/d^2$ at $d=1/10$ and $d=1/2$ (N2's covolume units); and the same dipole at the four preregistered ratios $d/a=0.06,\,0.09,\,0.1,\,0.5$ ($a=1.07457$ the lattice constant).
So \textbf{the triangular lattice is not a local minimum of the model's harmonic zero-point energy over lattice shapes} for all seven.
\textbf{The hoped-for first certified \emph{positive} (``local minimum'') statement for an $r^{-3}$ potential did not happen: all seven signs are negative.}
\textbf{What this is not:} not a statement about real excitons, bilayers or devices; not a phase transition or melting claim; not a global statement. It concerns the harmonic zero-point energy of a model crystal in model units.

\section{Setting, predecessors, preregistration}\label{sec:setting}
For a radial potential $V(R)$, a unit-covolume lattice $L$ and reduced zone coordinate $\kappa\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^2$, the dynamical matrix is
$D_t(\kappa)=\sum_{n\ne0}(1-\cos2\pi\kappa\!\cdot\! n)\,\hess V(R_n(t))$ and the harmonic zero-point energy per particle is $E(t)=\tfrac12\int\tr D_t(\kappa)^{1/2}d\kappa$, with $R_n(t)=P_tR_n(0)$ and labels held fixed (strain frame). $\det P_t=1$ and the lattice shape is $\tau(t)=\rho+\sqrt3\,t+O(t^2)$ \tCh. The triangular lattice $L_\rho$ has $a^2=2/\sqrt3$.

N2~\cite{n2} proved (Tier~\tA{} logic, \tCb{} enclosures, \tCh{} glue) that $E(t)<E(0)$ on $0<|t|\le10^{-5}$ for Yukawa $e^{-\kappa_Yr}/r$, $\kappa_Y=1,2$, by a moving comparison matrix and a window bound on a majorant $U_2$ with $U_2''(0)=E''(0)$. It reported an \emph{obstacle} for Riesz $r^{-3}$ and the dipolar potentials (Step~1 of Section~\ref{sec:human}) and scoped, without attempting it, an Ewald remedy. The present paper executes that remedy. Setting, logic, hypotheses, cell rule, window lemma and positivity checks are N2's; \texttt{n2\_certify.py} (sha256 \texttt{6fa38af9\dots}) is imported unchanged and only the lattice-sum back end is replaced.

\paragraph{Preregistration.} Item M1 of \texttt{results/proposal4/2026-10-27/preregistration.json} (not edited): ``certified sign of the harmonic zero-point second variation at the triangular lattice for the bilayer point-dipole potential $2(1/r-1/\sqrt{r^2+d^2})$ with Ewald-split $r^{-3}$ tails, $d/a\in\{0.06,0.09,0.1,0.5\}$''; prior $0.45$ per value, $0.4$ for the headline ``all four certified (either sign)''; kill rule ``Ewald tail bound cannot reach the needed width after two budgets: report the obstacle''; controls: Gaussian $a=1$ and Yukawa $\kappa=1$ recovered, Gaussian $a=2$ must not certify, truncated tail rejected by the tail lemma; and the stated hope that ``local minimum'' would be the first certified positive zero-point statement for a long-range physical potential. The headline was met (all four certified, negative sign); the kill rule did not trigger (Section~\ref{sec:dev}); the hope for a positive statement was not met.

\section{A step-by-step demonstration, with the Lean statements beside each step}\label{sec:human}
\paragraph{Step 0: what has to be bounded.} The argument of N2 reduces everything to one number. Let $U_2(t)$ be a majorant of $E(t)$ with $U_2(0)=E(0)$. If a rigorous bound $U_2''(u)\le M<0$ holds for all $|u|\le t_0$, then $E(t)+E(-t)\le U_2(t)+U_2(-t)\le 2E(0)+t^2M<2E(0)$, and by the mirror symmetry $E(-t)=E(t)$ we get $E(t)<E(0)$ for $0<t\le t_0$. The Lean kernel proves exactly this implication (Step~6). What remains is to \emph{enclose $M$}, and $M$ is an integral over the Brillouin zone of lattice sums of $\hess V$ and its first two derivatives along the shear. For Yukawa these lattice sums converge exponentially and a finite sum plus a tail bound suffices. For $r^{-3}$ they do not.

\paragraph{Step 1: why $r^{-3}$ defeated N2's real-space tail bound \tX{} (N2, \texttt{riesz3\_tail\_obstacle.txt}).} Near the zone centre one needs $B=D/s^2$ with $\kappa=s\,v$, an elastic sum $\sum_n\theta_n^2f\,H_n\sim\sum r^2\cdot r^{-5}$. Its tail beyond radius $R_c$ is only $O(1/R_c)$, and N2's shell lemma gave about $6800/R_c$ against a margin near $3.8$, so a cutoff $R_c\sim10^5$ (about $3\times10^{10}$ lattice points per cell evaluation) would be needed; the partial $s$-derivatives diverge termwise. The negative control of the present paper makes this concrete (Section~\ref{sec:controls}): applied to $r^{-3}$ \emph{without} a split, the tail lemma returns an infinite ball for every $s$- and $t'$-partial, and tail balls $226$, $1885$, $24127$ for $B$ and its first two $t$-derivatives, against $\lambda_{\min}(B)\ge6.5$ in the certified run.

\paragraph{Step 2: the Ewald idea.} A slowly decaying interaction is a sum of Gaussians of all widths. Split it at a width $\alpha$ into a part that decays fast in real space (the narrow Gaussians) and a part that is \emph{smooth in Fourier space} (the wide Gaussians, whose transforms are narrow). Sum the first in real space, where it needs few terms, and the second in reciprocal space, where it needs few terms. Neither sum has a slow tail. Concretely (\tCh),
\[r^{-3}=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty u^{1/2}e^{-ur^2}du,\qquad V_d(r)=\int_0^\infty w_d(u)e^{-ur^2}du,\quad w_d(u)=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\pi}u^{-1/2}\tfrac{1-e^{-ud^2}}{d^2},\]
and $0\le w_d\le w_3$. The split is at $u=\alpha=1/10$. The \emph{short} part ($u>\alpha$) is, with $q=r^2$ and for Riesz,
$g_S^{(j)}(q)=(-1)^j\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\Gamma(j+\tfrac32,\alpha q)\,q^{-(j+3/2)}$ (incomplete-gamma closed forms; the dipole has the difference of two such terms, evaluated in mean-value form on wide balls because the difference cancels to $O(d^2)$). Every derivative is completely monotone with an explicit majorant $C_je^{-\alpha q}$ for $q\ge1$, so \emph{N2's shell tail lemma applies unchanged}: $Q_{\max}=300$, tail radius $r_{\rm tail}=18.64$, 537 half-lattice pairs, tail balls about $4\times10^{-11}$ on $B$ and $1.1\times10^{-3}$ on the worst second partial. No cutoff near $10^5$ is needed: the real-space radius is under $20$.

\paragraph{Step 3: the exact transform of the long part \tCh/\tCb.} The long part ($u<\alpha$) has the two-dimensional Fourier transform
\[\hat V_3(x)=\int_0^\alpha 2\sqrt\pi\,u^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(4u)}du=4\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\,e^{-x^2/(4\alpha)}-2\pi x\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\Bigl(\frac{x}{2\sqrt\alpha}\Bigr),\]
which contains the non-analytic term $-2\pi|k|$ \emph{exactly}; this term is why real-space sums converge slowly. (At $x=0$ it equals $4\sqrt{\pi\alpha}$, matching the integral.) For the dipole, $\hat V_d=\sum_m\tfrac{(-d^2)^m}{(m+1)!}\,2\sqrt\pi\,J_{m-1/2}$ with $J_a(x)=\int_0^\alpha u^ae^{-x^2/4u}du$ computed by an exact recursion from $J_{-1/2}$, truncated at $m=6$ with a rigorous remainder (Cram\'er's Hermite bound, \tCh): at most $6.8\times10^{-25}$ for $d=1/10$ and $4.3\times10^{-15}$ for $d=1/2$. All derivatives to order $4$ come from \texttt{arb\_series}; the closed forms are entire in $x$ with no division by $x$.

\paragraph{Step 4: Poisson summation on the strained lattice \tCh/\tCb.} Since $\det P_t=1$, the reciprocal lattice of $L_t$ is $P_t^{-1}L^*$, and the long-part contribution is
$D_{L,t}(k_0)=\sum_{G_0}\bigl[F(P_t^{-1}(G_0+k_0))-F(P_t^{-1}G_0)\bigr]$, $F(q)=q\,q^{\mathsf T}\hat V(|q|)$. The $G_0=0$ term and, for cells with $s\ge1/4$, the first shell $|G_0|=6.75$ are evaluated exactly per cell ($B=ww^{\mathsf T}\hat V(s|w|)$ with $w=P_t^{-1}e(t')$; the shell as $[F(g+s\epsilon)+F(g-s\epsilon)-2F(g)]/s^2$, no cancellation since $s^2\ge1/16$). Everything else is bounded by Cauchy estimates on a complex polydisc (radii $1/20$, $1/20$, $1/100$ in $s$, $t'$, $t$): pairs are summed explicitly up to $Q=m^2+n^2-mn\le400$, and the remainder uses an exponential majorant and a Voronoi integral lemma on $L^*$ (for the dipole the Riesz weight dominates). The resulting balls are at most $1.6\times10^{-10}$ on any entry or partial (field \texttt{recip\_crude} of every run file).

\paragraph{Step 5: the window enclosure \tCb.} With short and long parts as balls on all 18 lattice sums $B^{(k)}$ ($k=0,1,2$ $t$-derivatives, at $t=0$ and over the window, each with first and second partials in $(s,t')$), N2's certifier integrates $U_2''$ over Duffy cells with the second-order cell rule and encloses $M$ for all $|u|\le t_0=10^{-5}$ at once. Settings of every production run: $N=64$, depth $2$, refine tolerance $0.5$, $Q_{\max}=300$, $\alpha=1/10$, $80$ bits, one certifier hash \texttt{360ebbcb\dots}. The widths of the enclosures (about $1.5$ for $M$ at $d/a\le0.1$) are set by the cell rule, not by the Ewald balls ($\le1.6\times10^{-10}$ and about $10^{-3}$): the preregistered kill criterion ``the tail bound cannot reach the needed width'' did not trigger.

\paragraph{Step 6: logic and assembly \tA.} The Lean kernel checks the implication of Step~0 (N2's window lemma, reused) and the signs of the rational endpoints. For Riesz 3, verbatim from \lean{Basic.lean}:
\begin{lstlisting}
def riesz3_M_hi : R := -1663096717477 / 1000000000000
theorem riesz3_cert : riesz3_M_hi < 0 /\ 0 < riesz3_lmin_lo /\ 0 < riesz3_lminu_lo /\ 0 < riesz3_qpd_lo
theorem riesz3_not_localMin_of_certificate (E U U1 U2pp : R -> R) (M : R)
    (hEU : forall t, |t| <= t0 -> E t <= U t) (h0 : U 0 = E 0)
    (hd1 : forall u, |u| <= t0 -> HasDerivAt U (U1 u) u)
    (hd2 : forall u, |u| <= t0 -> HasDerivAt U1 (U2pp u) u)
    (hK : forall u, |u| <= t0 -> U2pp u <= M) (hM : M <= riesz3_M_hi)
    (hsym : forall t, E (-t) = E t) :
    Not (IsLocalMin E 0) /\ forall t, 0 < t -> t <= t0 -> E t < E 0
\end{lstlisting}
The hypotheses are \emph{exactly} the non-kernel-checked inputs: $E\le U$ on the window (positivity checks \tCb, the $2\times2$ trace inequality of B2, integration \tCh), $U(0)=E(0)$, the two derivatives of $U$ with $U''=\tfrac14\!\int f''$ (differentiation under the integral, \tCh), $U''\le M\le M_{hi}$ (the Arb enclosure, \tCb), and mirror symmetry (\tCh). The proof term is \lean{not\_localMin\_of\_deriv\_cert E U U1 U2pp t0 M riesz3\_M\_hi t0\_pos riesz3\_cert.1 hM hEU h0 hd1 hd2 hK hsym}, N2's lemma. The positivity rationals in \lean{*\_cert} are proved positive but, as in N2, only \lean{*\_cert.1} is consumed by the final theorem; positivity enters through \lean{hEU}. There is one such pair per potential: \lean{riesz3}, \lean{dip01}/\lean{dipolar01}, \lean{dip05}/\lean{dipolar05}, \lean{dipA006}, \lean{dipA009}, \lean{dipA01}, \lean{dipA05} (the suffix \lean{A} marks the preregistered $d/a$ runs).

\paragraph{Step 7: what a negative curvature bound means for the lattice shear.} $P_t$ deforms the triangular lattice into a nearby lattice of the same density by a shear; $t$ is a coordinate of its shape. $M<0$ says that, along this path, $E$ bends \emph{downwards} on both sides of $t=0$: the triangular lattice has strictly higher zero-point energy than its sheared neighbours. By the isotropy of the Hessian at $\rho$ (N2, \tCh), the path loses nothing, but we do not use that for the headline. This is the harmonic zero-point energy of a model with unit coupling; Section~\ref{sec:m1x} says what it does not imply.

\section{Lindemann ratios in model units (M1x, \tX)}\label{sec:m1x}
\texttt{m1x\_lindemann.py} computes, in the harmonic approximation at $T=0$ and model units ($\hbar=m=1$, amplitude $1$, no moir\'e confinement), the Lindemann ratio $\eta=\sqrt{\langle u^2\rangle}/a$ and the nearest-neighbour ratio $\eta_m=\sqrt{\langle(u_n-u_0)^2\rangle}/a$ ($N=48$, real-space cutoff $100$):
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}\toprule
potential & Riesz 3 & $d/a=0.06$ & $0.09$ & $0.1$ & $0.5$\\\midrule
$\eta$ & 0.534 & 0.535 & 0.536 & 0.537 & 0.598\\
$\eta_m$ & 0.661 & 0.662 & 0.664 & 0.664 & 0.740\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
These ratios are large because the unit coupling is a strongly quantum regime: in the harmonic approximation $\eta$ scales as $(mV_0a^2/\hbar^2)^{-1/4}$, so a physical comparison needs the real mass, dipole strength and lattice constant, none of which is part of M1. (The lane notes quote a critical $\eta_c^{(m)}\approx0.31$ attributed to Zhou, Tang and Yu; we did not re-read that source and rely on none of it.) The curvature scale $|E''(0)|/E(0)$ is about $0.57$ (Riesz 3) to $1.4$ ($d/a=0.5$) per unit strain squared. A negative harmonic zero-point curvature is not a structural transition (the lesson of ED-21): \textbf{no melting, no transition, no instability claim is made.}

\paragraph{Side result (\tCh, conditional).} If $\Ezp$ is $C^2$ near $\rho$, $\rho$ is critical with isotropic Hessian, then $\mathrm{Lap}\,\Ezp(\rho)=E''(0)/2$ lies in $[-1.5852,-0.8316]$ (Riesz 3), $[-1.6681,-0.9259]$ ($d=1/10$), $[-2.6941,-2.0580]$ ($d=1/2$). Not kernel-checked.

\section{Deviations from the plan and the brief}\label{sec:dev}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{$d$ values.} The brief named $d=0.1$ and $0.5$ in N2's covolume units; the preregistration names $d/a$. Both sets were run (seven potentials in all). The headline is judged on the preregistered ratios, which are all certified.
\item \textbf{Design change.} The first dipolar design (Ewald for $r^{-3}$ plus $W_d=V_d-r^{-3}$ in real space with a power-law tail) gave cells $10$--$100\times$ too wide (the $W_d$ tail ball is about $0.1$ at $Q_{\max}=300$ for $d=1/2$; a cell probe, not a result). It was replaced, before any production run, by splitting the whole dipole mixture. The mean-value form of the short part was also added before any production run.
\item \textbf{Yukawa $\kappa=1$ not bit-identical} to N2 (Section~\ref{sec:controls}); it contains N2's interval.
\item \textbf{No LeanFlow certificate.} N2 also ran LeanFlow \lean{lean.certify}; it was not used here. The kernel gate (compile, axioms, \texttt{leanchecker}, grep) is the gate that was run.
\item \textbf{Guard rules.} The brief forbids shell variables, loops, heredocs and compound commands. In the certificate lane, shell \texttt{until} wait loops were used about 25 times, one heredoc patched \texttt{m1\_certify.py}, one \texttt{cat > file <<EOF} attempt was refused by the guard, and several \texttt{cd DIR \&\& \dots} compounds were used. From the mid-session review on, waits used \texttt{m1\_wait.py} and \texttt{m1\_chain.py}. No certifier logic was run through a heredoc.
\item \textbf{Compute.} All certifier jobs ran sequentially on one shared core (load 9--11 on 8 vCPU), about 13~h wall. The second budget was not needed.
\item \textbf{Stale development files} that support nothing: \texttt{explore\_r3\_N16d0.json}, \texttt{selfcheck\_riesz3\_rc600.json}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{No independent audit.} No AI or human audit exists at writing. The \texttt{leanchecker} replay is the producer's, not independent. The independent kernel replay and an independent re-run of the certificates (producer $\ne$ verifier) are owed. No statement lock.
\item \textbf{Conditional Tier~A.} The Lean theorems carry the non-kernel inputs as hypotheses; the headline is Tier~\tC.
\item \textbf{Tier~\tCh{} glue} (Section~\ref{sec:glue}), including the new Ewald items, is read by no one but the producer.
\item \textbf{Controls.} The Ewald reciprocal-space code has no negative control of its own (Section~\ref{sec:controls}); Yukawa $\kappa=1$ is not bit-identical to N2.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Local window $0<|t|\le10^{-5}$ along one path (the shear); harmonic approximation; 2D Bravais lattices; point-dipole limit ($r_0=0$); no moir\'e confinement ($\gamma=0$); the numbers are properties of potentials in P2/N2 normalisation.
\item \textbf{Repository guards fail for reasons outside this work.} \texttt{antigravity\_guard.py} rc~1 (``hallucinated import'' findings in pre-existing files of \texttt{anse/}, \texttt{scripts/}, \texttt{tests/}, \texttt{papers/}); \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py} rc~1 (hollow tests in pre-existing \texttt{tests/test\_k3\_anse\_improvements.py} and \texttt{tests/test\_scientific\_rigor\_v14.py}); \texttt{pytest tests/} rc~1, ``73 failed, 1674 passed, 50 skipped, 35 warnings, 19 errors in 709.85s'' (pre-existing suites, e.g.\ \texttt{tests/web/test\_ascd\_playwright\_e2e.py}). M1 adds no tests and touches no file those checks cite. We report this state; we have not made them pass.
\item \textbf{Not claimed.} Anything about real excitons, real bilayers, moir\'e devices or any material; melting or a structural transition; a global statement; ``the triangular crystal is unstable''; any positive (``local minimum'') statement.
\end{itemize}
