\begin{abstract}
We re-check, with exhaustive search and machine-checkable certificates, that \textbf{every simple cubic bipartite graph on at most $68$ vertices contains a cycle of length $4$, $8$ or $16$.}
Equivalently, no connected linear $v_3$-configuration with $v\le34$ points avoids Berge $4$- and $8$-cycles. We follow the growth search of Tranquilli (arXiv:2608.02675), whose certified range stops at $58$ vertices, in two root modes:
\emph{triangle-rooted} (his mode; for orders $62$--$68$ it needs the literature premise $n(3,10)=70$, read by us only through the Exoo--Jajcay cage survey) and \emph{arbitrary-root} (no triangle assumed, no cage premise).
Two oracles for the forbidden-cycle test give identical decision-transcript hashes at every cap. Route~2 caps $29$--$34$ (orders $58$--$68$) and route~1 caps $30$--$35$ close with $0$ completions.
Certificate streams are accepted by the producer's two checkers and by an independent auditor's own C checker; the cap-$34$ arbitrary-root stream was regenerated by the auditor and has the same sha256 (\texttt{0112b808\dots fad5}).
Controls reproduce Tranquilli's Table~2 at cap $29$, the canonical class counts $1,1,3,10,31,229,2036$ for $v=7..13$ (equal to the nauty \texttt{genbg} sets), and positive and negative filter-off controls.
\textbf{This is not a new bound.} Ripatti (BAIKAL/MOTOR 2017 abstract) claims, without a certificate, that the smallest cubic graphs without cycles of length $4,8,16$ have $78$ vertices, which covers every cubic graph on at most $76$ vertices, and Ducoffe--Dumitru (arXiv:2609.28594) cover bipartite orders up to $66$ by SAT without a proof log.
We know of no earlier witness-checked computation for these orders; the contribution is a recomputation with certificates and an independent audit.
Tier~\tC{} (exhaustive search plus witness checkers, no kernel check); the coverage lemma is argued in prose and not formalised.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small\textbf{What this is NOT.} It is \textbf{not} a new bound (see Section~\ref{sec:prior}), \textbf{not} progress on the Erd\H{o}s--Gy\'arf\'as conjecture, \textbf{not} a statement about non-bipartite, non-cubic or larger graphs, and \textbf{not} formalised in Lean or any proof assistant. Independent here means independent \emph{code}; the method (Tranquilli's search) is shared by producer and auditor.}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and equivalent forms}\label{sec:setting}
\paragraph{Statement.} Every simple cubic bipartite graph on at most $68$ vertices contains a cycle of length $4$, $8$ or $16$. A bipartite cubic graph has two sides of equal size $v$, so order $\le68$ means $v\le34$. A graph with no $4$-cycle is the Levi (point--block incidence) graph of a linear $v_3$-configuration (Tranquilli Prop.~3, Lemma~5): $v$ points and $v$ blocks of size $3$, any two points on at most one block. A Levi $8$-cycle is a Berge $4$-cycle and a Levi $16$-cycle is a Berge $8$-cycle (Lemma~6). Hence the statement is equivalent to:
\emph{no connected linear $v_3$-configuration with $v\le34$ has neither a Berge $4$-cycle nor a Berge $8$-cycle} (a disconnected counterexample contains a connected one of smaller order, so connectedness costs nothing).

\paragraph{Context.} Erd\H{o}s and Gy\'arf\'as conjectured that every graph of minimum degree at least $3$ has a cycle whose length is a power of two. For cubic bipartite graphs of order at most $68$ our statement is stronger than what the conjecture needs (it uses only the lengths $4,8,16$, not $32$ or $64$), but it is a finite computation about small orders; \textbf{nothing is claimed about the conjecture itself.}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Search (Tier \tC{}).} \texttt{data/code/cb\_search.c.txt} is a new C implementation written only from the text of Tranquilli \S3.1, Lemmas~5--7 and Prop.~10 (\texttt{EGC-L-0001}); the producer did not open his repository. The search installs root blocks, processes the least unfinished point $p$ and proposes a new block $\{p,q,r\}$ in lexicographic order after the last block through $p$ with least point $p$; $q,r$ range over eligible old labels ($>p$, degree $<3$, not yet collinear with $p$) or the next one or two fresh labels, within the cap. A proposal is rejected, in this order, if it is \emph{structural} ($q$ and $r$ already share a block), if it creates a \emph{C8} (a simple Levi path of length $6$ between two old points of the block), or a \emph{C16} (length $14$). A completion is recorded when every introduced point has degree $3$. There is no isomorphism pruning.
\paragraph{Two root modes.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Route~1, triangle-rooted} (Tranquilli's mode): roots $\{0,1,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{0,2,5\}$ plus $\{0,4,6\}$ (orbit~1) or $\{0,6,7\}$ (orbit~2). This is complete for configurations with a Berge triangle (Levi $6$-cycle). A $C_4$- and $C_8$-free cubic bipartite graph has girth $6$, $10$ or $\ge12$; girth $\ge10$ needs at least $62$ vertices (Moore, his Lemma~8), girth exactly $10$ needs at least $70$ (the premise $n(3,10)=70$, Tier~\tL{}, \texttt{EGC-L-0002}: O'Keefe--Wong 1980, computer assisted, read by us \emph{only} through the Exoo--Jajcay Dynamic Cage Survey, EJC DS16 v3, p.~7 Table~1 and p.~10 \S2.1.6; the original was read by nobody), and girth $\ge12$ needs at least $126$. So for order $\le60$ route~1 needs only the Moore bound, and for orders $62$--$68$ it additionally needs $n(3,10)=70$.
\item \textbf{Route~2, arbitrary root}: the single root block $\{0,1,2\}$, no triangle assumed. The same coverage argument applies because $\{0,1,2\}$ is the lexicographically first block through $0$ under first-occurrence labelling. \textbf{This route needs no Moore or cage premise.}
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Two oracles.} Oracle~A computes depth-first endpoint sets, cached per state. Oracle~B joins complete lists of half-paths (length $3$ or $7$) per pair, uncached. Both must give the same counters \emph{and} the same FNV-1a hash of the ordered decision transcript; they do at every cap and root mode where both were run (Tables~\ref{tab:r1}, \ref{tab:r2}).
\paragraph{Witness checkers.} A certificate stream records, for every proposal, either acceptance (then the checker recurses) or a rejection with its witness: for C8/C16 a simple path of length $6$ or $14$ in the pre-insertion Levi graph between two distinct introduced members of the block. Three checkers read these streams: the producer's \texttt{cb\_check.py} (Python) and \texttt{cb\_check\_fast.c} (C), and the auditor's independent \texttt{aud\_check.c}. They search for nothing; they re-derive the proposal schedule as a sorted, filtered candidate list (a different formulation from the nested loops of \texttt{cb\_search}), check every structural rejection by collinearity, check every witness path, recurse into expansions, refuse any completion, and recompute all counters against the END line. Accepting a proposal needs no proof (it only enlarges the tree), so soundness rests on the rejections being witnessed and the schedule being the Prop.~10 schedule.
\paragraph{Acceptance condition (audit correction 6).} The producer's two checkers read the cap, the orbit and the filter flags from the stream's own header. An honest stream with C6 pruning (\texttt{-c6}) or an honest cap-20 stream is accepted by them. An accepted stream therefore supports the claim \emph{only if} the checker's reported cap and orbit equal the claim, its flags are empty and its C6 count is $0$. All recorded check outputs satisfy this (route~2 cap~34 has $c_8>0$ and $c_{16}>0$, so neither filter was off, and $c_6=0$). The auditor's \texttt{aud\_check} hard-codes cap and orbit and rejects both such streams.
\paragraph{Coverage lemma (Tier \tC{}, not formalised).} Tranquilli's Prop.~10, re-derived by the producer and, separately, by the auditor (AUDIT.md \S1; \texttt{EGC-C-0001}). In outline: keep the invariant that labels are consecutive, every point below $p$ is finished, and the inserted blocks with least point $p$ form a lexicographic initial segment. Every uninserted block through $p$ is $\{p,q,r\}$ with $q,r>p$; at most two remain; blocks with two old points or one old and one new point sort by their least old point, blocks with two new points sort last; linearity forbids two remaining blocks sharing a point; new labels are handed out in that order; so the next target block is lexicographically after the last inserted one and is among the schedule's candidates; it passes the structural, C8 and C16 tests because a sub-configuration of a linear configuration without Berge $4$- or $8$-cycles has none either; connectedness forces every point to be introduced. \textbf{An argument is not a proof in a checker.} It is validated empirically (Section~\ref{sec:controls}) up to $v=13$ (arbitrary root) and $v=14$ (triangle roots) against nauty, and beyond that it rests on the text.

\paragraph{P3b and the number $128$ (audit correction 7).} Tranquilli writes of $128$ symmetric $19_3$-configurations. The single class found with C16 off at cap $19$ is self-dual: its colour-preserving automorphism group has order $57=19\cdot3$ (order $114$ as an uncoloured Levi graph), the $114$ rooted choices fall into two orbits, and the auditor's independent search finds the same single class in all three root modes ($128$ labelled leaves with root $\{0,1,2\}$, $16$ with orbit~2, $0$ with orbit~1). This supports, but does not prove, that the paper's $128$ are labelled copies of one configuration. The equality $128=128$ is therefore not evidence that no class was missed.

\section{Priority: this is not new}\label{sec:prior}
The preregistered novelty claim (order $68$ new) is \textbf{killed}. A same-day re-search on 2026-10-31, after the runs, found:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Ripatti}, BAIKAL/MOTOR 2017 one-page abstract (\texttt{isem.irk.ru/conferences/mopt2017/Abstracts/BAIKAL\_2017\_paper\_138.pdf}): ``the smallest cubic graphs without 4,8,16 cycles have 78 vertices''. This covers all cubic graphs on at most $76$ vertices, bipartite or not, hence our statement and the planned extension to order $72$. The method is canonical augmentation with ``probabilistic checking of every node''; no certificate is reported, and no full paper was found. Exoo's $78$-vertex graph is not bipartite.
\item \textbf{Ducoffe--Dumitru} (arXiv:2609.28594 v1): bipartite graphs of minimum degree $3$ and order $\le66$ by SAT modulo symmetries; they forbid $4,8,16,32$ (and $64$ at orders $64$--$66$), assume minimality reductions that are vacuous for cubic graphs, and ``did not ask the solver for a resolution proof''. Within cubic bipartite graphs our $4/8/16$ statement is formally stronger than theirs at orders $60$--$66$, but not stronger than Ripatti's claim.
\item \textbf{Garcia} (arXiv:2609.04686) quotes $54\le f(4)\le78$ (Exoo) and, like Tranquilli and Ducoffe--Dumitru, does not cite Ripatti.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Wording (audit correction 1).} \emph{We know of no earlier witness-checked computation for cubic bipartite orders $60$--$68$. Ripatti (2017, abstract, uncertified) and Ducoffe--Dumitru (2026, SAT without a proof log) cover these orders. This recomputation was independently audited (AI audit, not human).} This is a statement of absence, recorded as Tier~\tX{} (\texttt{EGC-X-0001}): the searches were an alphaXiv search and a web search on 2026-10-28 (which missed Ripatti) and on 2026-10-31, and one query each for OEIS and House of Graphs by the auditor. It is not a priority claim. Tranquilli's certified result stops at $58$ vertices.
\paragraph{Consequence for the work done.} The exploratory extension to arbitrary-root caps $\ge35$ (preregistered only by Addendum~1) was stopped at 17:34 UTC on 2026-10-31 without any result, because orders $70$ and $72$ would also have been re-checks; its outputs are not used (\texttt{data/logs/stopped\_runs.txt}). The file \texttt{data/logs/main/R\_cap35\_stream\_sha256.txt} belongs to that stopped run and is not a hash of any used stream.

\section{Limitations and deviations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{Not new.} A re-check of uncertified computations (Section~\ref{sec:prior}); the classification rests on a one-page abstract of Ripatti 2017 and on short searches.
\item \textbf{AI audit only; no human audit.} No person has read the code or the argument.
\item \textbf{Unverified, from the audit.} (a) The coverage lemma is argued in prose, not formalised; checked against nauty for every connected configuration with $v\le13$ (arbitrary root) and $v\le14$ (triangle roots), and beyond that it rests on the argument. (b) The auditor's search shares the Prop.~10 schedule and the kind of endpoint-set path test with the producer: independent code, not an independent method. (c) $n(3,10)=70$ was not read in the original; only route~1 at orders $62$--$68$ depends on it, route~2 does not. (d) There is no full text of Ripatti 2017. (e) \texttt{genbg} at $v=16$ was not repeated by the auditor (the producer's $v\le16$ run is same-author). (f) The Python check of the route-2 cap-34 stream never completed; two C checkers stand in. (g) Tranquilli's Table~4 leaf counts remain unexplained.
\item \textbf{Tier \tC{} is the ceiling.} No Lean, no kernel check, no statement lock.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Cubic bipartite graphs of order at most $68$ only. The route-1 cap-35 run covers the girth-6 part of order $70$ and says nothing about the $(3,10)$-cages. A statement about orders $\ge70$ is not made.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration.} (1) The parent assigns the search code to a Sonnet-tier agent; the producer was Opus. (2) Arbitrary-root mode (route~2) was added before the freeze, and the C checker \texttt{cb\_check\_fast} after it (logged in \texttt{freeze\_sha256.txt}, tamper-tested). (3) $n(3,10)=70$ read only in a survey. (4) N2 cap $7\to8$ (Section~\ref{sec:controls}). (5) \texttt{PREREG\_ITEM.md} was written by the producer and hashed before the first run; Addendum~1 (the extension) was written after the timing probe, hashed 2026-10-28 17:53:31 UTC (header says $\sim$18:10, see correction~8); Addendum~2 moved the extension's wall-clock deadline to 2026-11-01 14:45 UTC after an external API spend-limit outage (about 2026-10-28 18:30 to 2026-10-31 14:40 UTC), written before any extension run (hashed 14:41:15 UTC). (6) An exploratory girth-$\ge10$ run (C6 filter, C16 off, arbitrary root) was started and stopped at cap $33$ for cost, before its result; only its cap-$31$ output ($0$ completions) exists, in a session log, and it is not used. (7) The repository guards \texttt{antigravity\_guard.py} and \texttt{test\_rigor\_guard.py} both exit $1$ on pre-existing files elsewhere; neither flags an EG-CB68 file (\texttt{data/logs/guard\_*.txt}); \texttt{pytest tests/} was not run. (8) The novelty check failed (Section~\ref{sec:prior}). (9) Compute: about $7$ core-hours of the $48$ core-hour box, an estimate from process wall times, cores $2$ and $3$ only.
\item Not a physical claim of any kind.
\end{itemize}
