\begin{abstract}
The \emph{shifted} lonely runner conjecture (Blanco--Criado--Santos, arXiv:2603.24784, Conjecture~1.1(ii)) says: for distinct positive integer velocities $v_1,\dots,v_n$ and any real shifts $s_1,\dots,s_n$ there is a time $t$ with $\dist(s_i+v_it,\Z)\ge\frac1{n+1}$ for all $i$. It is known to fail for $5\le n\le17$ (Blanco--Criado--Santos) and for every $n\ge95$ (Poliakova, arXiv:2609.23952); Zhang (arXiv:2608.13599) asks about the range $18\le n\le94$.
\textbf{We give explicit exact certificates that it fails for every $n$ with $18\le n\le 38$}: distinct positive integers $v_i$ and rational shifts $s_i=a_i/D$ for which the $n$ open arcs of radius $\frac1{n+1}$ cover the circle. For $n\le28$ the velocities are $\{1,\dots,n\}$; for $29\le n\le38$ they are sets with smooth numbers up to $76$. The exact value of $\gamma(v;s)=\max_t\min_i\|s_i+v_it\|$ is $<\frac1{n+1}$ in every case, with $1/\gamma-(n+1)\in[0.0961,\,0.1098]$, hence $E_n\ge0.096$ in Poliakova's notation for $n=18,\dots,38$.
Each certificate was accepted by two checkers of the producer (different criteria and languages, \emph{same author}) and by an independent auditor's third checker (two exact methods), and the exact covering statement was kernel-checked in Lean by the auditor for all $34$ certificates $n=5,\dots,38$ (Tier~\tA{} \emph{pending a statement lock}); we re-ran the Lean gates for $n=18$ and $n=38$.
\textbf{For $39\le n\le94$ no certificate was found within a $30$ core-hour budget and the question stays open.} Together with the literature, the conjecture is known to fail for $5\le n\le38$ and $n\ge95$. These are upper bounds $\Gamma(n)<\frac1{n+1}$ only. \textbf{Nothing is said about the unshifted lonely runner conjecture, and the statement ``the shifted conjecture fails for all $n\ge5$'' is not supported by this work.}
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Markers.} \tA{}: kernel-checked Lean, axioms within $\{$\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}$\}$, controls caught. \tL{}: a cited source, taken as stated. \tC{}: executed code with an argument written down but not machine-checked. \tX{}: floating-point search output or a recorded absence, supporting nothing by itself. \section{Setting and the definition as pinned}\label{sec:setting}
The definition was pinned by the auditor from the sources themselves (read on alphaXiv), not taken from the producer (\texttt{AUDIT.md} \S1):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=0pt
\item \textbf{BCS} \cite{bcs} (arXiv:2603.24784 v4, 24 Oct 2026), p.~2, Conjecture~1.1(ii): for non-zero real $v_1,\dots,v_n$ with all $|v_i|$ different and every $s_1,\dots,s_n\in\R$ there is $t\in\R$ with $\dist(s_i+v_it,\Z)\ge\frac1{n+1}$ for all $i$. Def.~1.3: $\gamma(v;s)=\sup_t\min_i\dist(s_i+v_it,\Z)$ and $\gamma_{\rm sh}(v)=\min_{s}\gamma(v;s)$. Integer $v$ is no loss of generality (p.~3). With repeated velocities the conjecture fails trivially ($v_i$ all equal, $s_i=i/n$), so distinctness is essential.
\item \textbf{Zhang} \cite{zhang} (arXiv:2608.13599 v3), App.~A: distinct positive integers, $s\in[0,1)^n$, $\gamma=\max_t\min_i\|s_i+v_it\|$, $\Gamma(n)=\min_{|v|=n}\gamma_{\min}(v)$; Question~8.5 asks whether $\Gamma(n)<\frac1{n+1}$ for every $n\ge5$; p.~19: ``open only for $18\le n\le94$''.
\item \textbf{Poliakova} \cite{pol} (arXiv:2609.23952 v1), \S1: distinct positive integer velocities, shifts in $\R/\Z$, $\frac1{n+1+E_n}=\inf_{v,s}\max_t\min_i\|s_i+v_it\|$; Theorem~2 ($E_n\ge1$) covers only $n\ge95$; p.~3: finding counterexamples for the remaining $18\le n\le94$ ``would establish that SLR is false for all $n\ge5$''.
\end{itemize}
Consequences used in this paper (all \tL{}, \texttt{SLR-L-0001}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=0pt
\item A counterexample of size $n$ is $n$ runners with pairwise distinct positive integer velocities, real shifts and $\gamma(v;s)<\frac1{n+1}$.
\item For integer $v$ the function $t\mapsto\min_i\|s_i+v_it\|$ is $1$-periodic, so $t\in\R/\Z$. Its maximum is attained, so $\gamma<\delta$ iff for every $t$ some $i$ has $\|s_i+v_it\|<\delta$ iff the \emph{open} arcs $\{t:\|s_i+v_it\|<\delta\}$ cover the circle. Tangency (a single uncovered point) is therefore \emph{not} a cover. This is the criterion all checkers use with $\delta=\frac1{n+1}$.
\item Shifts matter mod $1$ and rational shifts are a special case of real ones; positive velocities suffice.
\item A certificate gives upper bounds only: $\gamma_{\rm sh}(v)\le\gamma(v;s)<\frac1{n+1}$, hence $\Gamma(n)<\frac1{n+1}$ and $E_n>0$. With the exact value, $E_n\ge1/\gamma(v;s)-(n+1)$.
\end{enumerate}

For $n\le28$ the velocities are $\{1,\dots,n\}$. For $n\ge29$ they are \emph{not} $[n]$: the search swaps velocities (all $\le2n+2$) and most of those sets drop large primes and add smooth numbers; all velocities are distinct positive integers and the trivial repeated-velocity cover is never used (both producer checkers and the auditor's checker reject repeated velocities).

\paragraph{Margins.} The exact margins $1/\gamma(v;s)-(n+1)$ lie in $[0.0961,\,0.1098]$ for $n=18,\dots,38$ (minimum at $n=30$, maximum at $n=25$; \texttt{AUDIT.md} \S2a, correction~6), so $E_n\ge0.096$ for these $n$. (The value $0.125$ that appears in the producer's text is the search \emph{target} radius $\delta^\ast=\frac1{n+1}-\frac1{8(n+1)^2}$, not an achieved margin.) The margins are too small to chain from one $n$ to the next, and no uniform rule was found; each $n$ was searched separately.

\paragraph{Where the conjecture is known to fail.} With the cited literature (\tL{}): $n=5,\dots,17$ \cite{bcs} (Prop.~5.14, as cited by the producer); $n=18,\dots,38$ this paper (to our knowledge the first published certificates; see Section~\ref{sec:scoop}); $n\ge95$ \cite{pol}. \textbf{$n=39,\dots,94$ remains open.}

\paragraph{What was searched for $n\ge39$.} The search stopped because the total CPU budget ($29.75$ core-hours of the $30$-core-hour cap) was reached. Attempts per $n$ at the frontier (producer's driver logs, \texttt{data/RESULT.md}): $n=39$: $0/23$ attempts ($6900$ CPU-s); $n=40$: $0/15$ ($4170$ CPU-s); $n=41,\dots,59$: $0/12$ each ($3600$ CPU-s each, i.e.\ $12$ attempts of $300$ s); $n=60$: $0/11$ ($3301$ CPU-s); \textbf{$n=61,\dots,94$ were never attempted.} For comparison the successes at the frontier took $1/20$ attempts at $n=36$, $1/8$ at $n=37$, $1/15$ at $n=38$. Failure to find a certificate for $n\ge39$ says nothing about whether certificates exist.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
Producer code is in \texttt{data/code/} (Python and C; the C sources are also stored as \texttt{.c.txt} because the bundler keeps only listed suffixes; compiled binaries are not shipped).
\paragraph{Search (untrusted, Tier \tX{}).} Floating-point coordinate moves on the uncovered measure at a fixed radius $\delta^\ast$: for runner $i$ the set not covered by the others is folded mod $\frac1{v_i}$ and the best window position is found exactly among the breakpoints (\texttt{cover\_ls.py}, ported to C as \texttt{cover\_ls\_fast.c} with velocity swaps, restarts and an init file). Earlier calibration tools (chain-LP min-max descent, continuation in $n$, a smooth surrogate, a deletion probe) reached the published optima at $n=5,6,8$ but failed at $n=9,10,12$ and could not get below $\frac1{n+1}$ for $12\le n\le30$; they are kept in \texttt{data/code/} as superseded. The C search was rebuilt several times during the run, so certificates $27,\dots,38$ come from different binary versions; this does not affect validity, since only the checkers are trusted. Each float solution is rounded to $s_i=a_i/D$ with $D\ge2/(\frac1{n+1}-\delta^\ast)$ and is then passed to the checkers. What worked at the frontier was repeated short restarts (for example $n=36$ failed $17$ times at $200$ s and then succeeded in the third attempt of a $300$-s batch); chain-LP polishing, a gap-repair LP, larger velocity pools and slack changes did not.
\paragraph{Producer's checkers (Tier \tC{}; same author).} \emph{Checker~1} (\texttt{slr\_exact.py}, Python integers): every arc endpoint $(k-s_i\mp\delta)/v_i$ is scaled by $M=\operatorname{lcm}(v)\,D\,(n+1)$ to an integer and a greedy sweep decides whether the open integer intervals cover $[0,M]$. \emph{Checker~2} (\texttt{check\_cover.c}, \texttt{\_\_int128}): the open arcs cover the circle iff every right endpoint of every arc lies strictly inside another arc, tested by cross-multiplication per runner pair; it parses the JSON itself. The two use different criteria and different languages, but \textbf{they were written by the same agent and are not independent in authorship}. All certificates were re-checked from the stored JSON alone by \texttt{verify\_all.py} (\texttt{data/logs/verify\_all.json}).
\paragraph{The auditor's third checker (Tier \tC{}, independent of the producer).} Written from scratch by the auditor, who read none of the producer's checkers (\texttt{data/code\_auditor/audit\_check.py}, standard library only). \emph{Method~A}: exact interval subtraction with \texttt{fractions.Fraction}; start from the closed set $[0,1]$ and remove every integer translate of every open arc; the arcs cover if nothing is left, single points count as uncovered. \emph{Method~B}: the exact value $\gamma(v;s)$ in integer arithmetic, evaluating $f(t)=\min_i\|s_i+v_it\|$ at every candidate maximiser (all pairwise crossings $s_i+v_it=\pm(s_j+v_jt)\bmod1$ and all single-runner peaks); the arcs cover iff $\gamma<\delta$. Both methods say COVERED for all $34$ certificates. We re-ran this checker on all $34$ files: $34/34$ valid, both methods COVERED, and the printed $\gamma$ values equal the auditor's table (\texttt{data/logs/auditor\_checker\_rerun.jsonl}). As a literature consistency check, for $n=5,\dots,15$ the certificates use $v=[n]$ and each certificate's $\gamma(v;s)$ is at least the exact $\gamma_{\rm sh}([n])$ of BCS Table~2 (all $11$ are; e.g.\ $n=5$: $189/1154\ge15/94$).

\section{Lean (Tier \tA{} pending a statement lock)}\label{sec:lean}
\paragraph{The statement.} The auditor's generator \texttt{gen\_lean.py} (untrusted; deterministic) writes, for each certificate, a file importing \texttt{Mathlib.Tactic} only (never \texttt{import Mathlib}); for $n=18$:
\begin{lstlisting}
def v : Fin 18 → ℕ := ![1, 2, ..., 18]
def s : Fin 18 → ℚ := ![3521/5777, ..., 4869/5777]   -- a_i / D of cert_n018.json
theorem v_injective : Function.Injective v := by decide
theorem v_pos : ∀ i, 0 < v i := by decide
theorem cover : ∀ t : ℝ, ∃ i : Fin 18, ∃ k : ℤ,
    |((s i : ℚ) : ℝ) + ((v i : ℕ) : ℝ) * t - (k : ℝ)| < 1 / 19
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{cover} says that for every real $t$ some runner is within distance $<\frac1{n+1}$ of an integer, which is exactly the negation of the conclusion of Conjecture~1.1(ii) for this $(v,s)$; together with \lean{v\_injective} and \lean{v\_pos} (distinct positive velocities) it is a counterexample of size $n$. It needs neither the maximum $\gamma$ nor compactness. The proof reduces $t$ to $t-\lfloor t\rfloor\in[0,1)$, splits on a chain of arc right endpoints ($82$ cases at $n=18$, $449$ at $n=38$) computed by the generator in exact rationals, and closes each case with \texttt{linarith}; the kernel re-checks every case, so the generator is not trusted.
\paragraph{What the auditor ran} (\texttt{AUDIT.md} \S2c; the auditor's run, not ours). All $34$ files $n=5,\dots,38$ compiled with \texttt{scripts/certified\_numerics/lean\_pinned.py} (Lean~4.34.1, Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7}) with \textbf{rc~0}, no banned token (\texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, \texttt{native\_decide}, \texttt{axiom}); \lean{cover} and \lean{v\_injective} depend on $\{$\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}$\}$ and \lean{v\_pos} on \lean{propext} only (\texttt{data/lean/lean\_all\_summary\_auditor.json}; $3456$ s in total on one core). Controls: the positive control (n=5, $\delta=1/6$) has rc~0 with standard axioms; the same file with $\delta$ tampered to the exact $\gamma=15/94$ has rc~1 (\texttt{linarith} fails), as it should. An early $n=38$ attempt with a parse error still printed an axioms line, so the return code and not the axiom line is the gate.
\paragraph{Our re-run (sample $n=18$ and $n=38$)} (\texttt{ledger/SLR-A-0001}, \texttt{data/logs/lean\_gate/}; script \texttt{data/code/lean\_gate\_sample.py}). For each file we ran, on one core of a loaded machine: (1) \texttt{lean\_pinned.compile\_file} with an olean (Lean~4.34.1, Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7}): $n=18$ rc~0 in $60.7$ s, $n=38$ rc~0 in $347.3$ s; (2) the repository's \texttt{anse/formal/lean\_runner.py} \texttt{verify\_file} gate with \texttt{lean\_cmd} pointed at the pinned toolchain (the way the producer of the sibling E1 result ran it): for both files all three theorems are compiled, not \lean{sorryAx}, with only whitelisted axioms (\lean{cover} and \lean{v\_injective}: \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}; \lean{v\_pos}: \lean{propext}); (3) \texttt{leanchecker} on the olean (\texttt{Programmes.SLR18.SLRN18}, \texttt{...SLRN38}): rc~0 for both. Controls through the same three gates: \texttt{SLRP1.lean} rc~0 with standard axioms; \texttt{SLRP1neg.lean} (the tampered $\delta=15/94$) rc~1 at compile, \texttt{lean\_runner} reports it as failed, and \texttt{leanchecker} finds no olean (rc~1). So the file-mode gate of \texttt{lean\_runner.py} \emph{can} be run on these files, but we ran it on the sample only ($2$ of $34$ plus the control).
\paragraph{Generator, hashes, and the link to the certificates.} \texttt{formal\_cert/Programmes/SLR18/} holds the generator (\texttt{auditor\_tools/gen\_lean.py}) and the $34$ generated files \texttt{SLRN05.lean}\,\dots\,\texttt{SLRN38.lean} plus the controls \texttt{SLRP1.lean} and \texttt{SLRP1neg.lean} (the latter fails by design), placed there as the audit's correction~8 asks (uncommitted at writing). \section{Literature position and scoop check}\label{sec:scoop}
Dated 2026-10-28 (producer) and 2026-10-31 (auditor).
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=0pt
\item \textbf{alphaXiv} (recency-ranked discovery, and with \texttt{published\_after} $2026$-$10$-$01$ / $10$-$20$ / $10$-$24$) for ``shifted lonely runner'', ``lonely runner'' and ``counterexamples'': the only shifted-conjecture counterexample papers are arXiv:2603.24784 and 2609.23952. Newer hits concern other problems (for example 2610.23790 on the unshifted $ML(V)$, 2610.37711 view-obstruction for spheres, 2610.35206 a runner-flow PDE); the producer read titles and abstracts only.
\item \textbf{Web search}: no source settles any $n$ in $18..94$. Zhang v3 (18 Oct 2026) and Poliakova v1 both call the range open; BCS v4 (24 Oct 2026) states failure only for $n=5..17$ and $E_n\ge1$ only for $n\ge95$.
\item \textbf{No conflict found.} The shifted conjecture is proved only for $n\le4$ (Alc\'antara--Criado--Santos; BCS Remark~1.2). Nothing published gives a lower bound on $\gamma_{\rm sh}$ or $\Gamma(n)$ for $18\le n\le38$. Beck--Everett (arXiv:2609.06259 v2, Thm~3.2) require a shifted counterexample to satisfy a small relation $m\cdot v=0$; the certificates do ($[n]$ has $2\cdot1-2=0$ and the swapped sets for $n\ge29$ contain many relations $a+b=c$). Zhang's Lemma~A.1 is consistent with them. BCS Table~2 gives $1/\gamma_{\rm sh}(1..15)=18$ ($E=2$), so it is plausible that $[n]$ still fails at $n=18..28$.
\item \textbf{Criado's repository} (latest commit 2026-04-24) has outputs only for $n\le13$ (\texttt{consecutive.out}) and $n=5,6,7$ censuses; only the listing and that file were looked at, at the end, as part of the scoop check, and nothing from it was used as a seed.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Residual risk.} Not checked: arXiv listings after the alphaXiv index date, unpublished BCS/Criado computations (the producer saw repository outputs only up to $n=13$), and private communications. ``To our knowledge, the first published certificates for $18\le n\le38$'' is not a priority claim.
\paragraph{A courtesy note.} We have not contacted the authors of the cited preprints (Blanco--Criado--Santos, Zhang, Poliakova), and they have not seen this work. Before any wide announcement the owner may wish to inform them.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item \textbf{AI audit only; human audit and statement lock owed.} The audit is by a separate AI agent instance, not a person. No person has certified that the Lean statements mean what their gloss says; no statement lock exists; the repository's \texttt{lean\_runner.py} gate was run on a sample ($n=18,38$) only, and the other $32$ files rest on the auditor's run with \texttt{lean\_pinned.py}.
\item \textbf{Still unverified (audit \S6).} (a) The repository-gate Lean verification of all files; (b) scoop completeness beyond the alphaXiv index and web search, including unpublished BCS computations; (c) the per-log CPU figures ($101746.4$ s by log plus $5344.8$ s manual runs $=107091.2$ s $=29.75$ core-hours; the controls/checks entry of $300$ s is an estimate; the margin to the $30$ core-hour cap is about $900$ s); (d) the producer's search code, which is untrusted and not needed for validity; (e) a full reading of every log for third-party text (grep-level only: no copy of Criado's \texttt{consecutive.out} and no paper text is tracked).
\item \textbf{The two producer checkers share an author.} Their agreement is not independent evidence; the independent evidence is the auditor's checker (two methods) and the Lean replay.
\item \textbf{Literature premises are taken as stated} (\tL{}): the definition and the statements about $n\le17$ and $n\ge95$ were read by the auditor on alphaXiv, not re-derived; $n=5..17$ are cited from BCS and used here only as positive controls.
\item \textbf{Scope.} $18\le n\le38$ only. The search is heuristic. $39\le n\le94$ is open; $61\le n\le94$ was never attempted. Nothing here concerns the unshifted conjecture, exact $\gamma_{\rm sh}$ values, or any physical claim.
\item \textbf{Bundle contents.} The $333$ raw search scratch files \texttt{logs/fast\_tmp/*.txt} are omitted (correction~5); compiled binaries are not shipped (C sources as \texttt{.c.txt}); the Lean files are the auditor's generated files, copied unchanged.
\item \textbf{Deviations from the preregistration} (from the producer's note): velocities for $n\ge29$ are swapped sets, which the preregistration allows as ``modified velocity sets''; the Poliakova uniform-rule route and add-a-runner chaining were not used; the N2 control wording was applied per runner and both signs (see Section~\ref{sec:controls}); everything ran on one core, one process at a time.
\end{itemize}
