\begin{abstract}
\noindent Q.~Guo \cite{guo} (arXiv:2610.13979, 11 Oct 2026, unrefereed; the paper says ChatGPT supplied the idea) exhibits a $2$-tetrahedron, one-vertex triangulation $T$ of the lens space $L(3,1)$ and claims that it refutes Conjectures~1--3 of F.~Luo \cite{luo} (arXiv:1003.4413) on Haken's normal surface equation, Thurston's gluing equation and the maximum of the volume on the space of angle structures. \textbf{The result is Guo's; we claim nothing new,} and nothing about the Poincar\'e conjecture. Two independent implementations (the second with Regina and Singular as instruments) reproduce the four clauses of the counterexample: (i) $\pi_1=C_3$ and a null-homotopic loop edge, (ii) Q-matching of rank $2$ with no cluster of three $2$-quad-type solutions, (iii) Thurston's equation has no solution (Groebner basis $\{1\}$), (iv) every maximum of the volume is a smooth point. Clause (iv) is upgraded from numerical to Tier~\tC{} by an elementary analytic bound. Minimality of two tetrahedra is reproduced by a Regina census. Guo's quotation of Luo's conjectures was checked against arXiv:1003.4413v2. Outcome: confirmed as a recheck; Tier~A (Lean) not reached.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim and its scope}\label{sec:claim}
Luo's conjectures concern a closed, irreducible, orientable, minimally triangulated $3$-manifold $M$. Conjecture~1: either Thurston's equation (3.7) has a solution, or Haken's equation has a cluster of three $2$-quad-type solutions. Conjecture~2: if all maxima of the volume on the space $\mathrm{SAS}(T)$ of (signed) angle structures are smooth points, then Thurston's equation has a solution. Conjecture~3: if some edge has equal endpoints and is null-homotopic, a cluster exists \cite{luo}. Guo's counterexample $T$ has $\pi_1=C_3$; clauses (ii)+(iii) refute C1, (iii)+(iv) refute C2, and (i)+(ii) refute C3 \cite{guo}. The deduction is elementary once the clauses and Luo's definitions are fixed. The auditor checked Guo's quotation of the conjectures and of the definitions (``$2$-quad-type'', cluster, signed real Haken solutions, Thurston's equation, $\mathrm{SAS}$, smooth point) against Luo's arXiv v2 (pp.\ 2--3, 6--9, 12--14, 17--20) and found it faithful (\texttt{LUO-L-0002}). The Contemp.\ Math.\ 541 version was not compared. \emph{Scope:} since $L(3,1)$ is not simply connected, the restricted versions behind Luo's route to the Poincar\'e conjecture are not touched (Guo's Remark~1.2).

\section{Triangulation, homology, fundamental group (\tC{})}\label{sec:tri}
From the four face pairings the gluing permutations are $1230,1230,3201,3201$, all odd; each of the $8$ faces is glued once. There is one vertex class with link $(V,E,F)=(6,12,8)$, $\chi=2$; three edge classes of degrees $2,5,5$; $\chi(M)=0$; the local-edge table equals the paper's table (2). The isomorphism signature is \texttt{cMcabbjak}; Regina~7.4 finds the triangulation isomorphic to its $\mathrm{lens}(3,1)$ and its census entry ``$L(3,1)$ : \#3''. Smith normal forms of the auditor's own boundary matrices ($d_2$ of size $3\times4$, $d_3$ of size $4\times2$, $d_2d_3=0$) give $H_1=\mathbb Z/3$, $H_2=0$, $H_3=\mathbb Z$. The fundamental group is $C_3$ by two routes: the producer's GAP computation and the auditor's sympy coset enumeration (order $3$, and the subgroup $\langle a\rangle$ of index $3$). Hence the loop edge $a$ is null-homotopic (\texttt{LUO-C-0001}).

\section{Q-matching and Lemma 3.1 (\tC{})}\label{sec:q}
The matrices $I$ and $W=I\Omega$ equal the paper's (5) entry by entry, and Regina's own quad matching matrix is identical to $W$. We have $\operatorname{rank}W=2$ (kernel of dimension $4$). The Haken system has rank $9$ with a $5$-dimensional solution space whose projection to the six quad coordinates equals $\ker W$. The only column pair of rank $<2$ is $(x_1,y_1)$, so every nonzero vector of $\ker W$ with at most two nonzero entries is a multiple of $(1,0,0,1,0,0)$ (Lemma~3.1) and there is no cluster of three. The same holds for nonnegative, integer or signed solutions, since restricting only shrinks the set. A Regina vertex normal surface is a torus with quads $(1,0,0,1,0,0)$, so a genuine $2$-quad-type solution exists, but only in $x_1,y_1$ (\texttt{LUO-C-0002}).

\section{Thurston's equation (\tC{})}\label{sec:thurston}
The edge equations from the edge classes coincide with the paper's, with $w=1/z$ the last two reduce to $1/z=1$ and $z=1$. The Groebner basis over $\mathbb Q$ (Singular, lex order, nondegeneracy $t\,z(1-z)w(1-w)=1$) is $\{1\}$ for the edge products $(+,+,+)$, in both orientation conventions, so Thurston's equation has no solution. For the generalised equation with edge products in $\{\pm1\}$, exactly $(+,-,-)$, $(-,+,-)$ and $(-,-,+)$ of the $8$ patterns are solvable, in both conventions; $(+,-,-)$ gives $z=w=-1$ (Guo's Remark~4.2). These solvable patterns serve as positive controls (\texttt{LUO-C-0003}).

\section{Clause (iv): the volume maximum (\tC{})}\label{sec:vol}
\emph{Numerical by the producer:} on $\mathrm{SAS}(T)$ (rank-$4$ constraints, a two-parameter family) a grid search with Nelder--Mead refinement gives the maximum $1.58864663930016$ at $8$ symmetric smooth points; the auditor reproduces it as a strict local maximum (Hessian eigenvalues $-13.7,-50.4$, $\min|\sin\theta|=0.3928$).

\emph{Analytic bound by the auditor (upgrade to \tC{}).} Angles are taken mod $\pi$; the Lobachevsky function $\Lob$ is odd and $\pi$-periodic. In a tetrahedron the three angles sum to $\pi$ mod $2\pi$; if one is in $\pi\mathbb Z$, the other two sum to $0$ mod $\pi$ and their $\Lob$ values cancel, so a partially flat tetrahedron contributes $0$ volume. Since $\Lob'(\theta)=-\log|2\sin\theta|$, $|\Lob|\le\Lob(\pi/6)$ everywhere, and $\Lob(\pi/6)\le0.507721$ by a partial sum with tail bound $1/(2N)$. At a non-smooth point one of the two tetrahedra is partially flat, so $\mathrm{vol}\le3\Lob(\pi/6)\le1.52316$. On the other hand the exact rational point $\theta/\pi=(3/5,1/8,11/40,7/5,17/40,47/40)$ lies in $\mathrm{SAS}(T)$ (tetrahedron sums $1$ and $3$, edge sums $2,4,2$, checked in rationals), all six angles are non-integer multiples of $\pi$, and its volume is at least $1.58529$ (partial sum $N=2000$ of $\Lob(\theta)=\tfrac12\sum\sin(2n\theta)/n^2$ with tail bound $0.0015$). Since $1.58529>1.52316$, every maximum is smooth. The numbers are 30-digit floats with a rigorous tail bound, not interval arithmetic; the rounding error is about $10^{-25}$, far below the margin $0.062$. This route uses neither Luo's Theorems~4.2(a), 4.4 nor Milnor's bound.

\emph{Side remark.} Using Milnor's bound ($\tL$), the non-smooth supremum is exactly $v_3=3\Lob(\pi/3)=1.0149416$, attained at $\theta/\pi=(2/3,0,1/3,-2/3,1/3,4/3)$ (exact membership in $\mathrm{SAS}$ checked). The producer's sampled non-smooth value $1.0149$ is this supremum, not a sampling artefact (\texttt{LUO-C-0004}).

\section{Minimality (\tC{})}\label{sec:min}
By Regina enumeration, none of the $108$ one-tetrahedron gluings has $H_1=\mathbb Z/3$, so two tetrahedra is minimal (this reproduces, rather than cites, the Jaco--Rubinstein--Tillmann fact). Among the $136080$ two-tetrahedron gluings, $5088$ are closed, orientable and valid, in $16$ classes; three have $H_1=\mathbb Z/3$ and $\pi_1=\langle a\mid a^3\rangle$: \texttt{cMcabbgaj} (one vertex, degrees $1,2,9$), \texttt{cMcabbjak} (Guo's) and \texttt{cPcbbbaai} (two vertices). \textbf{So Guo's triangulation is not the only one-vertex minimal triangulation of $L(3,1)$.} This does not affect the counterexample, but the other one-vertex triangulation was not tested against C1--C3. Calling the other classes ``$L(3,1)$'' uses geometrization (\tL{}); for Guo's own triangulation Regina's explicit lens space construction suffices (\texttt{LUO-C-0005}).

\section{Controls}\label{sec:ctrl}
Positive: Regina's figure-eight knot complement triangulation is solvable with $z=w=e^{\pm i\pi/3}$, and SnapPy's $4_1$ shapes satisfy the edge equations to $1.4\cdot10^{-15}$ (this does not distinguish the two orientation conventions); the producer's $\pi_1$ code gives $C_3,C_5,C_7,C_2$ and trivial for $L(3,1),L(5,2),L(7,2),L(2,1),S^3$, matching Regina. Negative: $0$ of $20$ single-pairing perturbations keep the claim profile (one vertex, degrees $2,5,5$, $|\pi_1|=3$, same $I$); $18$ single-entry increments of $I$ are all unsolvable, a weak control because the solver never said yes there; the discriminating ``yes'' cases are the figure-eight and the sign patterns of Section~\ref{sec:thurston} (\texttt{LUO-C-0006}).

\section{Limitations and what is not done}\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep=1pt
\item AI audit only; no human audit; no Lean (no Tier~A).
\item Not verified: Milnor's $v_3$ bound (side remark only); the step ``$\pi_1=\mathbb Z/3\Rightarrow L(3,1)$'' (geometrization) used to name the other census classes; the Contemp.\ Math.\ 541 text of Luo; Segerman--Tillmann (Guo's Remark~4.3); interval arithmetic for the volume bounds.
\item The other one-vertex minimal triangulation (\texttt{cMcabbgaj}) was not tested against C1--C3.
\item Literature: no independent verification or critique of Guo's paper and no earlier refutation of Luo's conjectures was found, but the search was limited (\texttt{LUO-L-0003}); ``not found'' is relative to the queries.
\item Trusted components: Regina~7.4, Singular, GAP~4.11.1, sympy, mpmath, SnapPy.
\end{itemize}
