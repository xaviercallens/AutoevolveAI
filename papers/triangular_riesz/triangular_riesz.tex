\begin{abstract}
The openai/math release contains an AI-generated Lean~4 proof that the triangular lattice $A$ of covolume one minimises the lower
$g$-energy among all locally finite planar configurations of density one, for every smooth completely monotone $g$
(theorem \lean{universal_energy_minimum}, Comparator-accepted by us under the pinned toolchain)~\cite{oai,oaicert}. We derive three named
consequences inside that pinned environment and check them with the Lean kernel, using only the axioms
\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}:
(D1) for every $s>0$ the Riesz potential $t\mapsto t^{-s/2}$ (in $t=|x-y|^2$, i.e.\ $|x-y|^{-s}$) is admissible, so the triangular lattice minimises the Riesz $s$-energy among
all locally finite density-one configurations;
(D1b) for $s>2$ the triangular lattice's Riesz $s$-energy (an Epstein zeta value) is finite, so D1 is a comparison with a finite, attained minimum
(for $0<s\le2$ the lattice side is $\infty$ and D1 only says $E(C)=\infty$);
(D2) for every density $\rho>0$ and every admissible $g$, the rescaled lattice $\rho^{-1/2}A$ has density $\rho$ and minimises the
$g$-energy among all locally finite density-$\rho$ configurations; without the rescaling the statement is false (proved in Lean at $\rho=1/4$, $s=4$).
Mathematically each step is short: complete monotonicity of $t^{-s/2}$ and a scaling argument. The upstream manuscript already states
informally that its theorem covers inverse powers. What is new is the kernel-checked form of these statements, with a statement-fidelity
audit, planted negative controls and re-verification by separate instances of the same AI model (producer $\neq$ verifier). Everything is conditional on the upstream theorem, which has not been
peer reviewed and which, by its authors' own account, does not claim the stronger per-potential form of the Cohn--Kumar
conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and scope}

Rankin, Cassels, Ennola and Diananda~\cite{rankin,cassels,casselsc,ennola,diananda} showed that the triangular lattice minimises the
Epstein zeta function $\sum_{a\neq0}|a|^{-s}$, $s>2$, among planar lattices of fixed covolume, and Montgomery~\cite{montgomery} did the same
for Gaussian (theta) sums. Comparison with \emph{all} configurations is the planar case of the universal-optimality question of
Cohn and Kumar~\cite[Conj.~9.4]{ck}, proved in dimensions 8 and 24 by Cohn, Kumar, Miller, Radchenko and Viazovska~\cite{ckmrv}. Partial
planar results concern restricted competitor classes~\cite{hardin} and small perturbations~\cite{leble}.

On 2026-09-23 the openai/math release posted a manuscript~\cite{oai} claiming the energy-comparison conclusion in the plane, together with a
Lean proof of it. By its own account that manuscript constructs sharp auxiliary functions for every Gaussian but not for each mixed completely monotone
potential, and it does not claim that stronger per-potential form of the Cohn--Kumar conjecture. The release of 2026-10-07 withdrew three unrelated manuscripts (on Weil classes of abelian eightfolds, Kuga--Satake for K3, and the Hodge conjecture for
products of K3 surfaces). It did not touch
the triangular manuscripts, and the Lean files we use are byte-identical at its commit (Section~\ref{sec:fidelity}).

\paragraph{What this note is and is not.} It is a kernel-checked derivation, inside upstream's pinned environment, of three statements
physicists use: Riesz energies, their finiteness, and arbitrary density. It is \emph{not} an independent proof of universal optimality,
\emph{not} a new mathematical idea (the manuscript already notes that inverse powers are included), and \emph{not} a peer review of
the upstream proof. If the upstream statement is a faithful formalisation and its proof is correct, then D1--D2 are theorems; otherwise they
are only implications.

\section{The upstream statement and a fidelity audit}\label{sec:fidelity}

Let $\mathrm{Plane}=\R^2$ with the Euclidean norm, and let $N_R(C)$ be the number of points of $C$ in the closed disk of radius $R$ about
$0$. The upstream Lean file (\lean{ComparatorChallenges/TriangularEnergy.lean}, sha256 \texttt{af51979c\ldots0616}) defines:
\begin{itemize}
\item $g$ is \emph{admissible} if $g\in C^\infty(0,\infty)$, $g\ge0$, and $(-1)^r g^{(r)}(t)\ge0$ for all $r\ge0$ and $t>0$;
\item $C$ has \emph{density one} if $N_R(C)/(\pi R^2)\to1$;
\item $E_g(C)=\liminf_{R\to\infty} N_R^{-1}\sum_{x}\sum_{y\neq x} g(|x-y|^2)$, the sums running over points of $C$ in the closed $R$-disk,
with values in $[0,\infty]$;
\item $A=\{b^{-1/2}(j+k/2,\,kb): j,k\in\Z\}$, $b=\sqrt3/2$ (covolume one), and $E_g^{\rm lat}=\sum_{a\in A\setminus0}g(|a|^2)\in[0,\infty]$.
\end{itemize}
The theorem is: for admissible $g$ and locally finite $C$ of density one,
$E_g^{\rm lat}\le E_g(C)$ and $E_g^{\rm lat}=E_g(A)$. The challenge file states it with \texttt{sorry} by design; the proof is in
\texttt{OAI/Analysis/Triangular/Energy/Universal.lean}, and Comparator certifies that the two statements agree. The file has the same sha256
at upstream commits \texttt{adc7f124} and \texttt{fd4aeeb2}, and \texttt{git diff} between them touches no file under
\texttt{lean/OAI/Analysis/Triangular/}, \texttt{LatticeSummability.lean}, the toolchain or the lake manifest.

We compared each item with the lower-energy and centred-density formulation of~\cite[Defs.~1.1--1.3]{ckmrv} that the manuscript cites
(\texttt{results/discovery/statement\_fidelity.md}). They match, including the ordered-pair normalisation by $N_R$ and the convention that
divergent sums equal $\infty$. Two points remain. First, upstream uses closed disks and we did not analyse whether the open-disk lower energy
can differ. Second, the audit was done by the producing agent; the independent verifiers read the same definitions separately.

\section{Results}

All statements below are Lean theorems in the AutoevolveAI repository (\texttt{formal\_cert/Triangular/}), compiled against upstream's
pinned Lean~4.34.1 / Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7} / openai/math build. Here $\mathrm{riesz}_s(t)=t^{-s/2}$, so
$\mathrm{riesz}_s(|x-y|^2)=|x-y|^{-s}$; this identity is kernel-checked for $|x-y|>0$ (\lean{TriangularControls.riesz_sq}).

\begin{theorem}[D1, \lean{TriangularRiesz.riesz_admissible}, \lean{triangular_riesz_optimal}]
For every $s>0$, $\mathrm{riesz}_s$ is admissible. Hence for every locally finite $C$ of density one,
$\sum_{a\in A\setminus0}|a|^{-s}\le E_{\mathrm{riesz}_s}(C)$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Mathlib's \lean{Real.iter_deriv_rpow_const} gives $\frac{d^r}{dt^r}t^{p}=\mathrm{desc}_r(p)\,t^{p-r}$. With
$p=-s/2$, $\mathrm{desc}_r(-s/2)=(-1)^r\mathrm{asc}_r(s/2)$ (\lean{ascPochhammer_eval_neg_eq_descPochhammer}), and $\mathrm{asc}_r(s/2)>0$, which
gives the sign pattern. Smoothness away from $0$ is \lean{Real.contDiffAt_rpow_const_of_ne}. No value at $t\le0$ is ever used.

\begin{theorem}[D1b, \lean{TriangularRiesz.latticeEnergy_riesz_lt_top}]
For every $s>2$, $\sum_{a\in A\setminus0}|a|^{-s}<\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} \lean{norm_sq_triangularPoint}: $|p(j,k)|^2=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}(j^2+jk+k^2)$. Reindex the sum over $A\setminus0$ by the
injective parametrisation, map $k\mapsto-k$ to match the form $j^2-jk+k^2$, and apply the openai/math lemma
\lean{LatticeSummability.summable_normForm_neg_rpow} with $\sigma=s/2>1$. For $0<s\le2$ the sum diverges (standard; not kernel-checked; the verifier confirmed
$2\pi\log R$ growth numerically at $s=2$), so D1 compares with a finite minimum exactly for $s>2$.

For $\rho>0$ say that $C$ has \emph{density} $\rho$ if $N_R(C)/(\pi R^2)\to\rho$. At $\rho=1$ this is upstream's definition by
\lean{Iff.rfl}.

\begin{theorem}[D2; \lean{universal_any_density}, \lean{triangular_optimal_any_density}, \lean{attained}]
Let $g$ be admissible and $\rho>0$. Then $\rho^{-1/2}A$ is locally finite and has density $\rho$, and for every locally finite $C$ of
density $\rho$,
\[ E_g(\rho^{-1/2}A)=\sum_{a\in A\setminus0} g(|a|^2/\rho)\le E_g(C). \]
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} $t\mapsto g(ct)$ is admissible for $c>0$; the identity $\frac{d^r}{dt^r}g(ct)=c^r g^{(r)}(ct)$ holds globally from
\lean{deriv_comp_mul_left} with no differentiability hypothesis. Next, $N_R(\lambda C)=N_{R/\lambda}(C)$, so $\sqrt\rho\,C$ has density one, and
the disk energies transform as $E_{g,R}(\lambda C)=E_{g(\lambda^2\cdot),R/\lambda}(C)$. The $\liminf$ is invariant under $R\mapsto R/\lambda$,
because this order isomorphism maps \lean{atTop} to itself. Applying upstream's theorem to $g(\cdot/\rho)$ and $\sqrt\rho\,C$ gives the
inequality, and its attainment conjunct gives the equality. The density and attainment lemmas (\lean{scaled_density}, \lean{attained}) were
written by the independent D2 verifier and merged with attribution.

\begin{corollary}[\lean{riesz_any_density_homogeneous}]
For $s>0$, $\rho>0$ and locally finite $C$ of density $\rho$: $\rho^{s/2}\sum_{a\in A\setminus0}|a|^{-s}\le E_{\mathrm{riesz}_s}(C)$
in $[0,\infty]$. The homogeneity step $\sum_a \mathrm{riesz}_s(\rho^{-1}|a|^2)=\rho^{s/2}\sum_a|a|^{-s}$ is kernel-checked
(\lean{riesz_lattice_homogeneous}). Informative for $s>2$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Negative control.} The unrescaled claim
``$E^{\rm lat}_g\le E_g(C)$ for every locally finite $C$ of density $\rho$'' is false at $\rho=1/4$, $s=4$, and this is proved in Lean (\lean{TriangularDensity.unscaled_false}). The witness
$C=2A$ has energy $\sum(4|a|^2)^{-2}$, which is strictly smaller, and finite by D1b. So the $\rho^{-1}$ in D2 cannot be dropped. For $\rho\ge1$ the
unrescaled claim is true but weaker (it follows from D2 because admissible $g$ are non-increasing; not kernel-checked).
A further control: $t\mapsto t$ ($s=-2$) is \emph{not} admissible (\lean{riesz_neg_two_not_admissible}).

\section{Verification}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kernel and axioms.} \lean{Riesz.lean}, \lean{RieszFinite.lean}, \lean{AnyDensity.lean} and \lean{Controls.lean} compile with
exit code 0 (each about 13--20\,s once upstream's oleans are cached; two harmless deprecation warnings). \texttt{\#print axioms} reports $\{$\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice},
\lean{Quot.sound}$\}$ for every theorem above and for upstream's \lean{universal_energy_minimum}. A planted \lean{sorry} and a planted axiom
in the same audit file are both flagged (\lean{sorryAx}, \lean{ctl_cheat}). There is no \lean{sorry}, \lean{axiom} or \lean{native_decide} in the four proof files; \lean{Axioms.lean} contains only the two planted
controls.
\item \textbf{Preregistration.} D1b and D2 had preregistrations with kill criteria committed before their Lean files (3--4 minutes earlier,
per git log; commit order is the only evidence of timing; \texttt{results/discovery/D1b,D2/preregistration.json}). D1 was not preregistered:
the proposal document (\texttt{docs/DISCOVERY\_PROPOSAL\_2026-10-10.md}), which assigned it a prior of about 85\%, was committed together
with its Lean file.
\item \textbf{Re-verification.} D1/D1b and D2 were each re-verified by a separate instance of the same AI model (producer $\neq$ verifier),
which recompiled the files and wrote its own axiom audit, controls and numerics. Both verdicts were CONFIRMED\_WITH\_CORRECTIONS. The
corrections were wording and scope, plus (D2) adding the verifier's lemmas \lean{scaled_density}, \lean{attained} and \lean{unscaled_false},
which no second party wrote or reviewed line by line. The final \lean{AnyDensity.lean} and \lean{Controls.lean} were recompiled with clean
axioms by the producer and by a pre-publication referee (another separate instance), but not re-examined by the D2 verifier. The verifiers' controls also included a check that the D1b proof cannot pass its only $s$-dependent step at $s=2$
(scratch file, not shipped).
\item \textbf{Numerics} (verifier code, mpmath; for the Riesz rows, closed forms agree with direct sums to about $10^{-7}$; the referee
reproduced every row independently). Table~\ref{tab:num}: the triangular
lattice beats the square lattice at equal density in every case, and for Riesz $s=4$ the ratio $0.959605$ does not depend on $\rho$, as
homogeneity predicts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Everything is conditional on upstream's \lean{universal_energy_minimum}. That proof is AI-generated and has not been peer reviewed, though we
checked that it is accepted by Comparator, with clean axioms, under the pinned toolchain. Its statement has been audited by AI agents, not by a
human expert.
\item The Cohn--Kumar conjecture in its per-potential form (a sharp auxiliary function for each potential) is not established, upstream or here.
\item For $0<s\le2$, D1 only says $E(C)=\infty$; renormalised logarithmic ($s=0$) and Riesz ($0<s<2$) energies are treated in the upstream manuscript, not here;
neither treats $s=2$.
\item Closed versus open disks in the energy definition was not analysed.
\item Other admissible families (Yukawa, $e^{-\kappa\sqrt t}/\sqrt t$) were not attempted. They need complete monotonicity of compositions,
which current Mathlib does not offer in usable form.
\end{enumerate}
