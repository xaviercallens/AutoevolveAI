# Style guide and shared interface — the discovery journal (binding for every writer)

Working title: **« Le journal des découvertes — Une année où l'IA propose, l'ordinateur calcule, la preuve juge et l'humain décide du sens »**.
Language: French. Readers: curious adults with no science training (a teenager can follow). Tone: warm, honest, modest, never triumphant.

## Sources (use nothing else for facts)
- `results/discovery_journal_2026/TOP20.json` — the 20 contributions (what it is, analogy suggestion, what was checked, place in the story).
- `ARC.md` (the story of the year in five phases), `SELECTION.md` (rubric and rules), the two inventories, `scores.json`.
- For numbers and caveats, the paper's own record (papers/*/AUDIT.md, REVISION.md, publication_meta.json) when TOP20.json points to it.
- A number or claim that is not in these sources is not used.

## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- Every result in the book was produced by AI agents under the author's direction and audited by other AI agents. **No human mathematician has reviewed them.** Say it plainly, once per chapter at most, never as a dramatic disclaimer.
- Results built on openai/math are conditional on theorems that are AI-generated and not yet peer reviewed.
- Never write "breakthrough", "revolution", "solved", "proved the conjecture" (Sandier–Serfaty is NOT proved; no Clay problem is solved; credit for the Navier–Stokes corpus belongs to its authors).
- A negative result or a retraction is told as a gain in understanding, with the same care as a success.
- Never state a confidence level beyond its Elenchus tier.
- Private repositories: describe at a high level only (no code, no quotes).

## The four confidence levels in everyday words (use these exact images)
- **A — « le juge a regardé »:** a computer program, the Lean kernel, checked every logical step. (Technical gate: the axioms stay within a short whitelist, a kernel replay passes, and no shortcut around the kernel is found. When a replay is missing, say « sans rejeu ».)
- **L — « c'est écrit dans un livre sérieux »:** published literature, read and cited.
- **C — « un raisonnement, mené ici par des agents d'IA, que personne n'a vérifié à la machine »**.
- **X — « une esquisse au crayon »:** a floating-point exploration; it guides, it never proves.
Use the macro `\niveau{A}` etc. for the badge. Do not use the Lean jargon without a gloss on first use.

## Each contribution chapter (about 3 pages) has this fixed shape
1. `\section{Title in plain words}` — with the dates and DOI(s) in a margin note: `\reference{doi:...}`.
2. **L'idée en une image** — the everyday analogy (`\begin{analogie}...\end{analogie}`), concrete, correct (an analogy must not mislead about the mathematics; say where it stops being valid in one sentence).
3. **Ce qui a été fait** — plain words; one small worked example or number a reader can follow with pencil and paper.
4. **Comment on s'est assuré que ce n'est pas faux** — the Elenchus level and what was checked, by whom, with controls (a test that must fail, and does).
5. **Ce que ça ne dit pas** — the honest limits (`\begin{limite}...\end{limite}`).
6. **Pour aller plus loin** — one or two sentences + the DOI or repository.
7. One illustration generated by code from the real numbers (figure file in `figures/`, built by a script in `code/`), in the book's palette.
Same headings, same order, same length (±20 %) for all 20, whatever the domain — this is the "constant manner".

## Shared macro interface (the frame writer implements these; chapters may use only these)
`\niveau{A|L|C|X|As}` badge · `\reference{...}` margin reference · environments `analogie`, `limite`, `encadre` (a boxed aside) ·
`\citationcourte{...}` for a short quote (NOT `\citation`, which is a LaTeX kernel command) · figures via `\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/NAME}` ·
`\chapter{...}` for each contribution within a part. No other packages or macros. A stub is in `macros_stub.tex`.

## Palette and look
Colour-blind-safe palette (Okabe–Ito), ivory paper, serif text. Figures: matplotlib, PDF + PNG, 300 dpi, one discreet caption line, French labels.

## Easter eggs
The book carries the family dedication and the hidden acronym theorems (ALIX, KAL, ILENE, ALEXIS, AURELIA). **Never state what ALIX or KAL stands for as a family.** Only the frame writer places the eggs; chapter writers must not mention the family names or the acronym meaning.
