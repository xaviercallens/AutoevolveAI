1. Introduction and main results
The Yukawa potential describes a screened Coulomb interaction and has been studied extensively
since 1935 [39], in which Yukawa combined Heisenberg’s idea of a short-range force interaction
and Fermi’s idea of an exchange particle in order to resolve the problem of the neutron-proton
interaction. Its screening parameter introduces a competition between two cubic lattice structures:
numerical phase diagrams and lattice calculations predict BCC at weak screening and FCC at
strong screening [31, 16, 17, 19]. The transition has also been studied through applied-strain
free-energy calculations [18]. The purpose of this paper is to determine the global minimizing
Bravais lattices for every positive screening parameter at zero temperature and to prove that there
is exactly one transition.

The lattice minimization problem belongs to the broader theory of crystallization; see the reviews
of Blanc–Lewin [5] and Lewin [21].
Fourier analysis provides another approach to energy minimization. Cohn–Elkies [8] developed
linear programming bounds for the density of sphere packings. Cohn–Kumar [9] proved univer-
sal optimality for sharp configurations on spheres. Cohn, Kumar, Miller, Radchenko and Via-
zovska [10] subsequently proved universal optimality of the E
8 
and Leech lattices at fixed density
in dimensions 8 and 24, respectively, for completely monotone functions of squared distance. The
Yukawa potential belongs to this class: its positive Gaussian representation (2.1) shows that it is
completely monotone as a function of squared distance.
The Gaussian representation also connects the present problem with the Gaussian core model.
Cohn, Kumar and Sch¨urmann [11] numerically investigated its ground states and identified formal
duality relations. Cohn–de Courcy-Ireland [7] established asymptotically sharp energy bounds for
Gaussian interactions in a range of parameters, at fixed density as the dimension tends to infinity.
Renormalized energy connects these problems with the microscopic theory of Coulomb and
Riesz gases. Sandier–Serfaty [33, 34] develop this approach for planar vortices and logarithmic
gases. Rougerie–Serfaty [32] and Petrache–Serfaty [28] identify microscopic renormalized energies
in next-order Coulomb and Riesz asymptotics. Lebl´e–Serfaty [20] prove an empirical-field large-
deviation principle, while Armstrong–Serfaty [1] obtain local laws and rigidity estimates. Petrache–
Serfaty [29] connect universal optimality with renormalized Coulomb and Riesz crystallization,
giving unconditional minimization results in dimensions 8 and 24. Serfaty’s monographs [36, 37]
provide systematic accounts of these developments.
For three-dimensional lattice energies, the work of Sarnak–Str¨ombergsson [35] on Epstein zeta
functions and heights of flat tori provides a fundamental analytic framework. B´etermin–Petrache [3]
develop dimension-reduction methods for lattice theta functions. Related Fourier-duality identities
for soft pair potentials are obtained by Torquato–Stillinger [38]. Classical local optimality results
are due to Ennola [14]; B´etermin [2] treats local variations for general interaction potentials. Three-
dimensional Riesz and Lennard–Jones lattice energies are studied in [4]. Ren–Wei [30] establish
BCC selection for a different long-range interaction.
Our argument follows the local–global method developed in our previous work [25], extending
the framework of Sarnak–Str¨ombergsson [35]. Our analysis of theta differences [22] includes the
hexagonal Yukawa minimum in two dimensions. Sharp lattice transitions for competing Epstein
zeta energies and for theta derivatives are studied in [23, 24], respectively. The present problem
requires a comparison in all five shape variables of a three-dimensional Bravais lattice, uniformly
in the screening parameter. The unique Yukawa transition is determined by integrating the FCC–
BCC theta difference, rather than by a pointwise theta comparison.
